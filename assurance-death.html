Facing death

Afraid of Dying

Christians still die, and many of them dread it. Hebrews says the Son of God took flesh and blood for this very reason.

About 4 minutes to read

That through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil; And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage.

Hebrews 2:14-15 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

If you’re afraid anyway, that doesn’t mean he never delivered you. Death is an enemy, the last one to be destroyed (1 Corinthians 15:26 (opens in a new tab)), and it’s no sin to dread an enemy that has already lost. Christ will be with you in the valley of the shadow of death (Psalm 23:4 (opens in a new tab)). He has gone through death itself ahead of you and come out the other side. For those who follow him there’s no waiting room between, and no purging fire.

The souls of believers are at their death made perfect in holiness, and do immediately pass into glory; and their bodies, being still united to Christ, do rest in their graves till the resurrection.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 37 (opens in a new tab)

The hope of a Christian doesn't end with the soul entering glory. Christ will raise the dead, and his people will dwell with him in resurrected bodies (1 Corinthians 15:42-49 (opens in a new tab) and Philippians 3:20-21 (opens in a new tab)). Death separates soul and body for a time, but it won't keep what belongs to the risen Lord.

The sin you’ve fought all your life doesn’t come with you. Even your body is still united to Christ, and it rests in the grave as in a bed until the resurrection.

O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory? The sting of death is sin; and the strength of sin is the law. But thanks be to God, which giveth us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.

1 Corinthians 15:55-57 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

The sting was sin, and the law gave sin its power to condemn. But Christ kept the law and bore the sin. So for a believer even dying now comes out of God’s love, to free him from sin and bring him to Christ (WLC Q&A 85 (opens in a new tab)).

If you dread leaving the people you love, commit them to God. Psalm 68:5 (opens in a new tab) calls him a father of the fatherless and a judge of the widows, which means he takes up their cause.

If the hard news has just come, the comfort page has Scripture for that day. If you’re grieving someone else, start there.

If you’ve had thoughts of ending your life, God hasn’t finished with it. In the United States, call 988 or text 988. Elsewhere, call your local emergency number. And tell someone near you.

When Death Frightens You

  1. Call for your elders. James 5 (opens in a new tab) says the sick should call for the elders of the church to pray over them. Don’t wait until the last days to ask.
  2. Tell your family where your hope is. Say it in your own words, or write it in a letter they can keep.
  3. Put your affairs in order. Make or update a will, name who should make medical decisions for you, and write down what you want for your funeral. It’s a kindness to the people you leave.
  4. Learn one passage by heart. Psalm 23 (opens in a new tab) or John 14:1-3 (opens in a new tab) is a good place to start. Have it ready for the hard nights.

While you wait

Do Not Fight This Alone

A page cannot sit with you or pray over you. Tell your pastor or an elder what you are afraid of. Keep sitting under the preached Word, and if you are a communing member, do not stay away from the Lord’s Table while your doubts are being answered. The Larger Catechism says it was appointed for the relief even of weak and doubting Christians (Q&A 172). Will God Keep Me to the End? gives a few things to do while you wait for the fear to lift.

If you cannot tell whether you have ever come to Christ, do not wait to settle it. Come to him now. He promised to cast out none who come (John 6:37), and the gospel is set out for you in full.
