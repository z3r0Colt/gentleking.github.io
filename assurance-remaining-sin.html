Remaining sin

When the Same Sin Keeps Coming Back

Remaining sin calls for repentance and help. It does not overturn the justification of those who trust Christ. Read how Scripture holds warning and pardon together.

On this page

  1. I keep going back to the same sin
  2. What I did is too great to forgive
  3. He must be tired of forgiving me
  4. When the Sin Returns
  5. Do Not Fight This Alone

About 6 minutes to read

I keep going back to the same sin

If you think a real Christian would be free of this by now, read Romans 7 (opens in a new tab). Paul wrote it as an apostle, and he says he does the very thing he hates. There’s a war in every believer between the flesh and the spirit, and it lasts as long as this life (WCF 13.2 (opens in a new tab)).

God doth continue to forgive the sins of those that are justified; and although they can never fall from the state of justification, yet they may by their sins fall under God’s fatherly displeasure, and not have the light of his countenance restored unto them, until they humble themselves, confess their sins, beg pardon, and renew their faith and repentance.

Westminster Confession of Faith 11.5 (opens in a new tab)

God gave his verdict when you believed, and he doesn’t reopen the case. What your sin can bring on you is his fatherly displeasure. He may hide the light of his face until you come back humbled and confess it. He may use the rod. Yet he won’t take his lovingkindness from you (Psalm 89:30-33 (opens in a new tab)). This is fatherly correction rather than a new verdict of condemnation.

My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous: And he is the propitiation for our sins:

1 John 2:1-2 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Your advocate doesn’t plead that you’re innocent. He pleads his own blood. That plea doesn’t wear thin as your record grows, because it never rested on your record.

There's a difference between falling into sin and making peace with it. A person who plans sin, excuses it, and refuses correction shouldn't use perseverance as permission to go on. Scripture warns against hardening the heart (Hebrews 3:12-13 (opens in a new tab)). If that's you, repent today. Don't reassure yourself with an old profession while resisting the Lord's commands.

So don’t hide the sin, and don’t make peace with it. Remaining sin may win for a time, but the part of you that's born again will overcome, because the Spirit of Christ keeps giving it strength (WCF 13.3 (opens in a new tab)). For the long fight itself, read Struggling With Sin.

What I did is too great to forgive

You may carry one sin heavier than all the rest. If you did it after you came to Christ, it may feel heavier still.

As there is no sin so small but it deserves damnation, so there is no sin so great that it can bring damnation upon those who truly repent.

Westminster Confession of Faith 15.4 (opens in a new tab)

David took another man’s wife and had the man killed to cover it. When he confessed, Nathan told him the Lord had put away his sin (2 Samuel 12:13 (opens in a new tab)). The consequences stayed in his house, but the guilt was gone. Paul had hunted Christians into prison. He called himself the chief of sinners. He said Christ showed him mercy as a pattern for everyone who would believe after him (1 Timothy 1:15-16 (opens in a new tab)). If your sin is great, that pattern was set up for sinners like you.

Once you’ve confessed it and turned from it, trusting Christ’s blood and not your confession, the pardon is yours. The memory may come back for years, and the shame with it, but the guilt doesn’t. Take him at his word, and thank him for it.

He must be tired of forgiving me

We get tired of forgiving because every pardon costs us something. Your pardon cost God his Son, and that price was paid once and in full. He isn’t paying it again each time you come.

Who is a God like unto thee, that pardoneth iniquity, and passeth by the transgression of the remnant of his heritage? he retaineth not his anger for ever, because he delighteth in mercy. He will turn again, he will have compassion upon us; he will subdue our iniquities; and thou wilt cast all their sins into the depths of the sea.

Micah 7:18-19 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

He has promised to subdue the very sins he pardons. When Peter asked how often he must forgive his brother, Jesus said seventy times seven (Matthew 18:22 (opens in a new tab)). He doesn’t ask more mercy of you than he shows you.

When the Sin Returns

  1. Confess it each time, right away. Don’t wait until you feel sorry enough. Name the sin to God plainly and thank him for the blood of Christ.
  2. Change the place and the hour. Romans 13:14 (opens in a new tab) says to make no provision for the flesh. If you keep falling in the same place at the same time, change your routine there. Move the phone, leave the room, go to bed earlier.
  3. Tell one person. Pick a mature Christian of your own sex, or an elder, and tell them what you’re fighting. Ask them to check on you every week.
  4. Go to church Sunday. Don't stay home because you fell. Sit under the preached Word. If you're a communicant member, examine yourself and speak with your elders if you're unsure about coming to the Lord's Table (WLC 171-173 (opens in a new tab)).
  5. Write down one verse. Put 1 John 1:9 (opens in a new tab) or Micah 7:18-19 (opens in a new tab) where you’ll see it, and read it when the shame comes back.

While you wait

Do Not Fight This Alone

Tell your pastor or an elder what you’re afraid of. Gather for public worship when you’re able. If you’re confined at home, ask for visits and prayer. If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table, speak with your elders about preparation and doubts. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to weak and doubting believers who sincerely seek Christ and desire to depart from sin (Q&A 172). Doubt alone doesn’t require staying away, but self-examination and the church’s oversight still matter. Will God Keep Me to the End? gives a few things to do while you wait for the fear to lift.

If you can’t tell whether you’ve ever come to Christ, don’t wait to settle it. Come to him now. He promised to cast out none who come (John 6:37), and the gospel is set out for you in full.
