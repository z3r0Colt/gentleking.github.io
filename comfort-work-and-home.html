Job or home loss

When You Lose Your Job or Home

Food, necessary medicine, and somewhere safe to sleep come first. Ask for practical help, keep track of deadlines, and read what Scripture teaches about need.

On this page

  4. If Illness or Disability Has Stopped You Working
  5. Ask the Church to Help

About 11 minutes to read

If you have nowhere safe to sleep tonight, seek emergency shelter now. Ask a trusted person, local housing service, or your church’s deacons for practical help. If your immediate need is food, rent, or utilities, tell someone who can help today.

In the United States, call 211 or use the 211 directory (opens in a new tab) to find local food, housing, or utility assistance. Ask what help is available, who qualifies, and what documents to bring. A referral doesn’t guarantee funding or a shelter place. Tell your deacons the amount and deadline for an urgent bill, and ask for help making calls if you can’t manage them alone.

The Son of God was rich, and for his people’s sake he became poor (2 Corinthians 8:9 (opens in a new tab)). He had nowhere to lay his head (Matthew 8:20 (opens in a new tab)). He isn’t ashamed of your need, and you may grieve what is gone. He said this to his disciples.

Therefore take no thought, saying, What shall we eat? or, What shall we drink? or, Wherewithal shall we be clothed? (For after all these things do the Gentiles seek:) for your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things. But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.

Matthew 6:31-33 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Take no thought means don’t be anxious. Jesus calls his disciples to seek God’s kingdom and trust their Father’s care. Scripture also records faithful people enduring hunger, so don’t read this as a guarantee that Christians always have food or housing. The Larger Catechism teaches us to ask for a competent portion, as to his fatherly wisdom shall seem best (Q&A 193). We ask for daily necessities while using lawful means to obtain them and helping those in need.

Habakkuk knew an army was coming to strip the fields and flocks his people lived on. He wrote this.

Although the fig tree shall not blossom, neither shall fruit be in the vines; the labour of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no meat; the flock shall be cut off from the fold, and there shall be no herd in the stalls: Yet I will rejoice in the Lord, I will joy in the God of my salvation.

Habakkuk 3:17-18 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Habakkuk’s joy wasn’t in the loss but in the God who wasn’t lost. He trembled first (3:16), and you may too. If a fire or a flood took your home, God is still his people’s refuge, a very present help in trouble (Psalm 46:1 (opens in a new tab)). If it took someone you love, that grief has its own place here.

If anyone told you that God wants you rich, or that you lost it for lack of faith, Scripture says otherwise. Paul went hungry too, and learned to be content in hunger and in plenty through Christ (Philippians 4:11-13 (opens in a new tab)). Losing work isn’t a sin. If your own sin had a part in it, confess it to God, who is faithful and just to forgive (1 John 1:9 (opens in a new tab)).

Christ died on the cross in the place of sinners and rose on the third day. By his resurrection God gives his people new birth into a living hope, and an inheritance that fadeth not away, reserved in heaven for them (1 Peter 1:3-4 (opens in a new tab)). If you’re his, no fire, flood, or lost job can reach it.

God’s adopted children are pitied, protected, and provided for by their Father (Westminster Confession 12.1 (opens in a new tab)), often through his church.

Saints, by profession, are bound to maintain an holy fellowship and communion in the worship of God, and in performing such other spiritual services as tend to their mutual edification; as also in relieving each other in outward things, according to their several abilities and necessities.

Westminster Confession of Faith 26.2 (opens in a new tab)

This excerpt states the duty of mutual fellowship and material relief.

Christians are bound to help one another with food, money, and a roof, and the deacons lead that work. Asking for help isn’t a failure of faith. It’s how the church does what Christ gave it to do.

Tell your deacons what help is needed, whether a place to stay, groceries, transportation, or help with a bill. If your church cannot meet the whole need, ask them to help you find additional assistance.

If You May Lose Your Home

If you've received a late notice, eviction notice, court papers, or a utility shutoff warning, read it now and write down the exact deadline. Save the notice and any payment records. A notice is not the same as a court order, and local rules differ. Don't assume you must leave immediately or that you can ignore it. Ask a qualified legal aid service about your rights and the next steps. In the United States, USAGov explains how to seek eviction help (opens in a new tab).

Contact your landlord, lender, or utility company and explain what you can pay. Ask whether a payment arrangement or hardship program is available and get the terms in writing when possible. Contact 211 for local housing and utility referrals (opens in a new tab). Funding and eligibility differ, so ask what documentation is required and whether an application can be made before the deadline.

If you may have to leave, begin a safe plan for your household. Keep identification, medicines, medical records, essential documents, and important contact numbers where you can find them. Consider children, pets, transportation, and how you'll reach work or school. If there is nowhere safe to sleep tonight, ask emergency shelter services, a trusted person, or your deacons for immediate help. A referral doesn't guarantee a place, so ask what options remain if the first service cannot help.

The promise in Matthew 6 (opens in a new tab) doesn't forbid seeking help through the means God provides. You can pray for daily bread while asking the church and appropriate local services for assistance.

If You Have Lost Your Job

Find out when your final paycheck is due and whether benefits or insurance coverage are ending. Save your separation letter, recent pay records, and important employment documents. Eligibility for unemployment benefits depends on state rules, so check promptly even if you're uncertain. USAGov links to state unemployment programs (opens in a new tab). Ask about health insurance options and other assistance if your family has lost coverage.

Tell your household what income has changed and make a short budget based on money you actually have. Give priority to food, safe housing, medicines, necessary utilities, and transport for work or care. Contact creditors before you miss payments where possible. If the gap is larger than your resources, tell the deacons what you need and when it is due. The money guide explains dealing with debts and bills in more detail.

Begin seeking work you can reasonably do. Ask people you trust about openings and keep a record of applications if your benefit program requires it. If you were dismissed unjustly, you may seek qualified employment-law advice about unpaid wages or other rights. If your own wrongdoing contributed, acknowledge it and make restitution where appropriate. Losing a job is not automatically a judgment on your spiritual condition.

Honest labor remains honorable even when the next position pays less or has a different title. Let your elders know when financial trouble begins to affect your household, and ask for practical assistance rather than waiting until the need becomes unmanageable.

If You Can't Pay for Food, Medicine, or Heat

Don't spend days deciding whether you're allowed to ask for help. Tell someone what you need and the date by which you need it. A friend may help with groceries, a church deacon may help coordinate aid, and local agencies can explain what assistance is available. In the United States, call 211 for referrals to nearby food, housing and utility services.

If a medicine is becoming unaffordable, contact the prescribing clinician or pharmacist before skipping doses. Ask whether a lower-cost option or patient assistance program is available. If a utility is about to be disconnected, call the provider and ask about its hardship procedures. Make a note of whom you spoke with and what they said.

Protect yourself from offers that require an upfront fee to guarantee housing assistance or make debts disappear. A legitimate benefit application should be checked through a known government agency, established nonprofit, or trusted provider. Don't give banking credentials to an unsolicited caller promising urgent aid.

If Illness or Disability Has Stopped You Working

Tell your health professional what duties you can no longer manage and ask about documentation or treatment. Check whether your employer offers leave, accommodations, disability benefits or other support. Eligibility depends on the job and applicable law. Ask for qualified advice instead of assuming a program applies to you.

If someone is caring for you or you are caring for another person, read When You Are Caring for Someone. There may be practical ways for family, church members, or services to share some of the burden.

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

You can hear Christ’s call while questions about your suffering remain unanswered. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.
