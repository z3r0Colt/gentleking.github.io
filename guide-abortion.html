What the Bible Says About Abortion

The unborn child bears the image of God. What Scripture says, the hard cases, help for a mother who is afraid, and mercy for the guilty.

About 12 minutes to read

This page makes the case from Scripture that the child in the womb is a person made in God’s image. But some readers need another part first. If you’re pregnant and afraid, read the part for you now. If you’ve had an abortion, or pushed for one, paid for one, or performed one, read the part for you. Christ receives repentant sinners. The weight of your past sin can't exceed the sufficiency of his sacrifice.

What God Says About the Unborn

Every human being bears the image of God. That's why taking an innocent life is so great a wrong.

Whoso sheddeth man’s blood, by man shall his blood be shed: for in the image of God made he man.

Genesis 9:6 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

The question of the unborn child's humanity matters because God's command protects innocent human life. Scripture speaks of him that way again and again.

For thou hast possessed my reins: thou hast covered me in my mother’s womb. I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my soul knoweth right well.

Psalm 139:13-14 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Psalm 139 (opens in a new tab) is poetry. Covered pictures God knitting a body together, and the reins are the inward parts. But the person in the picture is real. David doesn’t say God made some tissue that later became David. He says God covered me. The one praising God in the psalm is the same one who was in the womb.

Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, and I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations.

Jeremiah 1:5 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

God set Jeremiah apart for his work before he was born. Sanctified here means set apart. God deals with the unborn as persons with names and callings.

When Mary came to her cousin Elisabeth, the babe leaped in Elisabeth’s womb for joy (Luke 1:41 (opens in a new tab), 44). Luke uses the same Greek word for that unborn John as for the newborn Jesus in the manger (Luke 2:12 (opens in a new tab)). He uses it again for the infants brought to Jesus (Luke 18:15 (opens in a new tab)). To the Holy Spirit, a baby is a baby, inside the womb or out.

Behold, I was shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me.

Psalm 51:5 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

David traces his own sin back to his conception. Only a person can be a sinner. So Scripture counts a human being as one of Adam’s fallen race from the very start.

The law of Moses guarded the unborn too. If men fought and struck a woman with child, so that her fruit departed, and no harm followed, the man paid a fine. But if harm followed, then thou shalt give life for life (Exodus 21:22-23 (opens in a new tab)). The words for her fruit departing are the plain Hebrew words for children going out. That’s how the Bible speaks of birth. Many Reformed teachers read it as a child born early. If mother and child were unharmed, a fine was paid. If either one was harmed or killed, the same justice applied as for any other person. Some read the passage another way, but no reading of it makes the child nothing.

The Son of God in the Womb

The strongest word on this is the incarnation itself. The eternal Son of God didn’t come into the world as a grown man. He was conceived.

And, behold, thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son, and shalt call his name JESUS.

Luke 1:31 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

The Shorter Catechism says Christ became man by taking to himself a true body and a reasonable soul, being conceived by the power of the Holy Ghost in the womb of the Virgin Mary (WSC 22 (opens in a new tab)). From that moment he was God and man in one person. He passed through every stage of life that we pass through, from the womb on, so that he could redeem people at every stage of it. Christ has been a child in the womb. Every unborn child is that small and that hidden, and none of them is beneath his notice.

What People Say

  1. It is only a clump of cells. So are you, and so am I. From conception the child is a living human being with his own body and his own genetic code, distinct from his mother. Size, development, and where he lives don’t decide whether he is human. A newborn is smaller and weaker than a toddler, and no one thinks he matters less.
  2. It is my body. The child lives in his mother’s body but isn’t part of it. He inherits genetic material from both parents and is a distinct human organism. Scripture says something deeper. Ye are not your own, for ye are bought with a price (1 Corinthians 6:19-20 (opens in a new tab)). No one owns his body in a way that gives him the right to end someone else’s life.
  3. The child cannot live outside the womb yet. Needing someone to survive doesn’t make anyone less of a person. A newborn can’t live a day without care. Neither can a man on a ventilator. God puts the weak in our care so that we will protect them, not so that we may decide whether they live.
  4. It would be cruel to bring a child into poverty or a hard home. A hard life is still a life. The answer to a hard situation is help, not death, and that is where the church is meant to stand (James 1:27 (opens in a new tab)). Many children born into hard places have grown up to know Christ.
  5. The child will be disabled. And the Lord said unto him, Who hath made man’s mouth? or who maketh the dumb, or deaf, or the seeing, or the blind? have not I the Lord? (Exodus 4:11 (opens in a new tab)). A child with a disability bears God’s image as fully as any other. Jesus said of a man born blind that his blindness was so the works of God should be made manifest in him (John 9:3 (opens in a new tab)).
  6. Christians should not push their morality on others. Every law pushes someone’s morality on someone. The only question is whose. God gave rulers the sword to punish evil and protect the innocent (Romans 13:3-4 (opens in a new tab)). Protecting the weakest people in a land is one of the plainest things a ruler is for.

The Hardest Cases

These are real, and the people in them deserve patience, not slogans.

When a child is conceived by rape. Rape is a monstrous evil, and the woman bears no guilt for it. Under the law of Moses, a man who forced a betrothed woman was put to death, and the woman was counted innocent (Deuteronomy 22:25-26 (opens in a new tab)). But the child didn’t commit that crime. The law says every man shall be put to death for his own sin, and not children for their fathers (Deuteronomy 24:16 (opens in a new tab)). Ending the child’s life doesn’t undo the first wrong. It adds a second victim. A woman in this place needs her church to surround her and to carry her and her child. She needs them to see that justice is sought against the man. If she can’t raise the child, adoption is a good and loving choice.

When the mother’s life is in danger. An ectopic pregnancy develops outside the womb and cannot continue normally. It can rupture and cause life-threatening bleeding. Seek prompt medical assessment, and emergency care for severe pain, fainting, or other signs of serious illness. Don’t delay treatment while waiting for church counsel. Treatment aims to preserve the mother’s life when the child cannot survive, and the child’s death is grieved. Medical and legal uses of the word abortion vary. The moral distinction here concerns the purpose and circumstances of treatment. Other pregnancy emergencies require urgent assessment of how to protect both lives wherever possible. Faithful pastoral counsel can accompany medical care. See the NHS guidance on ectopic pregnancy (opens in a new tab).

When the baby has already died. When a doctor removes a baby who has already died, that's not an abortion. It's care for a mother after a death, and she has nothing to be ashamed of. If that's your sorrow, read If your baby died before birth.

If You're Pregnant and Afraid

Maybe you didn’t plan this. Maybe you’re young, or alone, or out of money, or someone is telling you this child will ruin everything. The fear is real, and God sees it.

Lo, children are an heritage of the Lord: and the fruit of the womb is his reward.

Psalm 127:3 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

However this child came, God calls him a gift. He doesn’t give gifts and then walk away from the one who receives them. You don’t have to see the whole road tonight. You only have to keep from the one step that can’t be taken back.

  1. Tell someone who will help. Call your pastor or a Christian friend today. If you have no church, call Option Line, 1-800-712-4357. They can help you find a pregnancy help center near you. Ask the center which services it provides, what they cost, and what its privacy policy covers before arranging an appointment. Seek qualified medical care for pregnancy symptoms or concerns.
  2. Do not let anyone rush you. If someone is pressuring you, threatening you, or hurting you, get safe first. No one has the right to force this choice on you.
  3. Remember adoption. If you can’t raise this child, there are families waiting and praying for one. Giving your child life and a home is an act of love, not of failure.
  4. Pray. Tell God plainly that you’re afraid. Casting all your care upon him, for he careth for you (1 Peter 5:7 (opens in a new tab)).

If You've Had an Abortion

Maybe it was years ago, and you’ve never told anyone. Maybe you were the father who drove her there, or the parent who insisted, or the one who paid for it. Maybe you worked in a clinic. Choosing or helping to end an unborn child’s life is a grave wrong that must be confessed. If you were forced, threatened, or deceived, seek independent help to understand what happened and who was responsible. You must not be made to carry another person’s guilt. Christ receives sinners who repent and trust him.

But hear this as clearly as you have ever heard anything. Your sin isn't beyond the blood of Christ.

As there is no sin so small but it deserves damnation, so there is no sin so great that it can bring damnation upon those who truly repent.

Westminster Confession of Faith 15.4 (opens in a new tab)

King David took a man’s wife and then had the man killed. When the prophet showed him his sin, he didn’t excuse it, and God didn’t destroy him. Psalm 51 (opens in a new tab) is the prayer he prayed afterward.

Deliver me from bloodguiltiness, O God, thou God of my salvation: and my tongue shall sing aloud of thy righteousness.

Psalm 51:14 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Bloodguiltiness is the guilt of having blood on your hands. That's the prayer for you. The apostle Paul once voted for Christians to be put to death (Acts 26:10 (opens in a new tab)). He later wrote that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom he counted himself the chief (1 Timothy 1:15 (opens in a new tab)). If Christ saved the chief, he can save you.

Come now, and let us reason together, saith the Lord: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool.

Isaiah 1:18 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Christ bore the wrath of God for every sin of everyone who trusts in him, this one included. He rose from the dead, and he says, Him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out (John 6:37 (opens in a new tab)). Come to him as you are. Confess the sin by name, and don’t dress it up. Then believe what he says, that he is faithful and just to forgive (1 John 1:9 (opens in a new tab)).

If you’re already in Christ and this was before you knew him, or even after, it isn’t hanging over you. There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1 (opens in a new tab)). You may still grieve your child, and you should. Forgiven grief is different from guilty grief. It weeps, but it doesn’t hide from God.

Tell your pastor. Many churches have women and men who have walked this same road and found mercy. You were never meant to carry this in secret. If the old guilt keeps coming back after you’ve confessed it, read What I did is too great to forgive.

What Christians Should Do

The Larger Catechism says the sixth commandment requires all lawful efforts to preserve the life of ourselves and others, and to protect and defend the innocent (WLC 135 (opens in a new tab)). Opposing abortion isn’t a hobby for a few believers. It's part of keeping the law of God.

Open thy mouth for the dumb in the cause of all such as are appointed to destruction.

Proverbs 31:8 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)
  1. Pray. Pray for mothers in fear, for fathers who have walked away, for the unborn, for clinic workers, and for rulers. Pray that God would turn hearts.
  2. Speak the truth. Say plainly that the unborn child is a person and that abortion takes his life. Say it with tears, not contempt, and never without the gospel.
  3. Help mothers. Give money, time, diapers, meals, a spare room. Support a pregnancy help center. A church that says no to abortion must say yes to the mother and her child.
  4. Consider adoption and foster care. God set the solitary in families (Psalm 68:6 (opens in a new tab)). Christians have taken in unwanted children since the earliest days of the church.
  5. Vote and call on rulers to protect life. Laws don’t change hearts. But they do restrain evil and protect the weak, which is what God gave them for.
  6. Welcome the guilty. There are people in your church who have had abortions. Let them hear from you that Christ forgives, so they never have to wonder whether there is room for them at his table.
