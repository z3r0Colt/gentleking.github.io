Loneliness

When You're Lonely

Ask for a definite visit, call, or shared meal. Find counsel for isolation, being overlooked at church, and receiving care when you can't get out.

  5. If You Cannot Get to Worship

About 9 minutes to read

Tell one trusted person what you need, such as a regular call, a shared meal, or help getting to worship. If you’ve tried and been overlooked, ask an elder for specific help. If you’re confined at home, the visiting guidance below is for you.

Loneliness comes in many shapes. You may be single and wish you weren’t. You may be divorced, or the last of your old friends still living. You may have moved where no one knows your name. You may be married and still alone in your own house. Or you may sit in church every Lord’s Day and leave without a word from anyone. If your husband or wife has died, that sorrow has its own page.

David knew this. He wrote it in a cave, hiding from a king who wanted him dead.

I looked on my right hand, and beheld, but there was no man that would know me: refuge failed me; no man cared for my soul.

Psalm 142:4 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

A refuge is a place to run to. David didn’t pretend he had friends he didn’t have. He said it to God, and in the next breath he said, Thou art my refuge and my portion in the land of the living (142:5). You may say it to God the same way, plainly, before you feel anything better.

Turn thee unto me, and have mercy upon me; for I am desolate and afflicted.

Psalm 25:16 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

The Son of God was left alone too. The night before he died, he asked three friends to watch with him, and they slept (Matthew 26:38-40 (opens in a new tab)). Within hours all the disciples forsook him, and fled (26:56). He had told them it would come.

Behold, the hour cometh, yea, is now come, that ye shall be scattered, every man to his own, and shall leave me alone: and yet I am not alone, because the Father is with me.

John 16:32 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

On the cross he cried, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? (Matthew 27:46 (opens in a new tab)). He bore that forsaking in the place of sinners, and on the third day he rose. So those who trust him will never be forsaken. He has said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee (Hebrews 13:5 (opens in a new tab)). If you’re his, the loneliness you feel tonight is real. But it doesn’t tell you the truth about where you stand. He is with you.

Paul was alone at the end of his life too. Most of his friends had gone, and one had left him, having loved this present world (2 Timothy 4:10 (opens in a new tab)).

At my first answer no man stood with me, but all men forsook me: I pray God that it may not be laid to their charge. Notwithstanding the Lord stood with me, and strengthened me;

2 Timothy 4:16-17 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Paul’s loneliness didn’t keep him from speaking kindly of those who failed him. You can ask the Lord for that grace while still telling the truth about how much their absence hurts.

God Gives the Lonely a Family

God doesn’t leave his people alone with him and no one else. The same psalm that calls him a father of the fatherless says, God setteth the solitary in families (Psalm 68:5-6 (opens in a new tab)). He does it through his church.

Jesus said that whoever does the will of God is his brother, and sister, and mother (Mark 3:35 (opens in a new tab)). To those who leave house and family for his sake he promised houses, and brethren, and sisters, and mothers, and children, now in this time, with persecutions (Mark 10:29-30 (opens in a new tab)). Those are the people of God, and if you’re his, they’re given to you.

Saints, by profession, are bound to maintain an holy fellowship and communion in the worship of God, and in performing such other spiritual services as tend to their mutual edification; as also in relieving each other in outward things, according to their several abilities and necessities.

Westminster Confession of Faith 26.2 (opens in a new tab)

Church membership creates real duties toward one another (1 Corinthians 12:25-26 (opens in a new tab)). A congregation may fail to notice a quiet member, and that failure should be corrected. It’s right to tell your elders when you need company, while remembering that the duty to offer fellowship belongs to the whole church.

If You're Single

If you long to be married, the longing isn’t sin. God himself said, It is not good that the man should be alone (Genesis 2:18 (opens in a new tab)). But marriage is his gift to give, and he doesn’t give it to every one of his people in this life. Paul was unmarried, and he said that every man hath his proper gift of God, one after this manner, and another after that (1 Corinthians 7:7 (opens in a new tab)). A single believer isn’t loved less by God than a married one.

If you marry, marry only in the Lord (1 Corinthians 7:39 (opens in a new tab)). Loneliness is a poor counselor in that choice. Talk it over with your elders and with believers who know you.

If You're Married and Alone

You may share a house and a name with someone and still be alone. Your husband or wife may not believe, or may have grown cold. Or they may simply not speak of the things you most need to say. Peter wrote to wives whose husbands obey not the word (1 Peter 3:1 (opens in a new tab)), so God hasn't overlooked you. Tell your pastor. If you’re afraid at home, get safe first. If your marriage is breaking, that has its own page.

When the Church Has Overlooked You

You may have attended public worship faithfully without being included in the ordinary friendships of the congregation. Some people are too ill, shy, or exhausted to make every first move. Other members should notice who is absent from meals and conversation and invite them in (Romans 12:13 (opens in a new tab)).

Tell an elder how long this has been happening, and ask for specific help meeting other members. If your congregation has a pattern of neglecting people who live alone, the elders should address it with the church. Fellowship grows through ordinary visits and shared meals as well as public worship.

If You Cannot Get to Worship

Age, illness, a disability, or caring duties may keep you at home. Ask the elders for visits, prayer, and help hearing the Word. Other members can call, bring food, or spend an afternoon with you.

If you can attend but find the gathering overwhelming, speak to a trusted member about sitting with them. A personal relationship may begin slowly. The church’s love is more than a Sunday greeting.

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

You can hear Christ’s call while questions about your suffering remain unanswered. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.
