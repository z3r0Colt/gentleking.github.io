Fighting Sin

Struggling With Sin

For anyone worn down by a sin that will not let go. A heart gives up an old love only for a stronger one, and Christ is the one love strong enough to drive the others out.

The Short Answer

Bring your sin into the light and turn to Christ again. Fight it by the Spirit through the means God gives, with the help of your church.

Read this first

If You're Tired of Losing

You may have fought this sin for years. You may be ashamed to make God the same promise one more time. This page is for you, and it doesn’t begin with a new promise.

If your sin is hurting another person, especially a child, it stops now. Read My sin is hurting someone else before anything else.

In danger, or thinking of ending your life? Call 911, or call 988 or text 988, now, and tell someone near you.

If someone is hurting you, read If You Are Not Safe.

Outside the United States, call your local emergency number.

If you write to me about your sin, know first that I can’t keep a secret that leaves someone in danger. If your sin is hurting a child or anyone else, go to your pastor and the authorities before you write to me.

Most of us tell God we’re done with a sin, and for a while the promise holds. Then a bad night comes, and the old sin walks back in as if it still had a key.

A sin keeps its hold because the heart still loves it. So this page begins with what you love, and with the King you’re coming to, who will not break a bruised reed (Isaiah 42:3 (opens in a new tab)).

If you’re not sure you’ve ever come to Christ, read Where to Start first. If you’re near despair, go straight to When You Feel Like Giving Up. If there’s a sin you love and mean to keep, there’s an honest word for you too. If you use Mortify (opens in a new tab) and temptation is on you now, open it and press Flee. Come back here later.

If what troubles you is a thought you hate and never chose, read Doubts and Unwanted Thoughts first, since a page like this can feed that fear. For you, the searching should be short and the looking to Christ long.

The root

Why Promises to Stop Don't Last

Sin breaks God’s law, and at its root it’s love turned from God to something else. That’s why a promise to stop so seldom holds.

The Westminster Shorter Catechism, part of the standard this site holds, begins with what you were made for.

Man’s chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him for ever.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 1 (opens in a new tab)

Your heart was built to want, and it never stops. The wanting is God’s gift, and sin has bent it. God named what we do instead through Jeremiah.

For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water.

Jeremiah 2:13 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

A cistern was a pit cut in rock to catch the rain, and a broken one leaks. The sin you keep going back to is a cistern you dug. It promised rest after a hard day, or the feeling of being wanted, and it never held. Paul calls covetousness idolatry, love given to something that should never have had it (Colossians 3:5 (opens in a new tab)).

Thomas Chalmers, a Scottish minister, preached on this two hundred years ago in a sermon, The Expulsive Power of a New Affection. An affection is what the heart loves, and expulsive means driving out. The heart must have something to cling to, he said, and the only way to dispossess it of an old affection is by the expulsive power of a new one. Jesus told of a house swept clean and left empty, and of what came back to fill it (Matthew 12:43-45 (opens in a new tab)).

Nobody blows out his candle because someone proves it’s small. He sets it down when the sun is up.

Chalmers didn’t mean that any new interest will do. The love that drives out the love of the world, he said, comes only when a man sees by faith God’s glory in the face of Jesus Christ. It comes when he hears Christ call all who will to a full pardon.

Willpower can sweep the house. It can't fill it.

A greater affection

Look to Christ

The one love strong enough to drive out the others is love for Christ, and it grows from seeing his love for sinners. God doesn’t ask you to work it up. He shows you his Son.

But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord.

2 Corinthians 3:18 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

A glass is a mirror, and the mirror is the gospel, which shows the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ (2 Corinthians 4:6 (opens in a new tab)). We slowly become like what we behold, and the Spirit does it. Paul says this of everyone whose heart has turned to the Lord (3:16).

Beholding him doesn’t mean picturing his face (Larger Catechism Q&A 109). It means looking by faith at who Scripture shows him to be and what he does. Read the Gospels slowly. Watch him touch a leper (Mark 1:41 (opens in a new tab)) and eat with sinners (Luke 15:2 (opens in a new tab)). Sit under preaching that holds him out. If you’re a communing member of a faithful church, come to his Table. God grows a new love by these ordinary means.

He offered living water to the woman at the well before she had changed anything, though her life was a row of broken cisterns (John 4:13-18 (opens in a new tab)). He offers the same water to you. Where his pardon is known, love follows. Of the woman who wept at his feet he said, Her sins, which are many, are forgiven (Luke 7:47 (opens in a new tab)). Her love was the fruit of that pardon, and it was her faith that saved her (7:50). We love him, because he first loved us (1 John 4:19 (opens in a new tab)).

John Owen, an English Puritan pastor, gave the same counsel in his book Indwelling Sin, to fill your affections with the cross of Christ, that there may be no room for sin.

If you're not his yet

Where to Start

Only those who belong to Christ can put sin to death. If you’re not his yet, your fight begins with him.

Mortify means to put to death. Before giving any direction, Owen said that unless a man is truly joined to Christ, he can never mortify any one sin. Paul says that if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his (Romans 8:9 (opens in a new tab)).

So conversion comes first, and Owen said that’s no unkindness. If a man patches a hole in his wall while the house burns, the friend who points to the fire isn’t his enemy.

Sin is a master, and only the Son can make you free indeed (John 8:34-36 (opens in a new tab)). He lived the life you haven’t lived. He bore the curse in the place of all who trust him, and he rose again. He is freely offered to you, and he turns away no one who comes.

If your sin is harming someone, stop today and get help (My sin is hurting someone else). Stopping is right, but it can’t make you his. The promises on this page belong to those who belong to Christ, and they can be yours. Come to Him tells you how.

Mortification

Putting Sin to Death

Once the heart has seen Christ, it turns against the old love that still pulls at it. The Spirit gives it strength to put that sin to death.

Paul joins what you love to what you do, and the hinge is one word.

If ye then be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ sitteth on the right hand of God. Set your affection on things above, not on things on the earth. For ye are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God. When Christ, who is our life, shall appear, then shall ye also appear with him in glory. Mortify therefore your members which are upon the earth; fornication, uncleanness, inordinate affection, evil concupiscence, and covetousness, which is idolatry:

Colossians 3:1-5 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

The therefore in verse 5 hangs on verses 1 to 4. If you’re his, you died with Christ and your life is hid with him in God. Only then does Paul say, Mortify. Inordinate affection is love out of order, and concupiscence is evil desire. The members to mortify are the sins in Paul’s list, not the limbs of the body.

Jesus spoke the same way when he said to pluck out a right eye, or cut off a right hand, that offends, which means one that makes you stumble (Matthew 5:29-30 (opens in a new tab)). He had just put lust in the heart (5:28), and a man with one eye can still lust. It’s a picture of cutting off whatever feeds the sin, even what you value most. God never asks you to harm your body (Larger Catechism Q&A 135-136). If you’re his, your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost (1 Corinthians 6:19 (opens in a new tab)). If you have hurt yourself, or feel driven to, call 988 or text 988 now. If you’re badly hurt, call 911.

In 1656 Owen built his book Of the Mortification of Sin in Believers on one verse.

For if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die: but if ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live.

Romans 8:13 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Ye means believers, who are under no condemnation (Romans 8:1 (opens in a new tab)). The flesh is the old sinful nature, not skin and bone. Ye do mortify means the work is yours, and through the Spirit means the strength is his. The if shows the path the believer walks. It doesn’t set a price on life, for Christ has already paid that for all who are his.

Owen said that even the best believers must make this their business all their days. As he put it, be killing sin or it will be killing you.

Read the full confessional statement

They who are once effectually called and regenerated, having a new heart and a new spirit created in them, are further sanctified, really and personally, through the virtue of Christ’s death and resurrection, by his Word and Spirit dwelling in them; the dominion of the whole body of sin is destroyed, and the several lusts thereof are more and more weakened and mortified, and they more and more quickened and strengthened, in all saving graces, to the practice of true holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord.

Westminster Confession of Faith 13.1 (opens in a new tab)

Quickened means made alive, virtue means power, and a lust is any strong sinful desire. Saving graces are the holy habits the Spirit grows in God’s people, such as faith and humility. Notice the words more and more. The change is real and slow, and never finished in this life (Westminster Confession 13.2 (opens in a new tab)).

Owen warned that a calm temper, a hidden sin, or zeal after a scare kills nothing. The man who trades one sin for another hath changed his master, but is a servant still.

The real thing is slower. Owen pictured a man nailed to a cross, who cries out loudly at first and more faintly as his blood drains away. He took the picture from Paul, who says, They that are Christ’s have crucified the flesh with the affections and lusts (Galatians 5:24 (opens in a new tab)). Sin in a believer dies that way, and a new grace grows in its place. Owen also asked why you fight. True mortification, he said, rests on hating sin because it's sin, not only because it shames you, and on a sense of Christ’s love shown at the cross.

The daily work

Your Part and the Spirit’s Part

The Spirit doesn’t do this in your place, and you can’t do it without him. The Confession holds both together.

Read the full confessional statement

Their ability to do good works is not at all of themselves, but wholly from the Spirit of Christ. And that they may be enabled thereunto, besides the graces they have already received, there is required an actual influence of the same Holy Spirit to work in them to will and to do of his good pleasure; yet are they not hereupon to grow negligent, as if they were not bound to perform any duty unless upon a special motion of the Spirit; but they ought to be diligent in stirring up the grace of God that is in them.

Westminster Confession of Faith 16.3 (opens in a new tab)

A special motion means a special stirring. Some teach that a Christian should stop trying and let God do it, and the Confession won’t have it. Owen said of the Spirit that he works in us and with us, not against us or without us. God works in, so you work out (Philippians 2:12-13 (opens in a new tab)), and work out never means work for.

If you’re his, do these things, and begin each day with the first.

  1. Look to Christ before you look at the sin. Owen’s chief direction for killing sin was to set faith at work on Christ. He called Christ’s blood the great sovereign remedy for sin-sick souls. Come to the throne of grace for mercy for the last fall and grace for the next hour (Hebrews 4:16 (opens in a new tab)).
  2. Strike at the first stirring. A man is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed, before the desire ever conceives and brings forth sin (James 1:14-15 (opens in a new tab)). Strike before it conceives.
  3. Cut off what feeds it. Make not provision for the flesh (Romans 13:14 (opens in a new tab)). With each sin God forbids all the causes, means, occasions, and appearances thereof (Larger Catechism Q&A 99). Put the phone out of the bedroom at night. Take a different road home. End the friendship that keeps leading you back. When you must, run. Joseph left his garment in her hand, and fled, and got him out (Genesis 39:12 (opens in a new tab)).
  4. Bring it into the light. The Confession asks you to repent of your particular sins particularly (15.5). That means naming this sin to God plainly, and then being done. It doesn’t mean listing every passing thought. Then tell your elders, or a believer you trust of your own sex. Sin deceives, and God’s remedy is to exhort one another daily (Hebrews 3:13 (opens in a new tab)). If you’re young, tell a parent, or another adult you trust, such as a teacher or your pastor. If you’re the pastor, tell an elder or a minister you trust.
  5. Keep to the means. Watch and pray (Matthew 26:41 (opens in a new tab)). Put your baptism to use by drawing strength from the death and resurrection of Christ, into whom we are baptized, for the mortifying of sin (Larger Catechism Q&A 167). Sit under the Word in a church, and not only on a screen. If you’re a communing member, come to the Table (Life After You Believe).

Jeremiah 3:22 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Particular sins

Porn, Adultery, Anger, and Addiction

Some sins bring danger with them, to you or to someone near you. They’re fought the same way, with your eyes on Christ, but they need more help than a page can give.

If none of these is yours, go on to When You Feel Like Giving Up.

Two things first. If you cut or starve or purge yourself, see a doctor this week. If you feel driven to hurt yourself, call 988 or text 988 now. And what was done to you by force, or by an adult when you were a child, isn’t your sin to put to death (If You Are Not Safe).

I can't stop looking at what I shouldn't

If these are thoughts you hate and never chose, this section isn’t for you. Go to Doubts and Unwanted Thoughts.

If it’s pornography, name it to yourself and to God. Christ was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin (Hebrews 4:15 (opens in a new tab)), and there's grace to help at his throne (4:16). Put the screen out of reach at the hours you fall, and tell someone. If you’re young, tell a parent, or if you can’t, another adult you trust. If you’re married, ask your pastor or an elder to help you bring it into the light at home.

If you’re an adult and what you look at shows anyone under eighteen, a real child was abused to make it, and looking at it's a crime. Stop today, and read My sin is hurting someone else.

If you’re young and an adult showed you these things or asked you for pictures, it’s being done to you. The same is true if anyone at any age threatens to share pictures of you. Don't pay, and don't send more. Stop answering, and don’t delete the messages. If you’re young, tell a parent or another adult you trust today. If you’re grown, tell the police. If it feels like more than you can live through, call 988 or text 988.

I've been unfaithful

If it’s still going on, end it today, with no contact, as Joseph fled (Genesis 39:12 (opens in a new tab)). If the affair was physical, see a doctor and be tested, for your spouse’s sake and your own. For confessing to your husband or wife, read If you are the one who broke faith. If you catch yourself thinking your family would be better off without you, that’s a lie. Call 988 or text 988.

My anger frightens my family

A harsh husband or wife, father or mother, is the opposite of the gentle King. Tears after an outburst aren’t yet repentance. If you have hit, choked, forced, or threatened anyone at home, or broken things to frighten them, that's abuse and a crime. Give them the distance they ask for. Don't press them to forgive you, to take back what they told anyone, or to sit in counseling with you. Tell your elders, and accept that the police may be involved. If you’re afraid you'll hurt someone, leave the house and call 911. Give any guns in the house to someone to keep. If you’re the one who is afraid, read If You Are Not Safe.

Drink or drugs have hold of me

Paul sets a better filling against drink, Be filled with the Spirit (Ephesians 5:18 (opens in a new tab)). God works through means (Westminster Confession 5.3 (opens in a new tab)), and a doctor and treatment are among them. If you drink heavily every day, or take a pill like Xanax or Valium every day, don't stop all at once on your own. That can bring seizures and can kill. See a doctor this week, tell the whole truth about how much you use, and let someone go with you. Tell your pastor or an elder too. After even a few days off opioids, your old dose can kill you. If someone has taken too much, call 911, and give naloxone (Narcan) if you have it. Don’t stop a prescribed medicine without asking your doctor.

My sin is hurting someone else

If these are thoughts you hate and never chose, this section isn’t for you. Go to Doubts and Unwanted Thoughts.

If you haven’t acted, but you fear a desire toward a child, don't be alone with children. Tell a doctor and your pastor this week. A doctor can tell a fear you hate from a desire you must fight, and there’s help for both.

If you've harmed someone, the harm stops first. If it touches a child, stay away from children today, your own included. If the child lives with you, you’re the one who leaves the house, today. Don't ask the child or anyone in the family to keep it quiet. Tell your pastor, and know that harm to a child must be reported to the police or to child protective services. A faithful church won’t keep it secret.

Accept what the law does (Romans 13:4 (opens in a new tab)), for pardon with God doesn’t cancel earthly justice. Christ turns away no one who comes, and coming to him means coming into the light, for he that doeth truth cometh to the light (John 3:21 (opens in a new tab)). This time is dangerous for your own life too. If you’re thinking of ending it, call 988 or text 988 now.

Assurance

When You Feel Like Giving Up

If you’re his, you don’t fight for your place with God. You fight from it.

Justification is God’s verdict that pardons a sinner and counts him righteous for Christ’s sake. Sanctification is his lifelong work of making him holy. The Larger Catechism sets them side by side.

Read the full confessional statement

Although sanctification be inseparably joined with justification, yet they differ, in that God in justification imputeth the righteousness of Christ; in sanctification his Spirit infuseth grace, and enableth to the exercise thereof; in the former, sin is pardoned; in the other, it is subdued: the one doth equally free all believers from the revenging wrath of God, and that perfectly in this life, that they never fall into condemnation; the other is neither equal in all, nor in this life perfect in any, but growing up to perfection.

Westminster Larger Catechism Q&A 77 (opens in a new tab)

Imputeth means credits to your account, and infuseth means pours in. The pardon is the same in every believer and perfect today, whatever your week was like. The subduing of sin is uneven in all and finished in none. Your standing rests on the first, and the second is the evidence that you have it (Justification by Faith Alone). A boy who breaks a window is in trouble with his father, but he isn’t an orphan again.

The Confession gives assurance three grounds. They're God’s promises, the graces he works in his children, and his Spirit’s witness (Am I Really Saved?). In Grace Abounding, John Bunyan tells how he saw that his good frame of heart didn't make his righteousness before God better, nor his bad frame make it worse. He wrote, my righteousness was Jesus Christ Himself.

Some think fear keeps a struggling Christian careful. The Confession says otherwise. A true believer may wait long and fight hard before he has assurance, so lacking it doesn’t prove you aren’t his. Seek it all the same. The Confession calls that seeking a duty, and assurance makes a Christian stronger in the fight, never looser.

Read the full confessional statement

This infallible assurance doth not so belong to the essence of faith, but that a true believer may wait long, and conflict with many difficulties before he be partaker of it: yet, being enabled by the Spirit to know the things which are freely given him of God, he may, without extraordinary revelation, in the right use of ordinary means, attain thereunto. And therefore it is the duty of every one to give all diligence to make his calling and election sure; that thereby his heart may be enlarged in peace and joy in the Holy Ghost, in love and thankfulness to God, and in strength and cheerfulness in the duties of obedience, the proper fruits of this assurance: so far is it from inclining men to looseness.

Westminster Confession of Faith 18.3 (opens in a new tab)

Looseness means careless living.

So Paul promises all who are under grace, For sin shall not have dominion over you, and he gives the reason, for ye are not under the law, but under grace (Romans 6:14 (opens in a new tab)). A hired hand works in dread of losing his place. That slavish fear, as the Confession calls it (20.1), never taught a heart to love. A son works his father’s field because it’s his father’s. His fear is a son’s fear of grieving the father who loves him, and in that fear believers go on perfecting holiness (2 Corinthians 7:1 (opens in a new tab)).

I fell again today

Then go back to Christ tonight, sin and all. Micah spoke for God’s people when they were brought low for their sin. When I fall, I shall arise, he says, and in the next breath, I have sinned against him (Micah 7:8-9 (opens in a new tab)). After Peter denied him, the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter (Luke 22:61 (opens in a new tab)), and Peter wept and was restored. Judas was sorry too (Matthew 27:3 (opens in a new tab)), but he carried his sorrow away from Christ, the sorrow of the world (2 Corinthians 7:10 (opens in a new tab)). Carry yours to him.

One grave sin can shake your assurance for a while, yet the seed of God remains in his children (Westminster Confession 18.4 (opens in a new tab), and see When God Feels Far). Confess it, for he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins (1 John 1:9 (opens in a new tab)). If you fear he must be tired of forgiving you, read When the Same Sin Keeps Coming Back. If the shame tells you that you’d be better off dead, or that others would be better off without you, it’s lying. Call 988 or text 988 now, and tell someone near you.

I don't feel that I love him

You may not feel your love for him, yet long to be free of your sin and to love him more. Look at the longing itself. Owen said that longing, breathing, and panting after deliverance is a grace in itself. Peter wrote to believers, Whom having not seen, ye love (1 Peter 1:8 (opens in a new tab)). The Larger Catechism (Q&A 172) asks what you want, not whether you have won yet.

You must truly be Christ’s to put sin to death, Owen said. But you needn’t first be sure that you are (Westminster Confession 18.3 (opens in a new tab)). Your hope rests on Christ and his promise, not on how the fight is going. The fight itself is one of the marks of his Spirit in you. So look to Christ, and fight. Richard Sibbes wrote in The Bruised Reed that there's more mercy in Christ than sin in us.

An honest word

If You've Made Peace With It

This part isn’t for the one who hates his sin and keeps losing. It’s for the one who has stopped fighting and made a home for it.

If you hate your sin, even as you fall, or if reading this makes you want to fight, it isn’t describing you. Go on to the last section.

The first half of Romans 8:13 (opens in a new tab) is a real warning to the church. If ye live after the flesh, ye shall die. Owen said that a man who finds no fight in himself against his sin is at peace with it, not dying to it.

Read the full confessional statement

Although hypocrites and other unregenerate men may vainly deceive themselves with false hopes and carnal presumptions of being in the favour of God and estate of salvation, which hope of theirs shall perish: yet such as truly believe in the Lord Jesus, and love him in sincerity, endeavouring to walk in all good conscience before him, may in this life be certainly assured that they are in the state of grace, and may rejoice in the hope of the glory of God, which hope shall never make them ashamed.

Westminster Confession of Faith 18.1 (opens in a new tab)

Carnal presumptions are hopes the sinful heart takes for granted. What divides the hope that perishes from the hope that never shames is a true faith in the Lord Jesus. Sincere love for him and an honest effort to walk with him always come with it.

If you love a sin and mean to keep it, you have no ground to call yourself safe, whatever you once prayed or professed. God’s foundation carries two sayings on one seal, The Lord knoweth them that are his, and, Let every one that nameth the name of Christ depart from iniquity (2 Timothy 2:19 (opens in a new tab)). That’s hard to hear. It’s said plainly because the door is still open.

Go to your elders and tell them the truth. Paul says, Let a man examine himself, and so let him eat (1 Corinthians 11:28 (opens in a new tab)). A man who means to keep his sin shouldn’t come to the Table until he has turned from it (Westminster Confession 29.8 (opens in a new tab)). Christ has given his church power to keep him from it until he does (Larger Catechism Q&A 173). That discipline is care that aims to bring you back (Where Christ Feeds His People). Owen’s rule, that only a believer can put sin to death, reaches you too. You may need to come to Christ as if for the first time, and you may come today. The goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance (Romans 2:4 (opens in a new tab)).

The end of the fight

The Fight Will End

For all who are his, this fight has an end. The last look at Christ will finish what the first look began.

Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is. And every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself, even as he is pure.

1 John 3:2-3 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Beholding him by faith changes us slowly now. Seeing him will finish it. At death the souls of believers are made perfect in holiness (Afraid of Dying). The sin you’ve fought won’t come with you.

If you’re his, take the same road again tomorrow. The King you fight under will not break a bruised reed (Matthew 12:20 (opens in a new tab)). Of faith set on Christ, Owen wrote, live in this, and thou wilt die a conqueror.

If you’re not his yet, all of this can be yours. Christ died for sinners and rose again, and he is gentle with those who come. Turn from your sin and trust him. Come as you are, today, and he will do the cleaning.

Let your elders know you. If you have no church, find a faithful one. If you’d like someone to pray for you, write to me. I’m not a pastor, so in an emergency don’t wait on me.
