Affliction

Is God Punishing Me?

Illness, loss, or a breaking marriage does not reveal a secret verdict against you. Read about fatherly discipline and the freedom from condemnation believers have in Christ.

On this page

  1. It feels as if God is against me
  2. I think he is punishing me
  3. While You Endure This
  4. Do Not Fight This Alone

About 4 minutes to read

It feels as if God is against me

If you need safety, medical care, food, or shelter, use Find Help first. For those who trust Christ, Paul answers the fear of being condemned by pointing to the cross.

He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?

Romans 8:32 (KJV)

God gave his Son for his people. Paul reasons from that gift to God’s commitment to bring them to glory. A few verses earlier Paul says all things work together for good to those who love God. He doesn’t say all things are good. Many aren’t, and you may be living inside one. Romans 8:29 tells you what the good is. God means to make you like his Son.

For the Lord will not cast off for ever: But though he cause grief, yet will he have compassion according to the multitude of his mercies. For he doth not afflict willingly nor grieve the children of men.

Lamentations 3:31-33 (KJV)

He does bring grief, but he takes no pleasure in it, and his compassion outlasts it. You may weep. Jesus wept over Lazarus, though he knew he was about to raise him.

I think he is punishing me

The punishment for a believer’s sin fell on Christ, and God doesn’t collect the same debt twice. What a believer may receive is chastening. That’s the training a father gives the son he loves. Hebrews 12 says God does it for our profit, that we might be partakers of his holiness. No chastening feels joyful at the time, but afterward it yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness.

God disciplines his children without casting them away (WCF 12.1). But not every illness, loss, or disappointment is correction for a particular sin. When the disciples asked whose sin had made a man born blind, Jesus said it was neither his nor his parents’ (John 9:3). You may never learn in this life why a thing was sent. If you belong to Christ, his sacrifice has removed condemning wrath. You can seek care without first discovering a hidden reason for your suffering.

For Scripture gathered by the kind of loss, a death or a diagnosis or a broken marriage, see Grief and Hard Times.

While You Endure This

  1. Tell God how it feels. Be honest. The psalms are full of believers asking why. Read Psalm 13 and pray it as your own.
  2. If you know you have sinned, confess it and turn from it. Repair harm where you owe that duty. Don’t dig for a hidden cause behind every hard thing. If nothing comes to mind, don’t invent one.
  3. Let people help. Tell your elders and your church what you’re facing. Say yes when someone offers a meal or a ride.
  4. Handle the practical things one at a time. Make the call, pay the bill, keep the appointment. Ask God for strength for today’s list, not next month’s.

While you wait

Do Not Fight This Alone

Tell your pastor or an elder what you’re afraid of. Gather for public worship when you’re able. If you’re confined at home, ask for visits and prayer. If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table, speak with your elders about preparation and doubts. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to weak and doubting believers who sincerely seek Christ and desire to depart from sin (Q&A 172). Doubt alone doesn’t require staying away, but self-examination and the church’s oversight still matter. Will God Keep Me to the End? gives a few things to do while you wait for the fear to lift.

If you can’t tell whether you’ve ever come to Christ, don’t wait to settle it. Come to him now. He promised to cast out none who come (John 6:37), and the gospel is set out for you in full.
