Affliction

Is God Punishing Me?

The diagnosis comes, or the child dies, or the marriage comes apart. Under the grief a harder question rises, about what God thinks of you.

On this page

  1. It feels as if God is against me
  2. I think he is punishing me
  3. What to Do This Week
  4. Do Not Fight This Alone

About 4 minutes to read

It feels as if God is against me

Paul asks who can be against us if God is for us, and he answers with the cross.

He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?

Romans 8:32 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

God has already given the most he had to give. If he meant you harm, he wouldn’t have paid so much for you. A few verses earlier Paul says all things work together for good to those who love God. He doesn’t say all things are good. Many aren’t, and you may be living inside one. Romans 8:29 (opens in a new tab) tells you what the good is. God means to make you like his Son.

For the Lord will not cast off for ever: But though he cause grief, yet will he have compassion according to the multitude of his mercies. For he doth not afflict willingly nor grieve the children of men.

Lamentations 3:31-33 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

He does bring grief, but he takes no pleasure in it, and his compassion outlasts it. You may weep. Jesus wept over Lazarus, though he knew he was about to raise him.

I think he is punishing me

The punishment for a believer’s sin fell on Christ, and God doesn’t collect the same debt twice. What a believer may receive is chastening. That’s the training a father gives the son he loves. Hebrews 12 (opens in a new tab) says God does it for our profit, that we might be partakers of his holiness. No chastening feels joyful at the time, but afterward it yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness.

Chastening and condemnation aren't the same thing. The Father may discipline those he loves, including by letting them feel the seriousness of their sin (Hebrews 12:5-11 (opens in a new tab)). But it's wrong to assume every illness, loss, or disappointment is punishment for a particular offense. God's providence has purposes you may not be able to see.

The Confession says God’s children are chastened by him as by a father, yet never cast off (WCF 12.1 (opens in a new tab)). And not every blow is a rebuke for some particular sin. When the disciples asked whose sin had made a man born blind, Jesus said it was neither his nor his parents’ (John 9:3 (opens in a new tab)). You may never learn in this life why a thing was sent. You can know it wasn’t sent in wrath.

For Scripture gathered by the kind of loss, a death or a diagnosis or a broken marriage, see Grief and Hard Times.

What to Do This Week

  1. Tell God how it feels. Be honest. The psalms are full of believers asking why. Read Psalm 13 (opens in a new tab) and pray it as your own.
  2. If a sin comes to mind, confess it and move on. Don’t dig for a hidden cause behind every hard thing. If nothing comes to mind, don’t invent one.
  3. Let people help. Tell your elders and your church what you’re facing. Say yes when someone offers a meal or a ride.
  4. Handle the practical things one at a time. Make the call, pay the bill, keep the appointment. Ask God for strength for today’s list, not next month’s.

While you wait

Do Not Fight This Alone

A page cannot sit with you or pray over you. Tell your pastor or an elder what you are afraid of. Keep sitting under the preached Word, and if you are a communing member, do not stay away from the Lord’s Table while your doubts are being answered. The Larger Catechism says it was appointed for the relief even of weak and doubting Christians (Q&A 172). Will God Keep Me to the End? gives a few things to do while you wait for the fear to lift.

If you cannot tell whether you have ever come to Christ, do not wait to settle it. Come to him now. He promised to cast out none who come (John 6:37), and the gospel is set out for you in full.
