Comfort

Scripture for the Hard Hour

For the day the hard news comes, and the long days after. King James Scripture and plain counsel, gathered by sorrow. Find yours below.

What Has Happened

In danger, or thinking of ending your life? Call 911, or call 988 or text 988, now.

Someone at home hurting you? Call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788. If a child is being hurt, call 911. If someone checks your phone, they may see this page and your calls. A friend’s phone leaves nothing on yours. Outside the United States, call your local emergency number.

Choose what has happened. Each one opens a page of its own, and you need not read any other.

When someone has died

  • Someone I love has died
  • My husband or wife has died
  • My child has died, before birth or after
  • I fear they did not know Christ
  • Someone I love took their own life

In body and mind

  • A serious illness, new or long
  • My own mind has gone dark
  • I am afraid of dying

At home and at work

  • My marriage is breaking
  • My parents are splitting up
  • My child has turned from the Lord
  • My work or my home is gone
  • I am alone

Fear and doubt

  • I am afraid I am not really his
  • God feels far away
  • It feels as if God is against me
  • What I did is too great to forgive
  • A sin has hold of me and will not let go

Danger

  • I am not safe
  • I am thinking of ending my life

For someone else

  • I came here for someone else
  • I am a pastor or an elder

The Lord is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.

Psalm 34:18 (KJV)

A contrite spirit is a crushed one. You do not have to read everything here tonight, or pray well. In the synagogue at Nazareth Jesus read that he was sent to heal the brokenhearted, and then he said, This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears (Luke 4:18, 21). That is still his work.

The Savior

He Knows This Sorrow

Whatever brought you here, the Son of God has been near it. He did not save us from a distance.

He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief: and we hid as it were our faces from him; he was despised, and we esteemed him not. Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted.

Isaiah 53:3-4 (KJV)

He wept at a grave. He was betrayed by a friend who ate his bread. In the garden he asked for the cup to pass. On the cross he cried out that God had forsaken him, so that those who trust him will never be forsaken (Matthew 27:46, Hebrews 13:5). On the third day he rose, and he lives now. The Larger Catechism says the Son became man so that he might have a fellow feeling of our infirmities, and so that we might have comfort and access with boldness unto the throne of grace (Q&A 39).

For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.

Hebrews 4:15-16 (KJV)

This is your time of need. Come.

Prayer

When You Cannot Pray

Grief can take the words out of your mouth. God has made provision for that too.

Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered.

Romans 8:26 (KJV)

If you belong to Christ, a groan with no words in it can be a prayer. The Spirit himself carries it. And if you are his, the Son is praying for you at the right hand of God, as he prayed for Peter before Peter ever fell (Romans 8:34, Luke 22:32).

When you have no words, borrow the prayers God gave his people. Read Psalm 13 aloud, or Psalm 42, or Psalm 88. Psalm 88 ends in the dark, yet its first words call God the God of my salvation. David asked God to put his tears into his bottle (Psalm 56:8). God has no bottle and needs none. The picture means that he keeps count of every tear, and not one is lost.

The church

Do Not Carry It Alone

This page can hand you Scripture. It cannot sit with you, pray over you, or bring you a meal. Christ gave those things to his church.

Call your pastor or an elder today. You do not have to explain it well. Say that something has happened and ask him to pray with you. If the need is money or meals or rides, tell your deacons. That is what the office is for. Peter told the elders, Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking the oversight thereof (1 Peter 5:2). Letting them do it is not a burden you lay on them. It is the work Christ gave them. When an elder comes, ask when he will come again.

Go to worship on the Lord’s Day, even if you only sit and weep. Sit under the Word, and pray with God’s people. If you are a communing member, come to the Lord’s Table. It was given for the relief of weak and doubting Christians too (Larger Catechism Q&A 172). The Word, the sacraments, and prayer are the means by which Christ gives his people the benefits of his redemption (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88), and by them weak faith is strengthened (Westminster Confession 14.1). Yours may be weak this week. Where you can, put off large decisions for a while, and do not make any of them alone.

If you have no church, find a faithful one near you and go this Sunday. Look for one where Christ is preached from the whole Bible, where baptism and the Lord’s Supper are given as he commanded, and where elders watch over the flock. Then ask how to join it. Christ never meant for one of his sheep to walk alone. If you do not know where to start, write to me. I keep this site. I am not a pastor, and I cannot always answer quickly. In an emergency, do not wait on me. Call 911, or call 988 or text 988. But I will pray for you, and I will help you look for a church.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

One day you may sit with someone else in this same dark. The comfort God gives you now is meant to be passed on.

If you do not know him

Believest Thou This

Maybe you came here because of a death, a diagnosis, a broken home, or a lost job, and you are not a Christian. The promises here belong to those who belong to Christ. They can be yours.

Jesus spoke these words to a grieving woman on the road outside her village, four days after her brother was buried (John 11:17, 20). Then he asked her a question. He asks you the same one.

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

If you came here grieving someone, this is not a word about them. Leave them with God. It is a word about you. Death came into the world through sin, and one day you will stand before God yourself (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27).

But Christ died in the place of sinners and bore the punishment their sins deserved, and on the third day he rose again. Everyone who turns from sin and trusts him alone is forgiven and counted righteous for his sake (Shorter Catechism Q&A 33, 87). He said that whoever comes to him he will in no wise cast out (John 6:37). Call on him tonight, in your own words. Whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved (Romans 10:13). Do not wait until the grief lifts. Come as you are, with the grief.

The gospel page sets it out in full. Read it slowly.

Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.

Matthew 11:29 (KJV)
