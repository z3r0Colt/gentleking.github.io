Grief and Hard Times

For the day the hard news comes, and the long days after. King James Scripture and plain counsel, gathered by sorrow. Find yours below.

What Are You Facing?

If someone you love has died, your home has changed, or you don’t know how to face tomorrow, begin with the trouble you’re carrying. Each article below brings Scripture and counsel to that particular sorrow.

If you're in danger or thinking of harming yourself, use the urgent safety contacts first. Otherwise, find the subject below that fits what you're facing.

You can go straight to any article without reading the others first.

Danger

  • I’m not safe
  • I’m thinking of ending my life

The Lord is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.

Psalm 34:18 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

A contrite spirit is one brought low before God. This psalm teaches that the Lord hears those who cry to him. Jesus announced in the synagogue at Nazareth that he came to heal the brokenhearted (Luke 4:18 (opens in a new tab), 21).

The Savior

Christ Knows Grief Firsthand

The Son of God took our nature and knew sorrow during his earthly life.

He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief: and we hid as it were our faces from him; he was despised, and we esteemed him not. Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted.

Isaiah 53:3-4 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Jesus wept at Lazarus’s grave, suffered betrayal, and prayed in agony before his arrest. On the cross he cried, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? (Matthew 27:46 (opens in a new tab)). As the incarnate Son, he truly suffered in his human nature and bore the judgment due to his people’s sins. The Trinity wasn’t divided, and his divine nature wasn’t separated from the Father. Christ rose on the third day. Through him, every believer has the promise, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee (Hebrews 13:5 (opens in a new tab)). The Larger Catechism teaches that he became man to sympathize with our infirmities and give us access to God (Q&A 39).

For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.

Hebrews 4:15-16 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

He remains a merciful high priest. Bring your sorrow to him.

Prayer

When You Can't Pray

Grief can take the words out of your mouth. God has provided for that too.

Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered.

Romans 8:26 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Paul doesn’t require a grieving believer to find the right words. He says the Spirit helps our infirmities and makes intercession according to God’s will (Romans 8:26-27 (opens in a new tab)). Christ also intercedes for his people at the Father’s right hand (Romans 8:34 (opens in a new tab)). Weakness in prayer doesn’t leave a believer without help.

When you can’t find words, read Psalm 13 (opens in a new tab), 42, or 88. Psalm 88 (opens in a new tab) ends without an easy resolution, but it begins by calling upon the God of salvation. In Psalm 56:8 (opens in a new tab), David pictures God keeping his tears in a bottle. The picture teaches that the Lord knows the grief his people bring to him.

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2 (opens in a new tab)).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1 (opens in a new tab)). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28 (opens in a new tab)). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26 (opens in a new tab)).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

You can hear Christ’s call while questions about your suffering remain unanswered. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12 (opens in a new tab), Hebrews 9:27 (opens in a new tab)). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37 (opens in a new tab)).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.

Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.

Matthew 11:29 (KJV)
