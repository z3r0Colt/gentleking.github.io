When Your Child Has Died

No parent should outlive a child, and every part of you knows it. Whether you lost a baby you barely held, or never held at all, or a son or daughter you raised, Christ hasn't looked away.

In danger, or thinking of ending your life? Call 911, or call 988 or text 988, now. If someone at home is hurting you, read If You Are Not Safe. Outside the United States, call your local emergency number.

About 13 minutes to read

If you lost a baby, before birth or soon after, or a little child, read the part for a little one. If your baby died before birth, in a miscarriage or a stillbirth, the part after it is yours too. If your son or daughter was older, read the part for an older child.

If you lost a baby or a little child

People brought young children to Jesus, some of them infants (Luke 18:15 (opens in a new tab)). When his disciples rebuked those who brought them, Jesus was much displeased, and said, Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not (Mark 10:13-14 (opens in a new tab)). Suffer here means allow.

And he took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them.

Mark 10:16 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

That's his heart toward little ones. Those hands were later nailed to a cross for sinners, and on the third day he rose (John 20:25 (opens in a new tab)). His heart hasn’t changed.

David said to God, Thou hast covered me in my mother’s womb (Psalm 139:13 (opens in a new tab)).

Thine eyes did see my substance, yet being unperfect; and in thy book all my members were written, which in continuance were fashioned, when as yet there was none of them.

Psalm 139:16 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Unperfect means not yet fully formed. David was speaking of life in the womb. God saw your child before your child was fully made. The psalm speaks of God's complete knowledge of your child before birth. However early the loss came, you lost a child.

When King David’s baby son died, David rose from the ground and worshipped (2 Samuel 12:20 (opens in a new tab)). Then he told his servants why.

And he said, While the child was yet alive, I fasted and wept: for I said, Who can tell whether God will be gracious to me, that the child may live? But now he is dead, wherefore should I fast? can I bring him back again? I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me.

2 Samuel 12:22-23 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

David stopped fasting because the time for asking was over. The time for grief wasn’t over. He spoke of his son with hope, and then he went on living. He ate that same day, and he comforted his wife (2 Samuel 12:20 (opens in a new tab), 24). You may weep for your little one.

Earlier in that chapter the prophet Nathan told David that this child would die because of David’s sin. In the same breath he told him, The Lord also hath put away thy sin (2 Samuel 12:13-14 (opens in a new tab)). No prophet has told you that your child died for your sin, and God hasn’t said it. Without a word from God, no one may trace a sorrow to a particular sin (John 9:3 (opens in a new tab), Deuteronomy 29:29 (opens in a new tab)).

Many mothers blame their own bodies. A body that couldn’t carry a child isn’t a sin. If you’re in Christ, there’s no condemnation for you (Romans 8:1 (opens in a new tab)), and your baby’s death isn’t a sentence on you. For a believer even a Father’s chastening is never payment for sin, because Christ paid it all.

A little one can’t understand a sermon or put faith into words. That’s no barrier to the Spirit of Christ, who works when, and where, and how he pleases (Westminster Confession 10.3 (opens in a new tab)). Grace doesn’t wait for anyone to be old enough.

The original Westminster Confession teaches that elect infants dying in infancy are regenerated and saved by Christ through the Spirit (WCF 10.3 (opens in a new tab)). It doesn’t tell us which particular infants are elect. David’s words about his son express his hope, but aren’t a general promise that every child who dies is saved. You can entrust your child to the Judge of all the earth, who will do right (Genesis 18:25 (opens in a new tab)). No one should pretend to know more than Scripture reveals.

If your baby died before birth, the next part is for you. If not, go on to the part for every grieving parent.

If your baby died before birth

A miscarriage or a stillbirth is the death of a child. Some people around you may not see it that way. They may say nothing at all. Or they may say it was for the best, or that you can try again. They usually mean well, but a child isn’t replaced by another child. Scripture counts the little one you carried as a person, known by God and made in his image (Jeremiah 1:5 (opens in a new tab), Luke 1:41 (opens in a new tab)). You aren’t making too much of this. You lost a son or a daughter.

Many mothers search their memories for what they did wrong. Doctors find that most early losses come from causes no mother could have caused or stopped. And even where you wonder, God hasn’t laid this at your feet. He knoweth our frame (Psalm 103:14 (opens in a new tab)).

Fathers grieve this too, though often more quietly and later. A father may feel he has to be strong for his wife and so never mourn at all. Husbands, weep with your wives, and let your church care for you both.

If the loss came early, there may be nothing to bury, and no one outside your home may have known. That can make the grief lonelier. You may still mark it. Give your baby a name, tell your pastor, and pray together as a family. Due dates and the day of the loss may be hard for years, and that isn’t a weakness of faith.

If you’re afraid to hope for another child, or if another child never comes, bring that fear to the God who gives and takes away (Job 1:21 (opens in a new tab)). He isn’t cruel, and he isn’t careless with you.

If in the past you chose to end a pregnancy, you may grieve that child too. A doctor’s care after your baby had already died isn’t that choice. For that choice there's full pardon in Christ, for all who come to him. Read If You Have Had an Abortion and What I did is too great to forgive.

The next part is for parents of an older child. The part for every grieving parent is for you too.

If your son or daughter was older

Jesus once met a funeral at the gate of a town called Nain. The dead man was the only son of his mother, and she was a widow (Luke 7:12 (opens in a new tab)).

And when the Lord saw her, he had compassion on her, and said unto her, Weep not. And he came and touched the bier: and they that bare him stood still. And he said, Young man, I say unto thee, Arise. And he that was dead sat up, and began to speak. And he delivered him to his mother.

Luke 7:13-15 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

A bier was the open frame the dead were carried on. Jesus had compassion on her before she asked him for anything. He could tell her not to weep, because he was about to give her son back. He wasn’t rebuking her tears, or yours.

He didn’t raise every dead son or daughter in Israel. The widow’s son at Nain, and the girl of twelve he took by the hand (Mark 5:41-42 (opens in a new tab)), were signs. They pointed ahead to the last day, when he will call his people out of their graves. They weren’t promises for today.

When a great wind brought a house down on Job’s children, Job tore his robe, fell down on the ground, and worshipped. In all this Job sinned not (Job 1:18-22 (opens in a new tab)). Grief and worship stand on the same ground.

If your son or daughter trusted Christ, no man can pluck them out of his hand (John 10:28-29 (opens in a new tab)). Death couldn’t do it either (Romans 8:38-39 (opens in a new tab)).

The souls of believers are at their death made perfect in holiness, and do immediately pass into glory; and their bodies, being still united to Christ, do rest in their graves till the resurrection.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 37 (opens in a new tab)

If they're his, they’re with him now, and every sin they ever fought is gone.

If you don’t know where your child stood with God, that’s too heavy to carry alone tonight. You aren’t asked to settle where they are. Leave them with the Judge of all the earth, who will do right (Genesis 18:25 (opens in a new tab)), and bring your own soul to Christ. Read When You Fear They Did Not Know Christ, and tell your pastor this week. If your son or daughter took their own life, that sorrow has its own place here.

If someone else took your child’s life, by carelessness or by violence, your anger at that wrong isn’t sin in itself. Scripture says, Be ye angry, and sin not (Ephesians 4:26 (opens in a new tab)). God says, Vengeance is mine, and he promises to repay (Romans 12:19 (opens in a new tab)). He gave the ruler the sword for this very wrong (Romans 13:4 (opens in a new tab)), so pressing for justice in the courts isn’t revenge.

For every grieving parent

Many parents replay every moment, what they ate or lifted, the last night, the keys they handed over, the sign they missed.

Like as a father pitieth his children, so the Lord pitieth them that fear him. For he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust.

Psalm 103:13-14 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

You couldn’t see what only God could see.

Moses told God’s people that underneath are the everlasting arms (Deuteronomy 33:27 (opens in a new tab)). Those arms are a picture, since God is a Spirit, and the care they picture is real. But the Son of God took a real body, with real arms. He took up little children in those arms and blessed them. Then he died for sinners, and on the third day he rose.

Now he says he was dead and is alive for evermore, and holds the keys of death (Revelation 1:18 (opens in a new tab)). Keys picture his authority over death, and that authority is real. He will lose none of those the Father has given him, and he will raise them up at the last day (John 6:39 (opens in a new tab)).

Ask a pastor or friend for help with arrangements. You may grieve a child lost before birth and remember that child by name. If you’re married, listen to how your spouse is grieving. You needn’t decide what to do with your child’s things now.

If you find yourself wanting to follow your child, call 988 or text 988 tonight, and tell someone near you.

When You Keep Asking Why

Your child’s death isn’t a command to search for the particular sin that caused it. The Lord hasn’t given you that knowledge (Deuteronomy 29:29 (opens in a new tab)). Even the account of David’s son in 2 Samuel 12 (opens in a new tab) mustn’t be turned into a verdict on your own family.

Remember your child by name and speak of the days you miss. If you and your spouse grieve differently, listen patiently to each other. Other children may need someone to hear their grief. If you’re unable to function, seek medical care as well as the care of your church. If you begin thinking of following your child in death, tell someone near you and call 988 or text 988.

Help With the Days Ahead

Ask a friend or elder to help with arrangements and daily care. A loss before birth is still worth grieving. If your body is recovering from pregnancy, seek medical care for heavy bleeding or fever. Your family needn’t decide right away what to do with your child’s belongings.

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

You don’t have to settle every question about the person you lost before hearing Christ’s call to you. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.
