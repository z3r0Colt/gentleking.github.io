The church

What About the Hypocrites?

Some of the strongest arguments against Christ come from people who carry his name, and they deserve an honest answer.

On this page

  1. The church is full of hypocrites
  2. Religion has done great harm
  3. Consider the Objection
  4. What God Calls You To Do

About 3 minutes to read

The church is full of hypocrites

Some who sit in churches are hypocrites, and Jesus said there would be (Matthew 7:21-23 (opens in a new tab)). His hardest words were for religious men who looked clean outside and were rotten within (Matthew 23:27-28 (opens in a new tab)). So on this point you and he agree. But a hypocrite fails to live by what he claims. That tells you about the man and nothing against the claim. Counterfeit money is only worth making because real money exists. Many of the Christians you have in mind aren’t hypocrites at all. They’re sinners who know it, which is why they’re in church.

They that are whole have no need of the physician, but they that are sick: I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.

Mark 2:17 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Religion has done great harm

It has, and some of it was done in the name of Christ. There’s no excuse to make, and Reformed Christians have no clean hands to hold up. Geneva burned Michael Servetus for heresy in 1553, and Calvin had pressed for his death. Cruelty like that defies Christ. He rebuked James and John when they wanted to call down fire on a village that would not receive him (Luke 9:54-55 (opens in a new tab)). That rebuke concerns the disciples’ appeal for a village’s destruction. It doesn’t by itself settle every question of civil punishment. The original Confession gives magistrates duties concerning true religion as well as public justice, while distinguishing their office from the church’s ministry. Read the magistrate’s duty toward true religion for that teaching.

But the harm doesn’t leave when religion does. The officially atheist governments of the last century killed their own people by the millions. The trouble is the human heart, which goes wherever men go, into churches and into prison camps alike. Am I a Good Person? turns to it.

Consider the Objection

  1. Read Mark start to finish. It’s the shortest Gospel, and you can read it in an evening. Judge Jesus by what he said and did, not by his followers.
  2. Write down what you hold against Christians. Be specific. Then ask a pastor about it and see whether he defends it or owns it.
  3. Visit a faithful church on Sunday. Listen to what they confess about themselves in the service. Ask whether these people claim to be good, or claim to need a Savior.

Under every objection

What God Calls You To Do

An objection can be answered and a man still stay away. Jesus said that men loved darkness rather than light, because their deeds were evil (John 3:19). The trouble between you and God is not finally an argument. You have sinned against him, and you know it.

Christ came for sinners exactly like you. He kept the law you have broken, and on the cross he bore its curse for everyone who trusts him, and God raised him from the dead. So the call is the one Jesus preached. Repent, and believe the gospel (Mark 1:15). You do not have to settle every question first.
