Widowhood

When Your Husband or Wife Has Died

The one who knew you best is gone, and half of every habit in the house went with them. There's no timetable for this. Christ saw to those left behind even while he was dying.

In danger, or thinking of ending your life? Call 911, or call 988 or text 988, now. If someone at home is hurting you, read If You Are Not Safe. Outside the United States, call your local emergency number.

About 8 minutes to read

When Jesus therefore saw his mother, and the disciple standing by, whom he loved, he saith unto his mother, Woman, behold thy son! Then saith he to the disciple, Behold thy mother! And from that hour that disciple took her unto his own home.

John 19:26-27 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Dying on the cross in agony, he still saw his mother standing there. He gave her a son to look after her, and a home. He hasn’t stopped seeing the one who is left behind.

A father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows, is God in his holy habitation.

Psalm 68:5 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

A judge of the widows is a defender who takes up their cause. God cares for children who have lost a parent. So if you've children at home, he hasn’t overlooked them. Let them see you cry, and let them cry too.

The next verse says that God setteth the solitary in families (68:6). He did that for his mother at the cross, and he does it still through his church. That verse is for the widower as much as the widow.

Paul tells a widow’s own family, and then the church, to honor her and care for her (1 Timothy 5:3-5 (opens in a new tab), 16). If the cards and meals have stopped, you may tell your elders so. That isn’t complaining. It lets the church obey.

Anna lost her husband after seven years of marriage and lived as a widow into great old age. Her years alone weren’t empty. She served God with prayer night and day, and her long road brought her to the child Jesus (Luke 2:36-38 (opens in a new tab)).

For many, the mornings are the hardest. For a moment you forget, and then you remember. God put a whole book of weeping in the Bible. In the middle of it, with his city in ruins, Jeremiah wrote this.

It is of the Lord’s mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.

Lamentations 3:22-23 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Jeremiah wrote these words amid a city’s ruin, after describing his distress in the same chapter. Remembering the Lord’s mercies gave him a reason to hope (Lamentations 3:21 (opens in a new tab)). The believer may endure chastening and grief, but Christ has borne the condemning wrath due to his people’s sins. You can ask the Father for help with the day before you.

The one who saw to his mother from the cross died there. On the third day he rose, and now he dieth no more (Romans 6:9 (opens in a new tab)). He lives to see to his own. Paul says marriage pictures Christ and his church (Ephesians 5:31-32 (opens in a new tab)). Christ’s love for his church endures, and if you’re his, he still holds you.

If you don’t know where your husband or wife stood with God, that’s too heavy to carry alone tonight. You aren’t asked to settle where they are. You may leave them with the Judge of all the earth, who will do right (Genesis 18:25 (opens in a new tab)), and bring your own soul to Christ. Read When You Fear They Did Not Know Christ, and tell your pastor this week. If they took their own life, that sorrow has its own place here.

You may notice the absence most in the daily habits you shared. Tell a friend which part of the day is hardest. A regular call or visit may be more useful than another offer of help that never becomes definite.

When you're ready to ask about heaven

If your husband or wife belonged to Christ, their soul is with him now, made perfect in holiness (Shorter Catechism Q&A 37).

At the resurrection, believers being raised up in glory, shall be openly acknowledged and acquitted in the day of judgment, and made perfectly blessed in the full enjoying of God to all eternity.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 38 (opens in a new tab)

Paul says that when the Lord comes, the dead in Christ shall rise first. Then his people who are still alive will be caught up together with them, and so shall we ever be with the Lord (1 Thessalonians 4:16-17 (opens in a new tab)).

In the resurrection, Jesus said, they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven (Matthew 22:30 (opens in a new tab)). Yet Christ’s people are raised as themselves, with bodies made like his glorious body (Philippians 3:21 (opens in a new tab)). Marriage is God’s gift for this life (Romans 7:2 (opens in a new tab)).

Scripture says less than we might wish about how his people will know one another there. But the one who gave his mother a home from the cross is there now, and he is their joy. If you’re his, you’ll see him on the day he chooses, not the day you choose. Until then, he hasn’t finished with your life here.

Evenings and Holidays

Some couples shared every meal and appointment for decades. After a death, even ordinary tasks can remind you of the person who is gone. You may wish to keep a family tradition, change it, or decline an invitation for a time. Tell family members what would actually help rather than leaving them to guess.

If you have children, they may fear losing you as well. Hear their questions and let them know which adults can help the family. If loneliness grows into hopelessness or you can no longer manage daily life, seek help from your pastor and a doctor.

Bills, Documents, and the House

A surviving spouse may have to deal with accounts, insurance, repairs, and appointments that the other person usually handled. Ask a trustworthy relative or friend to review urgent deadlines with you. Don’t sign away property or make major financial commitments under pressure.

Tell the deacons if you need money, rides, repairs, or help caring for children. The church’s duty toward widows does not expire when the funeral is over (1 Timothy 5:3-5 (opens in a new tab), 16).

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

You don’t have to settle every question about the person you lost before hearing Christ’s call to you. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.
