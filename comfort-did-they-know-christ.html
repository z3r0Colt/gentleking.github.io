The hardest question

When You Fear They Didn't Know Christ

You can grieve without pretending to know your loved one's final state. Read what Scripture reveals about salvation, judgment, and the things God keeps hidden.

On this page

  1. When You Wish You Had Said More
  2. When Others Speak With Certainty
  3. Ask the Church to Help
  4. Christ Is Offered to You

About 7 minutes to read

You can grieve someone without claiming certainty about their salvation. Ask a trusted pastor to read the passages below with you. If you’re helping arrange a funeral, tell him your concerns before the service.

When Jesus came near Jerusalem, he looked at the city that would not receive him, and he wept over it (Luke 19:41). You aren’t wrong to grieve for someone you fear never came to him.

When Absalom died in rebellion against his father, King David didn’t hide his grief.

And the king was much moved, and went up to the chamber over the gate, and wept: and as he went, thus he said, O my son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom! would God I had died for thee, O Absalom, my son, my son!

2 Samuel 18:33 (KJV)

Paul had great heaviness and continual sorrow for his own people who did not believe (Romans 9:2-3). That sorrow wasn’t disloyalty to God, and yours isn’t either.

Abraham once stood before the Lord and asked a hard question about judgment. It came down to this.

Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?

Genesis 18:25 (KJV)

It doesn’t tell you how it went for them. It tells you who decides, and that he will do right. The Shorter Catechism tells you who he is.

God is a Spirit, infinite, eternal, and unchangeable in his being, wisdom, power, holiness, justice, goodness, and truth.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 4

You can leave them with a Judge who is all of that at once. The Father has committed all judgment to the Son (John 5:22), and God gave assurance of it by raising him from the dead (Acts 17:31). The one who will judge them is the one who wept over Jerusalem, died for sinners, and rose again. You may trust him with them.

So no one may preach them into heaven, and no one may pronounce them in hell, not even your own fearful heart. That judgment belongs to Christ.

You may be replaying every word you didn’t say. Paul planted and Apollos watered, but God gave the increase (1 Corinthians 3:6). No one can come to Christ unless the Father draws him (John 6:44). Their salvation was never in your hands. Your love for them wasn’t wasted.

If your conscience still accuses you, bring it to the cross. Christ turns away no sinner who comes to him. For all who trust him, whatever was truly sin he bore there, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin (1 John 1:7).

A thief dying beside Jesus asked to be remembered. Jesus answered, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise (Luke 23:42-43). God’s mercy can reach a sinner in the last hour of life. That’s a truth about God. That truth gives no warrant to declare that your loved one was saved or lost. The secret things belong unto the Lord our God, but those things which are revealed belong unto us (Deuteronomy 29:29). What he has kept secret, you may leave with him. What he has revealed is yours to believe and live by.

If you’re his, on the last day God will wipe away all tears from your eyes (Revelation 21:3-4). How he will wipe away this tear, he hasn’t told us. He has told us that he will, and that the Judge of all the earth will do right.

If they took their own life, that sorrow has its own place here too.

Christ also calls every living hearer to repent and believe. You can bring your grief to him and hear the gospel invitation below without settling what God hasn’t revealed about another person.

When You Wish You Had Said More

You may remember times when you could have spoken of Christ and remained silent. If conscience shows you a real failure, confess it to God. Christ forgives those who come to him. But the salvation of another person was never in your power to accomplish. God gives the increase (1 Corinthians 3:6), and no one comes to Christ unless the Father draws him (John 6:44).

You cannot go back and change the last conversation. You can pray for those still living and speak plainly when opportunities come. Regret should lead you to faithfulness now rather than to inventing an answer about the dead.

When Others Speak With Certainty

At a funeral, one person may confidently speak of heaven while another speaks as if the judgment has already been made. You don’t have to repeat either claim. You can thank people for their kindness, say that God will judge rightly, and leave the matter there.

Scripture tells us the way of salvation through Christ and the certainty of God’s judgment. It doesn’t reveal every person’s last thoughts. Entrusting someone to God’s justice doesn’t require making guesses about unseen grace.

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

You can hear Christ’s call while questions about your suffering remain unanswered. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.
