One Story, Two Testaments

Covenant Theology

God has always dealt with his people by covenant. Learn the covenants and the Bible stops feeling like a pile of disconnected stories.

Scripture speaks of a people given by the Father to the Son before the world began (John 6:37-39 (opens in a new tab), Ephesians 1:4 (opens in a new tab)). Reformed theologians commonly call God's eternal purpose in the work of the Father, Son, and Spirit the covenant of redemption. That name is a theological explanation, not the name of a separate chapter in the 1647 Westminster Confession. The Confession clearly teaches the eternal decree and the Son's saving work (WCF 3 (opens in a new tab) and 8).

In the garden God dealt with Adam in a covenant of works. Perfect obedience, and life. Disobedience, and death. Adam stood there not only for himself but for all who would come from him. He failed, and the ruin spread to every one of us.

After the fall, God promised salvation in Christ. The Confession calls this the covenant of grace (WCF 7.3 (opens in a new tab)).

Man by his fall having made himself incapable of life by that covenant, the Lord was pleased to make a second, commonly called the covenant of grace; wherein He freely offereth unto sinners life and salvation by Jesus Christ; requiring of them faith in Him, that they may be saved, and promising to give unto all those that are ordained unto eternal life His Holy Spirit, to make them willing, and able to believe.

Westminster Confession of Faith 7.3 (opens in a new tab)

Here’s the part that changes how you read. There aren’t two ways of salvation in the Bible, law for the old and grace for the new. There's one covenant of grace administered in two ways. Under the old administration it came through promise, sacrifice, priesthood and shadow. Under the new it comes with the substance itself, Christ crucified and risen, preached to the nations. Abraham was justified by faith exactly as you are. The difference is that he looked forward and you look back.

And if ye be Christ’s, then are ye Abraham’s seed, and heirs according to the promise.

Galatians 3:29 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

The covenant of grace is one in substance under both testaments, not two different ways for a sinner to be justified (WCF 7.5-6 (opens in a new tab)). Its outward administration has changed, and the old sacrifices have passed away in Christ. This matters for baptism as well. The original Confession teaches that infants of believing parents are to receive the covenant sign (WCF 28.4 (opens in a new tab)). The sign doesn't make every recipient regenerate, but God's promise and command aren't limited to adults who can already describe their faith.

So when you open Leviticus, you aren’t reading a foreign religion. You’re watching God teach his people, in blood and smoke and careful ritual, what sin costs and what a substitute is for. When you read the prophets, you’re hearing a covenant Lord pleading with a covenant people. And at the Supper you hear Jesus say the cup is the new covenant in his blood. The whole Old Testament leans forward into that sentence.

For a fuller treatment of the covenant sign, read Baptism. Families can also use Family Worship and Church Membership.
