The Gospel

The Gospel

The Gospel of Jesus Christ

Christ died for His people and is freely offered to every sinner who hears the gospel. He rose bodily from the dead, and everyone who trusts in Him alone is forgiven and counted righteous for His sake.

His command and promise

Come to Him

If you haven't trusted Christ, come to Him. Your sin is a reason you need Him. Don't make waiting until you feel worthy an excuse for refusing His mercy.

Come to Christ now. Your warrant is His command and promise, not an inward sense of being elect. You do not have to finish these explanations before trusting Him.

Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.

Matthew 11:28-30 (KJV)

The God who saves

Who God Is

God made us, and we owe Him our worship and obedience. He is eternal, holy, and unchanging. There is one God in three Persons, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. The Persons are distinct, yet each is fully God. They share one divine being and one will.

God's holiness means He cannot approve of evil. His mercy means sinners have reason to come to Him. These aren't competing parts of His character. At the cross, He saves His people without setting His justice aside. Salvation begins in His eternal purpose of grace, not in anything He foresaw in us.

And one cried unto another, and said, Holy, holy, holy, is the Lord of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory.

Isaiah 6:3 (KJV)

God governs all that he has made and judges in righteousness. His mercy never sets aside his holiness. To understand the salvation he gives, we must understand our need before him.

Our need

What Sin Is and What It Has Done

Sin is any want of conformity unto, or transgression of, the law of God. It includes what we fail to do as well as the wrong we commit (Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 14).

God made Adam upright and placed him under a covenant requiring perfect obedience. Adam disobeyed as the representative head of mankind. All who descend from him by ordinary generation inherit his guilt and a corrupted nature. Christ alone was conceived without sin.

We also commit sins of our own. We break God's law because our hearts are turned from Him. Being spiritually dead doesn't mean we're incapable of thought or choice. It means we won't turn to God in saving faith unless His Spirit makes us alive. We need forgiveness and a new heart. Neither can be earned.

For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;

Romans 3:23 (KJV)

Sin affects every part of us. Total depravity does not mean everyone is as wicked as possible. It means no part of our nature is untouched by sin, and we cannot turn ourselves to God or earn his favor.

God judges sin

The Wrath of God and the Judgment to Come

God's wrath is holy and just. It is never a loss of control. He judges sin because he is righteous, and every one of us must answer to him (Romans 1:18, Hebrews 9:27).

Jesus warned of everlasting punishment and spoke of it alongside life eternal (Matthew 25:46). Those who remain outside Christ will face conscious punishment that does not end. Our need is greater than relief from guilt or help to live better. We need deliverance from sin and its just condemnation.

For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

Romans 6:23 (KJV)

His person and saving work

Jesus Christ, the Gentle King

Jesus Christ is the eternal Son of God who took a true human nature to Himself. He was conceived by the Holy Ghost and born of the virgin Mary. He is one Person, fully God and fully man. His two natures are neither mixed nor divided.

And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.

John 1:14 (KJV)

His humanity includes a true body and a reasonable soul. He shared our human weakness and suffered real temptation, yet he was without sin (Hebrews 2:14-17, 4:15).

He obeyed the law His people had broken. His whole life was righteous, and His death was willing obedience to His Father. We need more than the removal of our guilt. We need the righteousness that Christ alone supplies. God counts His obedience and satisfaction to believers, who receive Him through faith alone.

Christ fulfilled the law perfectly and bore the punishment his people deserved. His obedience continued through his death, and his suffering extended throughout his humiliation. Active and passive obedience name these two aspects of his one saving work (Philippians 2:8).

Christ died as the substitute for His people and is freely offered to every sinner who hears the gospel. He bore the judgment His people's sins deserved and fully satisfied God's justice. His suffering included the anguish of bearing divine wrath, not only the pain inflicted by men.

Propitiation means that Christ's sacrifice satisfied God's justice and turned away His wrath from His people. The Father Himself provided this sacrifice in love.

Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God;

Romans 3:25 (KJV)

Herein is love, not that we loved God, but that he loved us, and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins.

1 John 4:10 (KJV)

But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.

Isaiah 53:5 (KJV)

For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.

2 Corinthians 5:21 (KJV)

His cry of forsakenness must be taken seriously. He was deprived of the comforting sense of His Father's presence as He bore the curse. Yet the Trinity was never divided, the Father never ceased loving the Son, and Christ's divine and human natures remained united. His sufferings were real, and His trust in His Father did not fail.

His sacrifice fully satisfied God's justice and secured salvation for all whom the Father gave him. He reconciles his people to God and redeems them from bondage by his blood. The Father sent his Son in love. Christ's death accomplished the salvation that divine love purposed (John 6:37-39, Romans 5:8-10, Westminster Confession 8.5, 8.8).

On the third day He rose bodily from the dead. He later ascended into heaven, where He reigns and intercedes for His people. His resurrection confirms the victory of His finished work. He will return bodily to raise the dead and judge the world.

For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures:

1 Corinthians 15:3-4 (KJV)

Salvation is God's gift

Grace Alone

God chose his people in Christ before the foundation of the world. His choice was according to his own good pleasure, without any foreseen faith, obedience, or merit in them. The faith through which they receive Christ is itself his gift (Ephesians 1:4-5, 2:8-9, Westminster Confession 3.5).

For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.

Ephesians 2:8-9 (KJV)

Man by his fall having made himself incapable of life by that covenant, the Lord was pleased to make a second, commonly called the covenant of grace; wherein He freely offereth unto sinners life and salvation by Jesus Christ; requiring of them faith in Him, that they may be saved, and promising to give unto all those that are ordained unto eternal life His Holy Spirit, to make them willing, and able to believe.

Westminster Confession of Faith 7.3

In effectual calling, the Spirit renews the sinner and brings him willingly to Christ. Regeneration is the source of faith, not its reward. God justifies the believer through faith alone, on the basis of Christ's righteousness. Faith doesn't merit acceptance. It receives the Savior who has secured it.

In those able to receive the outward call of the gospel, the Spirit's renewing work enables their response of faith. This priority is not a period in which they should wait before believing. They are commanded to trust Christ now (Westminster Confession 10.1-2).

Elect infants dying in infancy, and other elect persons incapable of receiving the outward call through the ministry of the Word, are regenerated and saved by Christ through the Spirit. He works when, where, and how he pleases (Westminster Confession 10.3).

The call of the gospel

Repentance and Faith

God commands everyone who hears the gospel to repent and believe in Jesus Christ. You don't have to discover whether you're elect before coming to Him. Your warrant to come is His command and promise, not an inward sense of being elect.

Saving faith receives and rests upon Christ alone for salvation. Repentance is a turning from sin to God, with grief for sin and a purpose to walk in obedience. Neither is a payment for mercy. Both are gifts of God's grace, and neither should be confused with merely knowing religious facts or feeling afraid of judgment.

Repentance unto life is a saving grace, whereby a sinner, out of a true sense of his sin, and apprehension of the mercy of God in Christ, doth, with grief and hatred of his sin, turn from it unto God, with full purpose of, and endeavour after, new obedience.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 87

Faith in Jesus Christ is a saving grace, whereby we receive and rest upon him alone for salvation, as he is offered to us in the gospel.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 86

Repentance is necessary and always accompanies saving faith. It does not satisfy God's justice or earn pardon. Faith is the only instrument of justification, and the faith that receives Christ is never without the other saving graces (Westminster Confession 11.2, 15.3).

Christ is freely offered to every sinner who hears the gospel. His command and promise are your warrant to come. Religious interest or fear of judgment alone does not prove that you have been born again. You do not need to settle that question before obeying his call. Receive and rest upon Christ himself.

All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.

John 6:37 (KJV)

Accepted for Christ's sake

Justification by Faith Alone

Justification is God's act of pardoning sin and accepting a sinner as righteous for Christ's sake. It changes our standing before him. Its ground is Christ's perfect obedience and satisfaction, credited to us. Faith alone receives this righteousness. Neither faith itself nor any work in us earns God's acceptance.

Justification is an act of God’s free grace, wherein he pardoneth all our sins, and accepteth us as righteous in his sight, only for the righteousness of Christ imputed to us, and received by faith alone.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 33

Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:

Romans 5:1 (KJV)

Justification is a complete declaration of acceptance. Sanctification is the Spirit's work of renewing us inwardly. Both belong to everyone united to Christ, but our acceptance rests wholly on Christ's righteousness. Our progress in holiness adds nothing to it (Westminster Confession 11.1-2, 13.1).

Believers cannot fall from the state of justification. Through sin they can fall under God's fatherly displeasure and lose the comfort of his countenance. They must confess their sins and renew their faith and repentance. God's continuing forgiveness does not mean sin is harmless or that repentance can be neglected (Westminster Confession 11.5).

Life with Christ

Life After You Believe

Everyone united to Christ is justified and adopted into God's family. Justification changes our standing before God. Sanctification changes us inwardly as the Spirit teaches us to hate sin and walk in obedience. These blessings belong together, but they aren't the same thing.

We obey because God has received us in Christ. Our obedience contributes nothing to the righteousness by which we're justified. It is nevertheless necessary as the fruit of living faith. Repentance continues throughout the Christian life because sin remains in us.

The same Christ who saves His people ordinarily feeds and strengthens them through His Word, the sacraments, and prayer. Read your Bible and belong to a faithful church. Receive the ministry Christ has appointed rather than measuring spiritual health by the strength of an experience.

Christians suffer, and some endure long seasons of darkness. Their suffering isn't God's condemning wrath. A Father's discipline may correct particular sins, but we shouldn't assume every illness or loss can be traced to a particular offense. Job warns against that judgment.

God preserves His people in faith. He uses warnings, correction, and the help of other believers to keep them. Assurance can weaken, but Christ doesn't become a weaker Savior when our confidence falters. Look first to His promises. Then consider the imperfect but real fruits of His Spirit without making them the foundation of your acceptance.

There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.

Romans 8:1 (KJV)

At death, believers' souls are made perfect in holiness and received into God's presence. Their bodies remain united to Christ and await the resurrection. When He returns, He will raise them in glory. They will enjoy Him forever, free from sin and suffering.

Baptism and the Lord's Supper are signs and seals of the covenant of grace. Their benefit comes from Christ through the work of his Spirit, rather than from a power in the outward elements. Receive them as Christ appointed, within the fellowship and oversight of a faithful church (Westminster Confession 27.1-3).

For practical help, read How to Find a Faithful Church. If you are fighting a persistent sin, read Struggling With Sin.

If you already belong to Christ, continue in the same faith. Confess your sins and depend on his mercy. If assurance is weak, read Assurance and Doubt. These pages can help explain the gospel, but they cannot replace Scripture or the ministry of a faithful local church.
