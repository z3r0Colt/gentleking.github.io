Depression

When You're Depressed

For weeks or years the heaviness hasn't lifted, or it keeps coming back. Sleep does not rest you, and the promises you once loved seem to belong to someone else. Christ has been in the dark before you.

On this page

  1. When Prayer Feels Beyond You
  2. Getting Help and Continuing Care
  3. Ask the Church to Help
  4. Christ Is Offered to You

About 7 minutes to read

If thoughts of ending your life come, call 988 or text 988 at any hour, tell someone near you, and read If You Are Not Safe.

In Gethsemane Jesus began to be sorrowful and very heavy, and he said so. My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death (Matthew 26:37-38 (opens in a new tab)). He didn’t hide his sorrow, and you don’t need to hide yours. Matthew also tells how he treats the weak.

A bruised reed shall he not break, and smoking flax shall he not quench,

Matthew 12:20 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

A bruised reed is a cracked stalk, and smoking flax is a wick burned down to smoke. They picture someone nearly spent, and his gentleness to such a person is real. If you belong to him, he won’t snap you or snuff you out.

In Psalm 42 (opens in a new tab) a man with tears for his food day and night speaks to his own soul (42:3).

Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God.

Psalm 42:11 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Disquieted means troubled, and countenance means face. The question comes three times (42:5, 42:11, 43:5), and each time the answer is hope in God.

Deep sadness isn’t, by itself, evidence of sin or weak faith. Elijah once lay down under a tree and asked God to take his life. The Lord sent him food and allowed him to rest before speaking further to him (1 Kings 19:4-8 (opens in a new tab)). That account doesn’t diagnose Elijah with a medical disorder, but it does show God’s care for a weary servant. If depression began after childbirth, tell your doctor. Postpartum illness needs proper attention.

Depression can affect your ability to think, pray, work, or enjoy ordinary things. Receiving professional care is consistent with trusting God, who ordinarily works through means (Westminster Confession 5.3 (opens in a new tab)). Rest matters, but serious or lasting symptoms may need medical treatment. When God Feels Far addresses spiritual darkness in greater detail.

Your darkness may feel like God’s wrath. The man who wrote Psalm 88 (opens in a new tab) said, Thy wrath lieth hard upon me (88:7), yet he kept crying to God day and night (88:1). On the cross, in the darkness over all the land, Christ bore the weight of God’s wrath for his people (Matthew 27:45 (opens in a new tab), Larger Catechism Q&A 49). If you’re his, no condemning wrath is left for you, however heavy this feels (WCF 20.1 (opens in a new tab)).

He died there, rose on the third day, and still doesn’t quench smoking flax. We don't know when this darkness will lift. Scripture gives no timetable. The man in Psalm 42 (opens in a new tab) didn’t know either, yet he said, I shall yet praise him. Many believers have known long seasons of depression. Some recover quickly. Others need years of care. A doctor’s care can help. It may lift soon, or late, or only when he comes. Either way, if you’re his, the dark won’t have the last word. When Christ comes again, he will make his people’s lowly bodies like his glorious body (Philippians 3:21 (opens in a new tab)).

The dark may tell you that you were never his. It’s a poor judge of that, because even those who fear God may walk in darkness and have no light (WCF 18.4 (opens in a new tab)). If you can’t tell, read Am I Really Saved? Come to Christ now. Come to Christ with the question still troubling you.

Tell someone you trust how much daily life has changed. You can ask them to help with a medical appointment or stay beside you while you make the call.

When Prayer Feels Beyond You

Isaiah speaks to one who fears the Lord yet walks in darkness. His words aren’t a diagnosis of depression. They do teach that God’s people may endure a season when they cannot see the way forward.

Who is among you that feareth the Lord, that obeyeth the voice of his servant, that walketh in darkness, and hath no light? let him trust in the name of the Lord, and stay upon his God.

Isaiah 50:10 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

If you can read only a few lines, read a psalm. If you cannot pray aloud, ask a member of your church to pray with you. The Spirit helps believers in their weakness (Romans 8:26 (opens in a new tab)). A difficult day of prayer doesn’t settle whether you belong to Christ.

Getting Help and Continuing Care

Tell a doctor how long symptoms have lasted, how you’re sleeping, and whether you’ve had thoughts of self-harm. If treatment isn’t helping, arrange a follow-up. Don’t change or stop prescribed medicine without medical advice.

If you cannot get to public worship because of illness, let your elders know and ask them to visit. If you might harm yourself, tell someone safe immediately and call 988 or text 988 in the United States. If danger is immediate, call 911.

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

You don’t have to settle every question about the person you lost before hearing Christ’s call to you. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.
