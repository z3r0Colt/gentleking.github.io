Is God Real?

Every argument against God is made with tools that only work if he is there.

About 5 minutes to read

There's no evidence for God

What would you count as evidence? That's worth asking before either of us begins. The Christian doesn't believe the world is silent about its Maker. Psalm 19 says the heavens declare his glory, and Romans 1:19-21 says God's power and divinity are shown in what he has made. Scripture also says we suppress that truth in unrighteousness. So the disagreement reaches further than a shortage of facts.

You can look through a telescope, examine a cell, and study the laws of nature without admitting that God made them. The same observations can be interpreted in different ways. That doesn't make observations worthless. It means our starting beliefs about what exists and what can be known matter when we discuss them.

Reason itself raises questions. We reason as though truth matters, contradictions must be avoided, and our minds can know something about a world outside us. The Christian understands these gifts as coming from a wise Creator who made man in his image. Someone who doesn't believe in God may give other accounts of logic and knowledge. We need to examine those accounts fairly rather than declaring that unbelievers can't reason. They plainly can, and Scripture says they still live in God's world.

Science also depends on an orderly world. We test an idea because we expect nature to behave with enough regularity for a test to mean something. That expectation is useful, but the method of science doesn't by itself explain why there is a world to investigate. The Lord made and upholds all things (Genesis 8:22 and Colossians 1:17).

And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.

Colossians 1:17 (KJV)

There's an important distinction. The orderly world isn't a mathematical demonstration that compels every mind to confess the Trinity. The God of Scripture has also spoken through prophets and apostles and, at last, in his Son (Hebrews 1:1-2). The resurrection of Christ is a historical claim that deserves serious attention. So I won't merely tell you to look at a tree and pretend every question has been answered.

If you say that nothing could convince you, consider whether you've ruled the answer out before examining it. If you have honest questions, ask them. Christians shouldn't fear serious examination. But the Bible doesn't promise that any argument, by itself, can give a sinner a new heart. That is the Spirit's work through the Word (1 Corinthians 2:14).

For the moral question, see Can You Be Good Without God?. For the records of Christ's person and work, read Can the Bible Be Trusted? and Did Jesus Rise From the Dead?. The message I want you to hear is larger than a debate about first causes. Jesus Christ died for sinners and rose again. The gospel calls you to repent and believe him.

Who made God?

God never began. He is eternal and has life in himself (Psalm 90:2 and John 5:26). Christians don't claim that everything has a maker, including God. We claim that the created world depends on the uncreated Lord. The question is whether the world has its source in something sufficient to account for its existence. Saying that God is eternal isn't an attempt to make an exception to a rule that all things must have a cause. It's a claim about the difference between the Creator and what he made.

Science has replaced God

Science studies how the world normally works. Why there's a world at all is another question. A man who learns how an engine runs hasn’t learned that no one built it. Robert Boyle, a major figure in experimental science, defended his Christian faith in The Christian Virtuoso in 1690. He argued that experimental inquiry could help a rightly disposed person understand and live out that faith. Studying God’s world needn’t mean denying its Maker.

Faith is believing without evidence

Not in the Bible. When Jesus blessed those who have not seen and yet believe (John 20:29), he was speaking to Thomas. Thomas had refused the word of the disciples who saw him (John 20:25). Believing good witnesses isn’t believing without evidence. Faith as the Bible means it's trust in someone who has proved trustworthy, and Luke wrote so that his reader could know for certain (Luke 1:1-4). You trust the record that Julius Caesar lived. You trust your memory, though you can only check it with your memory. What matters is whether your trust rests on something that can hold it. If we receive the witness of men, the witness of God is greater, and God has testified of his Son (1 John 5:9).

Examine the Claims

  1. Read John start to finish. It ends with Thomas, the doubter, and with why the book was written (John 20:24-31). Read it as testimony and weigh it as you would any witness.
  2. Write down your strongest reason for unbelief. Then ask what you’re assuming to make it. Take both to a pastor and ask him to answer.
  3. Visit a faithful church on Sunday. Don’t go to argue. Sit and listen to the God you doubt being addressed as if he’s there.

Under every objection

What God Calls You To Do

An objection can be answered and a man still stay away. Jesus said that men loved darkness rather than light, because their deeds were evil (John 3:19). The trouble between you and God is not finally an argument. You have sinned against him, and you know it.

Christ came for sinners exactly like you. He kept the law you have broken, and on the cross he bore its curse for everyone who trusts him, and God raised him from the dead. So the call is the one Jesus preached. Repent, and believe the gospel (Mark 1:15). You do not have to settle every question first.
