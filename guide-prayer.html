How to Pray

Bring your needs to God through Christ. Use the Lord’s Prayer as a pattern, confess sin, give thanks, and begin with words you understand.

On this page

  2. How to Pray
  3. Use the Lord’s Prayer as the Pattern

  5. Make It a Habit

About 5 minutes to read

Bring your needs to God through Christ, confess your sins, and give thanks. If you don’t know where to begin, use the Lord’s Prayer, pausing to put each request into your own words. You can pray silently or aloud. A long prayer or a particular posture isn’t required.

What Prayer Is

Prayer is an offering up of our desires unto God, for things agreeable to his will, in the name of Christ, with confession of our sins, and thankful acknowledgment of his mercies.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 98 (opens in a new tab)

Prayer includes asking for what agrees with God’s revealed will. We may ask for health, work, or relief while submitting the outcome to his wisdom. It comes through Christ, because no sinner can come to God any other way (John 14:6, Hebrews 4:14-16). And it’s honest about sin and thankful for mercy.

The Spirit helps believers in their weakness when they don’t know what to ask (Romans 8:26). Difficulty finding words does not close the way to God through Christ.

How to Pray

  1. Come in Christ’s name. To pray in Christ's name is to approach God through his sacrifice and intercession (Hebrews 10:19-22). If you’ve never come to Christ, start there. The gospel tells you how.
  2. Begin with God’s name and mercy. Say who he is and thank him for what he’s done. Jesus taught us to begin, Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name (Matthew 6:9).
  3. Confess your sins by name. Confess the sins you know, and come to the Father rather than delaying prayer through endless self-examination. He is faithful and just to forgive (1 John 1:9).
  4. Ask for what you need. Daily bread, help against a particular sin, wisdom for a decision, the salvation of someone you love. Ask plainly and specifically (Philippians 4:6).
  5. Pray for others. Your family, your church and its elders, the persecuted church, those in authority (1 Timothy 2:1-2), and your enemies (Matthew 5:44).
  6. Leave the answer with him. Ask, and then submit, as Christ did in the garden, Not my will, but thine, be done (Luke 22:42).

Use the Lord’s Prayer as the Pattern

The Shorter Catechism says the Lord’s Prayer is given as a direction for prayer (Q&A 99). Each line teaches you what to ask.

  1. Our Father which art in heaven. Come with reverence and with a child’s confidence.
  2. Hallowed be thy name. Ask that God be honored, in you and everywhere.
  3. Thy kingdom come. Ask for the gospel to go out, for sinners to be converted, and for Christ’s return.
  4. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven. Ask for willing obedience, yours first.
  5. Give us this day our daily bread. Ask for what this day needs, and leave tomorrow with him (Matthew 6:34).
  6. Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. Ask for pardon, and forgive the people who wronged you.
  7. Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Ask to be kept from sin and from the evil one.

The prayer ends by praising God’s kingdom, power, and glory (Matthew 6:13). Amen expresses agreement with the prayer. These petitions give direction without requiring you to repeat a prayer until it feels perfect. For help with repeated fears about praying correctly, see Doubts and Unwanted Thoughts.

What Displeases God in Prayer

  1. Empty repetition. Jesus warned against vain repetitions, as the heathen do (Matthew 6:7). Saying the same words isn’t the problem. Jesus warns against thinking that a great many words make God listen. Repeating a sincere request, including the Lord’s Prayer, is different (Matthew 26:44).
  2. Praying to be seen. Do not pray to impress anyone (Matthew 6:5-6).
  3. Holding on to a sin you mean to keep. If I regard iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear me (Psalm 66:18). Confess it now and ask God for help to turn from it. Don’t wait to become sinless before seeking mercy.
  4. Praying to anyone but God. Not to saints, not to angels, not to Mary. Prayer is part of worship, and it belongs to God alone, through the one mediator, Christ (1 Timothy 2:5, WCF 21.2 (opens in a new tab)).
  5. Treating God like a vending machine. Faith asks boldly and trusts him with the answer (James 1:5-7, 4:3).

Make It a Habit

  1. Pick a time and a place. Morning is one biblical example (Psalm 5:3, Mark 1:35). Choose a time that fits your responsibilities, including shift work and caring for dependents. Brief prayers during the day can accompany a regular time apart.
  2. Pray with your Bible open. Read a passage, then turn what it says into prayer.
  3. Pray with your household. Even a few minutes after supper. See How to Lead Family Worship.
  4. Pray with the church. Join the public prayers on the Lord’s Day and the prayer meeting if your church has one.
  5. Keep a short list. Note names and needs if that helps you remember them. Protect private information when someone has confided in you.

When words are difficult, read or listen to a psalm and use its requests as your own. If speaking or reading is difficult because of illness or disability, ask someone to read and pray with you. Psalm 13, Psalm 51, and Psalm 86 are good places to start. If God feels far away, read When God Feels Far.
