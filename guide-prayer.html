How to Pray

Bring your needs to God through Christ. Use the Lord’s Prayer as a pattern, confess sin, give thanks, and begin with words you understand.

About 15 minutes to read

Bring your needs to God through Christ, confess your sins, and give thanks. If you don’t know where to begin, use the Lord’s Prayer, pausing to put each request into your own words. You can pray silently or aloud. A long prayer or a particular posture isn’t required.

What Prayer Is

Prayer is an offering up of our desires unto God, for things agreeable to his will, in the name of Christ, with confession of our sins, and thankful acknowledgment of his mercies.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 98

Prayer includes asking for what agrees with God’s revealed will. We may ask for health, work, or relief while submitting the outcome to his wisdom. It comes through Christ, because no sinner can come to God any other way (John 14:6, Hebrews 4:14-16). And it’s honest about sin and thankful for mercy.

The Spirit helps believers in their weakness when they don’t know what to ask (Romans 8:26). Difficulty finding words does not close the way to God through Christ.

What It Means to Pray in Christ's Name

Sin makes us guilty before the God we approach. We can't secure a hearing by presenting our service, intensity, or the excellence of our prayer. Christ has offered the sufficient sacrifice and continues as his people's High Priest. Hebrews 4:14–16 directs believers to approach the throne of grace because they have this sympathetic and sinless Mediator.

WLC 180–181 explains praying in his name as asking in obedience to his command and drawing our confidence from his mediation. Saying his name at the end can express that dependence, but the words aren't a formula that gives an otherwise unbelieving request authority. The confidence belongs to Christ and what he has done.

This also shapes what we ask. We can't sincerely appeal to the holy Savior while deliberately requesting help to deceive, exploit, or cherish a sin. Confess that desire and ask for a changed heart. If your conscience accuses you of a real sin, come for mercy through the very Mediator who receives sinners. Don't make a clean recent record the qualification for prayer.

Prayer is a duty God requires of all people (WCF 21.3). If you haven't trusted Christ, your need isn't a reason to remain silent or postpone coming. Ask God for mercy and turn to the Savior freely offered in the gospel. Acceptance rests on him, and no other mediator is needed or able to reconcile you to God.

How the Spirit Helps a Weak Person Pray

Romans 8:26–27 acknowledges that believers don't know how to pray as they should. The Spirit helps their weakness and intercedes according to God's will. This promise belongs within a chapter about suffering, hope, adoption, and God's faithful purpose. It gives grounds for prayer when trouble has made both the outcome and your own words difficult.

WLC 182 describes his help in understanding what to ask and in awakening the faith, affection, and other graces needed for prayer. He leads us to receive God's revealed truth and approach through Christ. His work doesn't make a passing impression an infallible direction or provide permission to ask against Scripture.

Use the help he has given. Read a petition in Scripture, ask someone in the church to pray with you, and bring the particular need as plainly as you can. A believer who struggles to speak may still approach God through Christ. The Spirit's faithful intercession isn't measured by the ease with which you can produce sentences.

How to Pray

  1. Come in Christ’s name. To pray in Christ's name is to approach God through his sacrifice and intercession (Hebrews 10:19-22). If you’ve never come to Christ, start there. The gospel tells you how.
  2. Begin with God’s name and mercy. Say who he is and thank him for what he’s done. Jesus taught us to begin, Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name (Matthew 6:9).
  3. Confess your sins by name. Confess the sins you know, and come to the Father rather than delaying prayer through endless self-examination. He is faithful and just to forgive (1 John 1:9).
  4. Ask for what you need. Daily bread, help against a particular sin, wisdom for a decision, the salvation of someone you love. Ask plainly and specifically (Philippians 4:6).
  5. Pray for others. Your family, your church and its elders, the persecuted church, those in authority (1 Timothy 2:1-2), and your enemies (Matthew 5:44).
  6. Leave the answer with him. Ask, and then submit, as Christ did in the garden, Not my will, but thine, be done (Luke 22:42).

Use the Lord’s Prayer as the Pattern

The Shorter Catechism says the Lord’s Prayer is given as a direction for prayer (Q&A 99). Each line teaches you what to ask.

  1. Our Father which art in heaven. Come with reverence and with a child’s confidence.
  2. Hallowed be thy name. Ask that God be honored, in you and everywhere.
  3. Thy kingdom come. Ask for the gospel to go out, for sinners to be converted, and for Christ’s return.
  4. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven. Ask for willing obedience, yours first.
  5. Give us this day our daily bread. Ask for what this day needs, and leave tomorrow with him (Matthew 6:34).
  6. Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. Ask for pardon, and forgive the people who wronged you.
  7. Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Ask to be kept from sin and from the evil one.

The prayer ends by praising God’s kingdom, power, and glory (Matthew 6:13). Amen expresses agreement with the prayer. These petitions give direction without requiring you to repeat a prayer until it feels perfect. For help with repeated fears about praying correctly, see Doubts and Unwanted Thoughts.

Pray as Children before the Heavenly Father

The opening of the Lord's Prayer joins confidence with reverence. Through Christ, believers approach God as their Father, with a real interest in his goodness. He is also in heaven, sovereign over every creature and worthy of holy fear. WLC 189 keeps both together. Familiarity that forgets his majesty is wrong, as is fear that refuses the access his Son has opened.

The prayer also teaches us to say our and us. Bring the needs of other people before him alongside your own. A child of God belongs to a family whose burdens and blessings matter. You can pray alone without praying as though you were the only person God cares for.

WLC 187 permits using the Lord's Prayer itself as well as following its pattern. Read it with understanding and supply particular needs as you go. Its order helps enlarge a prayer that has become absorbed entirely in one private concern.

Ask That God's Name Be Honored

God's name is already holy. In the first petition, we ask that he enable us and others to know, esteem, and honor him as he deserves. WLC 190 includes his Word, ordinances, attributes, and works in the scope of this request. We need grace even to respond rightly to what he has revealed.

Pray that unbelief and idolatry would be overcome, that the church would teach truthfully, and that your own speech and conduct would honor him. If you're defending an untruth because it protects your reputation, this petition calls your priorities into question. God's honor must govern the way you seek an answer.

You can also ask him to glorify himself through a situation whose purpose you don't understand. That isn't a claim that the evil involved is good. It expresses confidence that his holy government isn't defeated by it. Ask for the removal of evil and for his name to be vindicated.

Ask for the Coming of Christ's Kingdom

The second petition asks for the overthrow of sin and Satan's dominion and the advance of Christ's gracious reign. WLC 191 includes the spread of the gospel, the calling of the Jews, the fullness of the Gentiles, and the church's provision with faithful officers and ordinances. Pray for the conversion of actual people and for the means Christ uses to gather them.

Ask that preaching be faithful and effectual, that error and corruption be removed, and that believers be strengthened in holiness and comfort. Pray that civil rulers fulfill their duty to protect and support Christ's church. This is a larger request than the success of your own congregation's plans.

The petition reaches its completion in Christ's return and his people's eternal reign with him. Pray for that promised end while serving him in the present. Asking for the kingdom should make you more willing to receive his rule over a particular cherished sin, speak his gospel, and support the work of the church.

Ask for Willing Obedience to God's Will

God's revealed will tells us what to believe and do. His providence orders what happens. In the third petition, we ask for grace to know and obey his commands and to submit to his wise government. WLC 192 names both our natural resistance and the Spirit's work in making us willing and able to obey.

The angels' obedience supplies the comparison. Ask to serve with humility, sincerity, diligence, and constancy. If you know that you owe an apology, don't pray only that God would make the conversation unnecessary. Ask for the willingness to do the duty and then do it.

Submission to providence doesn't forbid seeking relief, protection, or a lawful improvement. Christ prayed earnestly in Gethsemane and submitted to his Father's will. You can ask for a burden to be removed while trusting that God remains righteous and wise if it continues. This request places your plans under his authority without making known evil acceptable.

Ask for Daily Bread with Dependence and Contentment

Daily bread includes the outward provision needed for life. Food, shelter, work, and other ordinary necessities are proper subjects of prayer. WLC 193 teaches us to seek a suitable portion of God's gifts, their continuance and blessing, and a holy and contented use of them. Work and planning remain necessary, but neither puts God in our debt.

The plural us includes people who lack what you have. Pray for a struggling family and consider what help you can responsibly give. Ask God to provide lawful employment while pursuing suitable opportunities. Dependence on providence works through the lawful means he supplies.

This petition also corrects unlawful acquisition and restless accumulation. You can't honestly ask God to bless goods you're deliberately obtaining by fraud. Nor should you treat another person's abundance as proof that your Father has neglected you. Bring actual need without pretending that every desired possession is necessary bread.

Ask for Pardon with a Forgiving Heart

Our debts are sins for which we owe an answer to God's justice. We can't discharge that debt by remorse, future improvement, or forgiving someone else. WLC 194 grounds pardon in Christ's obedience and satisfaction, received through faith. Ask for daily forgiveness and the renewed assurance of your Father's favor on that foundation.

The words about forgiving our debtors also search our hearts. Matthew 6:14–15 makes a deliberate refusal of mercy a serious warning. Someone who cherishes revenge contradicts the mercy he professes to receive. Repent of that refusal and seek the forgiving spirit God commands.

Your forgiveness doesn't purchase God's forgiveness. It is an evidence of his grace and an encouragement to ask as one who has begun to show mercy. The forgiveness guide explains the duties involved, including receiving the repentant, seeking reconciliation, and distinguishing restored fellowship from particular responsibilities of trust.

Ask to Be Kept from Temptation and Delivered from Evil

God never entices anyone to sin (James 1:13–15). He may nevertheless order trials in which our weakness and the assaults of Satan are real. The final petition asks him to preserve us from being overcome, restrain evil, and supply the grace needed to stand. WLC 195 also includes recovery after a fall and final deliverance from sin and all evil.

Pray with an honest knowledge of your weakness. Ask God to close a dangerous opportunity and make you watchful in the means of grace. Don't request preservation while deliberately arranging the next occasion for a cherished sin. When you have fallen, seek pardon and recovery rather than treating the petition as something only the successful may use.

The prayer closes by ascribing the kingdom, power, and glory to God. WLC 196 teaches us to draw our arguments from his sufficiency rather than our worthiness. Amen expresses the desire and confidence with which we entrust our requests to him. We depend on the God who can accomplish what we can't.

What Displeases God in Prayer

  1. Empty repetition. Jesus warned against vain repetitions, as the heathen do (Matthew 6:7). Saying the same words isn’t the problem. Jesus warns against thinking that a great many words make God listen. Repeating a sincere request, including the Lord’s Prayer, is different (Matthew 26:44).
  2. Praying to be seen. Do not pray to impress anyone (Matthew 6:5-6).
  3. Holding on to a sin you mean to keep. If I regard iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear me (Psalm 66:18). Confess it now and ask God for help to turn from it. Don’t wait to become sinless before seeking mercy.
  4. Praying to anyone but God. Not to saints, not to angels, not to Mary. Prayer is part of worship, and it belongs to God alone, through the one mediator, Christ (1 Timothy 2:5, WCF 21.2).
  5. Treating God like a vending machine. Faith asks boldly and trusts him with the answer (James 1:5-7, 4:3).

Pray Through a Psalm

Psalm 13 gives words for a believer who feels forgotten. David begins with questions about God's apparent absence, asks God to consider and hear him, and ends by trusting his mercy. Read the whole psalm before turning it into your prayer. Its movement gives room for sorrow without making sorrow the final judge of God's faithfulness.

You might tell the Father what has made the day difficult, ask for help to carry out a particular duty, and thank him for his salvation in Christ. The psalm doesn't require you to manufacture David's circumstances or promise that your distress will end tonight. You can speak truthfully about your own circumstances while learning to ask and trust as Scripture teaches.

Other passages will lead you to different requests. A command to speak truth should move you to confess a lie and ask for honest speech. A promise of Christ's keeping should move you to ask for persevering faith. A description of God's holiness should lead to reverence and praise. Let the meaning of the passage govern the prayer instead of attaching the same request to everything you read.

When the Answer Waits

God commands perseverance in prayer (Luke 18:1-8). Continuing to ask can express faith in his care. It doesn't earn a hearing through exhaustion or force him to change his counsel. Christ himself prayed repeatedly in Gethsemane while submitting to his Father's will. Faithful prayer includes both an earnest request and that submission.

James warns against asking for things to spend on our lusts (James 4:1-3). Examine your motives honestly. Ask whether your request would require disobedience, neglect someone entrusted to you, or treat another person as a possession. Where the request is sinful, repent of it. A delayed answer, however, doesn't prove that a secret sin explains the delay.

Paul asked repeatedly for his thorn to be removed. Christ answered with sufficient grace rather than the removal Paul requested (2 Corinthians 12:7-10). This doesn't forbid seeking treatment or relief. It teaches that Christ's faithful care can sustain his people while an affliction remains. You may keep asking for healing and receive medical care without claiming that either outcome measures how much God loves you.

Keep doing the work that belongs with your prayer. Pray for reconciliation while correcting the lie you told. Pray for employment while seeking suitable work. Pray for a loved one's conversion while speaking the gospel patiently. Using lawful means doesn't make prayer unnecessary, and prayer doesn't release you from the duties God has already made clear.

If a request has occupied your mind for months, ask a trusted elder to pray with you. Explain the request and the weight it has become. You don't have to hide disappointment behind cheerful language. Nor must you decide God's undisclosed purpose before coming to him again through Christ.

Make It a Habit

  1. Pick a time and a place. Morning is one biblical example (Psalm 5:3, Mark 1:35). Choose a time that fits your responsibilities, including shift work and caring for dependents. Brief prayers during the day can accompany a regular time apart.
  2. Pray with your Bible open. Read a passage, then turn what it says into prayer.
  3. Pray with your household. Even a few minutes after supper. See How to Lead Family Worship.
  4. Pray with the church. Join the public prayers on the Lord’s Day and the prayer meeting if your church has one.
  5. Keep a short list. Note names and needs if that helps you remember them. Protect private information when someone has confided in you.

When words are difficult, read or listen to a psalm and use its requests as your own. If speaking or reading is difficult because of illness or disability, ask someone to read and pray with you. Psalm 13, Psalm 51, and Psalm 86 are good places to start. If God feels far away, read When God Feels Far.
