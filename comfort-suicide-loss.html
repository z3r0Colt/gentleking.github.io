Loss by suicide

When Someone You Love Took Their Own Life

A death like this leaves questions no other death leaves. You may be carrying grief and guilt and fear all at once. God saw what you couldn't see, and he is near you now.

On this page

  1. The Questions That Won't Leave
  2. After the Funeral
  3. Ask the Church to Help
  4. Christ Is Offered to You

About 7 minutes to read

If the thought of following them has come to you, you're not strange and you're not alone. Call 988 or text 988 now. It answers at any hour. Outside the United States, call your local emergency number.

If you’re thinking of taking your own life, please turn to the immediate safety guidance and tell someone close to you now. Christ’s promise to keep his sheep is never permission to harm yourself. This page is written for those mourning a death.

Yea, the darkness hideth not from thee; but the night shineth as the day: the darkness and the light are both alike to thee.

Psalm 139:12 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

You couldn’t see all that was in their heart. He could.

When Lazarus died, his sisters met Jesus one after the other with the same cry, Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died (John 11:21 (opens in a new tab), 32). It’s the if-only cry, and he answered it with tears, not a rebuke (11:33, 35). You may weep for the one you lost, without shame. Bring him every what if. Bring the sign you think you missed, the message you didn’t answer, the last words between you. He forgives the sins of all who come to him, and he lifts the guilt that was never yours to carry.

Taking one’s own life is a grave sin against the sixth commandment (Exodus 20:13 (opens in a new tab), Larger Catechism Q&A 136). That’s true, and so is this. For those who are Christ’s, salvation never rested on their last hour. It rests on what he finished on the cross. Those God has accepted in Christ can neither totally nor finally fall away from the state of grace (Westminster Confession 17.1 (opens in a new tab)).

For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Romans 8:38-39 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

If the one you lost belonged to Christ, even this death couldn’t separate them from his love. Christ died for sinners, and on the third day he rose. He said of his sheep that no man can pluck them out of his hand (John 10:28-29 (opens in a new tab)). Christ's promise to keep his sheep is sure. The risen Lord who makes that promise also showed his wounded hands to Thomas. After he rose, he showed Thomas the print of the nails in them (John 20:25-27 (opens in a new tab)). His grip doesn’t fail.

You may have heard that taking one’s own life is the unforgivable sin. It isn’t. Jesus named one sin that's never forgiven, blasphemy against the Holy Ghost. He spoke of it because men said he had an unclean spirit (Mark 3:28-30 (opens in a new tab)).

The devil once misused a promise of God to tempt Jesus into danger, and Jesus refused to put God to the test (Matthew 4:6-7 (opens in a new tab)). Scripture never treats God’s preserving grace as permission to take one’s life.

If you fear the one you lost didn’t know Christ, that grief is real. You aren’t asked to settle where they are, and your fear doesn’t get the last word. Leave them with the Judge of all the earth, who will do right (Genesis 18:25 (opens in a new tab)), and bring your own soul to Christ, who turns away no one who comes. When You Fear They Did Not Know Christ says more.

Ask a trustworthy person to be with you through the first hard night. You don’t have to answer everyone’s questions. If shock or memories of what happened make daily life hard, speak with a doctor or counselor as well as your pastor.

The Questions That Won't Leave

Those left after a suicide often search for the message they missed. You knew only what you could see. You didn’t make another person’s final choice. You can grieve someone and still recognize that taking one’s life is a grave sin. Scripture doesn’t authorize us to decide a particular person’s eternal state from that act alone.

If you truly wronged the one you lost, confess your sin to God. Don’t assume responsibility for what you could not control. Tell a trusted person if shock or distressing memories keep returning. Medical care or grief counseling may be needed. If thoughts of ending your own life come, get someone with you and call 988 or text 988.

After the Funeral

You may have questions for investigators or family that you cannot answer. It’s right to say you don’t know. Ask someone to remain close when the house goes quiet. Medical help is appropriate for ongoing shock or sleeplessness.

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

You don’t have to settle every question about the person you lost before hearing Christ’s call to you. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.
