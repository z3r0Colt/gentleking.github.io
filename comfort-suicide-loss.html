Loss by suicide

When Someone You Love Took Their Own Life

For those grieving a death by suicide. Find support for the first days, questions about guilt, and what Scripture says about Christ keeping his people.

On this page

  1. Christ Does Not Turn Away From Grief
  2. When You Keep Asking Why
  3. Does Suicide Mean They Were Lost?
  4. When Others Ask About the Death
  5. Finding Help After Suicide Loss

About 9 minutes to read

Someone you love has died by suicide. You may be in shock, or you may have been living with this loss for years. The way they died can become all anyone talks about. But you knew a whole person. Their life mattered to you long before its last day, and you can mourn them by name.

If this loss has left you afraid that you might hurt yourself, go straight to help for staying safe. You don't have to face that danger alone.

People sometimes offer explanations you didn't ask for, or say nothing because they don't know what to say. You may want to talk about the person you lost rather than the manner of death. Tell a trusted friend what you need. It's right to ask them simply to listen.

You may miss them and be angry with them at the same time. Perhaps you feel abandoned. Perhaps you feel numb, or ashamed to speak about what happened. You don't have to settle those feelings before you ask someone to sit with you. Grief after suicide can be painful in ways that are difficult to explain even to people who love you.

Christ Does Not Turn Away From Grief

When Lazarus died, Mary and Martha each told Jesus that their brother would have lived if he had been there (John 11:21 (opens in a new tab), 32). Jesus knew he was about to raise Lazarus, yet he wept at the tomb (John 11:35 (opens in a new tab)). He didn't treat their tears as unbelief. Scripture gives us a Savior who knows human sorrow and receives those who come to him in distress (Hebrews 4:15-16 (opens in a new tab)).

Christ doesn't promise to answer every question about this death. He does invite sinners to come to him, and he gives his people a church whose members are commanded to bear one another's burdens (Galatians 6:2 (opens in a new tab)). Ask your pastor or an elder to hear what has happened. If you can't bring yourself to speak much, ask him to come and pray with you.

When You Keep Asking Why

You may go over the last conversation, a message you didn't answer, or something that now looks like a warning. You might ask whether one different choice could have kept them alive. Those questions can return long after the funeral.

Yea, the darkness hideth not from thee; but the night shineth as the day: the darkness and the light are both alike to thee.

Psalm 139:12 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Psalm 139 (opens in a new tab) says that even darkness is not hidden from God. He knew what you couldn't see. You may never know everything your loved one was facing or what happened in their final hours. You are responsible for your own actions, but you couldn't know every thought in another person's mind or control every decision they made.

If you know you wronged them, confess that sin to the Lord. Where there is someone you ought to make things right with, do so as you can. But don't turn an unanswered question into a verdict that the whole death rests on you. Talk about those memories with someone who will hear them patiently, especially if you found the person or witnessed what happened.

Does Suicide Mean They Were Lost?

Some people speak as though suicide is the one sin Christ cannot forgive. Scripture doesn't teach that. Taking one's life is a grave wrong against God's command to preserve human life (Exodus 20:13 (opens in a new tab) and Westminster Larger Catechism Q&A 135-136 (opens in a new tab)). But Jesus did not call suicide the unforgivable sin. He spoke of blasphemy against the Holy Ghost (Mark 3:28-30 (opens in a new tab)).

Salvation rests on Christ, not on whether a Christian managed to confess every sin before death. The original Westminster Confession teaches that those whom God has effectually called cannot totally or finally fall away from grace (17.1). That promise is grounded in the saving work and keeping power of Christ.

For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Romans 8:38-39 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

If the one you lost truly belonged to Christ, their death did not separate them from his love. Their last act cannot undo his finished work (John 10:28-29 (opens in a new tab)). At the same time, we cannot decide that a particular person was saved or lost solely from how they died. The Lord knows their heart. If you don't know where they stood with God, you can leave that judgment with him (Genesis 18:25 (opens in a new tab)). When You Fear They Did Not Know Christ speaks to that question more fully.

You don't have to solve that question in order to grieve them. You can remember what was good in their life without excusing how it ended.

When Others Ask About the Death

You can say you don't know why it happened, or that you don't want to discuss the details. You owe no one a full account of another person's final hours. Ask someone you trust to handle difficult calls or visitors if you need distance from those questions.

Children need a truthful explanation suited to their age, without graphic details or blame. Let them ask questions, and don't make them responsible for comforting the adults. A pastor or grief counselor can help you find words if you are unsure what to say.

Finding Help After Suicide Loss

In the first days, accept practical help with food, arrangements, or the calls you can't bear to make. Later you may need company when the house is quiet or on an anniversary others have forgotten. Tell a friend or elder what kind of help would matter most. It's fine to speak about your loved one's ordinary life, not only their death.

If you are having nightmares, distressing memories, or trouble managing daily life, ask a doctor or qualified counselor about support for grief and trauma. The American Foundation for Suicide Prevention lists suicide-loss support groups (opens in a new tab), and its Healing Conversations program (opens in a new tab) can connect you with someone who has also lost a loved one to suicide. Those services aren't a replacement for a faithful local church, but people who have lived through a similar loss may understand questions you have found hard to say aloud.

If you begin thinking of ending your own life, tell someone safe near you now. In the United States, call 988 or text 988 for crisis support. If you are in immediate danger, call 911 or go to an emergency department. You can ask for pastoral care as well, but don't delay urgent help.

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

You can hear Christ’s call while questions about your suffering remain unanswered. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.
