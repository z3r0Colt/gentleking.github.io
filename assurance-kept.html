Perseverance

Will God Keep Me to the End?

Underneath all these fears is the same suspicion, that in the end it all hangs on you. Paul closes Romans 8 by naming everything that could come between a believer and God.

About 3 minutes to read

For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Romans 8:38-39 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Paul doesn’t say you're kept by your love for God. He says nothing can separate you from God’s love for you, and that love is in Christ Jesus our Lord, where no fear of yours can reach it. When Zion said the Lord had forgotten her, God answered through Isaiah.

Can a woman forget her sucking child, that she should not have compassion on the son of her womb? yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee. Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands; thy walls are continually before me.

Isaiah 49:15-16 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Moses, blessing Israel before his death, told them that the eternal God was their refuge and that underneath were the everlasting arms (Deuteronomy 33:27 (opens in a new tab)). On the nights you can’t hold on to anything, that’s still where you are.

God preserves his people by sustaining their faith and repentance through the means he has appointed (WCF 17.1-2 (opens in a new tab) and 14.1). A believer may stumble badly, but the Lord won't leave him finally hardened and lost. His promises give strength to obey the commands to persevere.

Do these things while you wait for the fear to lift.

  1. Seek counsel about the Lord’s Table. Question 172 of the Larger Catechism says the Supper was appointed for the relief even of weak and doubting Christians. One who doubts should mourn his unbelief and labor to have his doubts resolved, and as he does, he may and ought to come, so that his faith is made stronger. Take your doubts to your elders. The counsel of question 172 is for those who mourn their unbelief and sincerely seek Christ, not those persisting without repentance. Self-examination and the church's oversight still matter (WLC 171-173 (opens in a new tab)).
  2. Keep sitting under the preached Word. The Word that first worked your faith is how God goes on strengthening it (WCF 14.1 (opens in a new tab)). This page can’t feed you week by week. A faithful pulpit can. If you don’t yet belong to a church with a pulpit like that, find one and join it. Go even when you feel nothing.
  3. Pray Mark 9:24 (opens in a new tab) honestly. Tell him you believe and ask him to help your unbelief. Ask again tomorrow.
  4. Learn a few places in Scripture you can find in the dark. Romans 8 (opens in a new tab) answers the accuser. Psalm 103 (opens in a new tab) speaks to shame, and John 10 (opens in a new tab) to the fear that you will slip from his hand.

Isaiah 40 (opens in a new tab) opens with God commanding comfort for his people. A few verses on, Isaiah sees the Lord God come with a strong hand, his arm ruling for him. The next thing that arm does is gather lambs.

He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather the lambs with his arm, and carry them in his bosom, and shall gently lead those that are with young.

Isaiah 40:11 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)
