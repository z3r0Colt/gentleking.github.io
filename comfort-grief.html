Grief

When Someone You Love Has Died

The house is quiet, or it's full of people, and either way the one you want isn't in it. You don't have to be strong tonight. Christ has stood where you are standing.

In danger, or thinking of ending your life? Call 911, or call 988 or text 988, now. If someone at home is hurting you, read If You Are Not Safe. Outside the United States, call your local emergency number.

About 7 minutes to read

When Jesus therefore saw her weeping, and the Jews also weeping which came with her, he groaned in the spirit, and was troubled, And said, Where have ye laid him? They said unto him, Lord, come and see. Jesus wept.

John 11:33-35 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Jesus knew he was about to raise Lazarus, yet he wept with those who mourned. His tears show that sorrow at death is no failure of faith. You may grieve the person you’ve lost while trusting Christ’s promise of resurrection.

If they took their own life, that grief has its own place here. If you fear they didn’t know Christ, there’s a word for you below.

Paul doesn’t tell Christians not to grieve. He tells them not to grieve as those who have no hope.

But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.

1 Thessalonians 4:13-14 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Scripture calls the death of a believer sleep. That’s a picture, and Jesus explained it himself. When the disciples thought Lazarus was only resting, he told them plainly that Lazarus was dead (John 11:11-14 (opens in a new tab)). The picture belongs to the body, which lies down for a while and will wake. The soul neither dies nor sleeps (Westminster Confession 32.1 (opens in a new tab)).

The souls of believers are at their death made perfect in holiness, and do immediately pass into glory; and their bodies, being still united to Christ, do rest in their graves till the resurrection.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 37 (opens in a new tab)

If the one you love belonged to Christ, they’re with him now, free of every sin they ever fought. Paul calls being with Christ far better (Philippians 1:23 (opens in a new tab)). In the next breath he says that to stay is more needful, because others still need him (1:24). Others still need you too, and God hasn’t finished with your life. If you find yourself wanting to follow them, call 988 or text 988 tonight, and tell someone near you.

And if they're his, the grave won’t keep them. The one who wept at the tomb of Lazarus went into a tomb of his own. On the third day he left it empty.

But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.

1 Corinthians 15:20 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

The firstfruits were the first sheaves of the harvest. They were brought to God as a pledge that the rest of the field was coming in. Christ’s empty tomb is that pledge. On the last day God will wipe away all tears from the eyes of his people, and there will be no more death (Isaiah 25:8 (opens in a new tab), Revelation 21:3-4 (opens in a new tab)). The Lord himself will put an end to their mourning, and death will never return.

If you fear they didn’t know Christ, that grief is real, and Scripture makes room for it. Jesus wept over a city that would not come to him (Luke 19:41 (opens in a new tab)). You aren’t asked to settle where they are. Leave them with the Judge of all the earth, who will do right (Genesis 18:25 (opens in a new tab)), and bring your own soul to Christ. When You Fear They Did Not Know Christ says more.

In the first days you may need someone to answer calls, help with arrangements, or simply sit with you. If children are grieving, let them ask questions and tell the truth gently.

When a Date Brings It Back

Birthdays, holidays, and the anniversary of a death can reopen sorrow after months of relative peace. There’s no required date by which a Christian must stop weeping. It may help to speak with someone who knew the person, or to set aside time to remember them.

Grief can leave you impatient or angry with people around you. Where you sin, confess it. But don’t treat the return of sadness as wrongdoing. Paul’s teaching on sorrow with hope is grounded in Christ’s resurrection, not in how quickly a person recovers (1 Thessalonians 4:13-14 (opens in a new tab)).

After the Visitors Leave

Church members may not know how hard the evenings have become. Tell a pastor or friend when you need a visit. Read a psalm with someone if you can’t pray much alone. If sadness or sleeplessness makes ordinary life unmanageable, speak with a doctor.

You may want to keep photographs and stories close, or put them away for a season. Neither choice measures your faith. Remembering someone who has died can remain part of your life while you continue to serve the people God has placed beside you.

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

You don’t have to settle every question about the person you lost before hearing Christ’s call to you. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.
