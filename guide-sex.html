What the Bible Says About Sex

Sexual faithfulness, mutual marital duty and tenderness, and pardon for those who repent. If you have been assaulted or coerced, seek safety and medical care first.

About 17 minutes to read

Scripture teaches sexual faithfulness within marriage and chastity outside it. For married readers, mutual marital duty and tenderness explain what husband and wife owe one another. Begin with urgent care if you have been harmed. If someone forced you, or did something to you when you were a child, read the part for you. If you aren’t safe now, go to If You Are Not Safe. If sexual sin weighs on your conscience, read the part for you. If shame has you thinking of ending your life, call 988 or text 988 in the United States. For immediate danger, contact your local emergency service.

God’s Good Gift

Before there was any sin, God made man male and female and blessed them, saying, Be fruitful, and multiply (Genesis 1:28). God created sexual union for marriage, and his creation was very good (Genesis 1:31). Sin has corrupted our use of that gift.

Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh. And they were both naked, the man and his wife, and were not ashamed.

Genesis 2:24-25 (KJV)

A man leaves his parents and cleaves to his wife, which means he holds fast to her. The two become one flesh. Two whole lives are joined, and the bodily union of husband and wife seals it. Jesus added, What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder (Matthew 19:6). So the gift belongs inside marriage, one man and one woman for life (WCF 24.1 (opens in a new tab)). Sexual activity outside that covenant violates the order God established.

Let thy fountain be blessed: and rejoice with the wife of thy youth.

Proverbs 5:18 (KJV)

A father tells his son to drink waters out of his own well (5:15). The well and the fountain are pictures of married love, and the delight they picture is real. God tells a husband to be ravished always with his wife’s love (5:19). In the old English that means carried away with joy.

Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled: but whoremongers and adulterers God will judge.

Hebrews 13:4 (KJV)

Whoremongers are those who use sex outside marriage. In one verse God honors the gift and guards it.

The Song of Solomon

In the Song of Solomon a bride and her beloved delight in each other. My beloved is mine, and I am his (Song of Solomon 2:16). Read plainly, it celebrates married love. It shows that God isn’t embarrassed by the love he made.

Many Reformed teachers, James Durham among them, have also read the Song as a picture of Christ and his church. Scripture elsewhere calls God’s people his bride (Isaiah 62:5). Others read it first as a song of husband and wife that points beyond itself to Christ. Faithful men differ on which reading leads, and neither reading takes Christ out of it.

The Song speaks to the young as well. Three times the bride says this.

I charge you, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes, and by the hinds of the field, that ye stir not up, nor awake my love, till he please.

Song of Solomon 2:7 (KJV)

Many read this to mean, don’t wake desire before its time. There's a right time, and it’s worth waiting for. For more on marriage itself, read What the Bible Says About Marriage.

Mutual Duty and Tenderness in the Marriage Bed

Let the husband render unto the wife due benevolence: and likewise also the wife unto the husband. The wife hath not power of her own body, but the husband: and likewise also the husband hath not power of his own body, but the wife.

1 Corinthians 7:3-4 (KJV)

Defraud ye not one the other, except it be with consent for a time, that ye may give yourselves to fasting and prayer; and come together again, that Satan tempt you not for your incontinency.

1 Corinthians 7:5 (KJV)

The Duty to Give, Not to Withhold

Marital intimacy is a real duty for husband and wife. Paul tells each spouse to render the other what is due, and then commands both not to defraud one another. The wife owes her husband loving faithfulness in the marriage bed, and the husband owes his wife the same. Scripture doesn't treat this duty as something either spouse may ignore whenever desire is absent.

In verse 5 Paul names a period of abstinence agreed upon by both spouses for prayer and fasting, and then tells them to come together again. His point is that withholding intimacy is not to become the normal, unilateral pattern of marriage. Refusing it to punish a spouse, gain control, or simply disregard a spouse's continuing need is contrary to the mutual obligation he teaches. The seventh commandment requires conjugal love and cohabitation, and forbids neglect of those duties (Westminster Larger Catechism Q&A 138-139 (opens in a new tab)).

This command addresses the spouse who doesn't feel like it, as well as the spouse who longs for intimacy. A lack of spontaneous desire isn't a complete answer to a long-standing pattern of refusal. If one spouse regularly turns the other away, the question deserves attention rather than being dismissed. Neither husband nor wife should expect the other to live indefinitely without a sincere effort to address the problem.

When Intimacy Is Difficult

Paul isn't giving a complete list of medical and personal circumstances that can make intimacy difficult or impossible. Pain, illness, recovery from childbirth, disability, trauma, and severe exhaustion require real care. A refusal on one occasion does not by itself prove sin, and no spouse is required to submit to force or threats. The duty to give oneself lovingly is never permission to take by force. Husband and wife must consider one another with patience and honor (Ephesians 5:28-29, 1 Peter 3:7).

But patience isn't the same as pretending a continuing problem doesn't exist. If the cause is pain, seek medical advice. If fear or past harm is involved, seek qualified care without pressure to resume sex before it is safe. If time and energy are continually spent elsewhere while the marriage is neglected, ask together whether priorities need to change. Having strength for other activities doesn't by itself prove that intimacy is possible, but the concern shouldn't be brushed aside either.

When You Feel Repeatedly Rejected

Speak plainly rather than keeping a tally or beginning an argument after another refusal. You might say, "I miss being close to you. We've hardly been intimate, and it hurts. I know we both have responsibilities, but I need us to take this seriously and work on it together." Hear what your spouse says. Ask whether there are health concerns, unresolved conflict, resentment, fear, or changes in desire. Then agree on a realistic next step instead of making a promise neither person intends to keep.

A spouse who feels unwanted should not be mocked or called selfish merely for wanting marital intimacy. The spouse who struggles with desire should not be shamed or treated as a body to be used. Both are accountable before God to seek the other's good. Where there has been sinful neglect, confess it and seek change. Where there is illness or another genuine obstacle, bear the burden together and seek appropriate help. If the conversation keeps failing, ask for wise pastoral counsel or professional assistance suited to the problem.

Marriage includes affection outside sex. Ordinary tenderness and friendship matter, and they should not become demands with an expected payment. For the wider biblical responsibilities of married life, see Affection, Sex, and Different Needs. If anyone is using violence, threats, or sexual coercion, seek independent safety help through If Someone Is Hurting You.

What the Seventh Commandment Requires and Forbids

God said, Thou shalt not commit adultery (Exodus 20:14). Jesus showed how far that reaches.

But I say unto you, That whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her hath committed adultery with her already in his heart.

Matthew 5:28 (KJV)

Seeing that someone is beautiful isn’t lust. Looking to cultivate sexual desire for someone who isn’t your spouse, and returning to feed that desire, is. The Larger Catechism first says what the command requires.

The duties required in the seventh commandment are, chastity in body, mind, affections, words, and behavior; and the preservation of it in ourselves and others; watchfulness over the eyes and all the senses; temperance, keeping of chaste company, modesty in apparel; marriage by those that have not the gift of continency, conjugal love, and cohabitation; diligent labor in our callings; shunning all occasions of uncleanness, and resisting temptations thereunto.

Westminster Larger Catechism Q&A 138 (opens in a new tab)

Chastity is purity, in the single life and in marriage alike. The gift of continency is the gift of living single without burning desire (1 Corinthians 7:7-9). Conjugal love and cohabitation mean that husband and wife love each other and live together. Here is part of what it forbids.

adultery, fornication, rape, incest, sodomy, and all unnatural lusts; all unclean imaginations, thoughts, purposes, and affections; all corrupt or filthy communications, or listening thereunto; wanton looks, impudent or light behavior, immodest apparel

Westminster Larger Catechism Q&A 139 (opens in a new tab)

Fornication is sex between people who aren’t married to each other. Adultery is sex with someone other than your husband or wife. The catechism goes on to forbid lascivious songs, books, and pictures (WLC 139 (opens in a new tab)). Lascivious means made to stir up lust. That's what pornography is. It feeds lust in the heart and uses real people made in God’s image as things. On the catechism’s words about unnatural lusts, read What the Bible Says About Homosexuality and Gender.

Why the Body Matters

Some in Corinth thought what they did with the body didn’t touch the soul. Paul answered that the body is not for fornication, but for the Lord, and God will raise it up (1 Corinthians 6:13-14). Flee fornication, he said (6:18).

What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God’s.

1 Corinthians 6:19-20 (KJV)

Sexual sin joins your whole self to another in a way God made for marriage alone (6:16). Paul calls the believer’s body a temple because the Holy Spirit lives within him. Christ purchased that body with his blood.

Paul also wrote, this is the will of God, even your sanctification, that ye should abstain from fornication (1 Thessalonians 4:3). Each is to possess his vessel in sanctification and honour (4:4). Many take the vessel to be one’s own body, others a man’s wife. Either way the call is self-control with honor. And no man is to defraud his brother in this (4:6). Sexual sin always wrongs someone, often a future husband or wife who isn’t even in the room.

When It Was Done to You

If someone forced you, or pressured you past your no, or touched you when you were a child, what was done to you isn’t your sin. You don’t need to repent of it, and you aren’t unclean in God’s eyes because of it.

Moses’ law said that when a man forced a betrothed young woman, the man only that lay with her shall die (Deuteronomy 22:25).

But unto the damsel thou shalt do nothing; there is in the damsel no sin worthy of death: for as when a man riseth against his neighbour, and slayeth him, even so is this matter:

Deuteronomy 22:26 (KJV)

God put all the guilt on the man. When a son of King David forced his sister Tamar, she cried, whither shall I cause my shame to go? (2 Samuel 13:13). Scripture records her grief and calls his act what it was. It never calls her the guilty one.

The Lord is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart (Psalm 34:18). Christ himself was stripped and shamed by men who had power over him (Matthew 27:28). He knows that wound.

If the assault was recent, seek prompt medical care even if you are unsure about reporting. A clinician or sexual-assault advocate can explain care and reporting options. If it’s still happening, get safe first with If You Are Not Safe. If you’re young, tell a safe parent, teacher, school counselor, or another trusted adult. If the person hurting you is a parent, choose someone independent of them. If someone is asking you for pictures or threatening to share them, that's being done to you too. This part of Struggling With Sin tells you what to do.

Washed, Sanctified, Justified

Maybe you’re the one who sinned, and you feel ruined. Paul warned that neither fornicators, nor idolaters, nor adulterers would inherit the kingdom of God (1 Corinthians 6:9-10). No one who goes on in these sins and won't turn will be there. Then he wrote this.

And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.

1 Corinthians 6:11 (KJV)

They had been fornicators and adulterers, and now they were washed, set apart for God, and declared righteous. That isn’t a second-class pardon. It’s the pardon every saint has.

It rests on Christ alone. He lived a pure life in your place, bore God’s wrath for sin on the cross, and rose from the dead. If you turn from your sin and trust him, his blood washes all of it away. If you’ve never come to him, read The Gospel, and come today.

Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean: wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.

Psalm 51:7 (KJV)

David prayed this after he took another man’s wife and had the man killed. Nathan told him, The Lord also hath put away thy sin (2 Samuel 12:13). Whiter than snow is a picture, and the cleanness it pictures is real. God doesn’t see a forgiven sinner as spoiled goods. He sees him in his Son.

Your Bible also tells of a woman taken in adultery and brought to Jesus (John 8:3-11). Many of the oldest manuscripts don’t have this passage. Faithful teachers differ over whether John wrote it, so we don’t rest on it alone. But what it shows is taught all through Scripture. Jesus said to her, Neither do I condemn thee, and then, go, and sin no more (8:11). Christ’s mercy gives no permission to continue in sin. The forgiven are called to leave it. There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1).

If you’ve been unfaithful in marriage, read I have been unfaithful. If guilt keeps returning after you’ve confessed, read What I did is too great to forgive.

The Fight Against Sexual Sin

A forgiven believer still has a fight, and Scripture says to flee. Joseph left his garment in her hand, and fled (Genesis 39:12). Paul told young Timothy, Flee also youthful lusts (2 Timothy 2:22).

For if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die: but if ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live.

Romans 8:13 (KJV)

To mortify is to put to death. You do it through the Spirit, not by willpower alone. Set Christ before you as better than the sin. Job said, I made a covenant with mine eyes (Job 31:1). So remove what you reach for in weak hours, and tell someone who will ask how it’s going.

When you fall, don’t hide or wait to feel worthy before you come back. Christ was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin, so come boldly to his throne for mercy (Hebrews 4:15-16). If thoughts come that you hate and never chose, that’s a different trouble, with its own help in Doubts and Unwanted Thoughts. For pornography, read I cannot stop looking at what I should not. For adults, Mortify is a free daily help in this fight.

A Picture of Christ and His Church

For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and shall be joined unto his wife, and they two shall be one flesh. This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church.

Ephesians 5:31-32 (KJV)

Christ loved the church, and gave himself for it (5:25). He holds fast to his people and will present them without spot (5:27). The faithful, lifelong love of a husband and wife is a living picture of that, and the union it pictures is real. That’s why sexual sin is so serious. It tells a lie about Christ. Faithful married love tells the truth about him.

It’s also why no one is left out. In the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage (Matthew 22:30), for the picture gives way to the thing itself. Paul told a whole church, I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ (2 Corinthians 11:2). Every believer, single or married, belongs to that bride. The single believer who stays pure isn’t missing the point of sex. He is living toward what it was always meant to show.

Faithfulness in Your Circumstances

If You're Single or Young

The desire for marriage isn’t sin. God made it, and it’s a good thing to bring to him. Lust is something else. It wants what God hasn’t given. Wherewithal shall a young man cleanse his way? by taking heed thereto according to thy word (Psalm 119:9).

  1. Decide now, before the moment comes. Settle what you won’t do, and what places and screens you won’t go near.
  2. Keep your love life in the light. If you’re dating, do it where others can see. Don’t be alone behind closed doors.
  3. If someone asks for sexual pictures, don’t send them. If you already have, or someone threatens to share them, you still deserve help. Tell a safe adult and use the protection and reporting guidance. The person threatening you is responsible for that wrongdoing.
  4. Singleness isn’t second best. Christ was never married, and Paul called his single life a gift (1 Corinthians 7:7). If you long to marry, pray for it and take honest steps toward it. See Singleness as a Gift and a Calling.
  5. If you have fallen, start again today. Remember not the sins of my youth (Psalm 25:7) is a prayer God hears.

If You're Married

  1. Honor your marital duty. Give your spouse loving attention and don't make repeated withholding the normal pattern of marriage. Address ongoing difficulties honestly and never use force, threats, or sex as a weapon.
  2. Guard the door. Keep no secret friendships or hidden screens. If you’re hiding something, bring it into the light with your pastor’s help.
  3. Pray together. You are heirs together of the grace of life (1 Peter 3:7).

If You're a Parent

  1. Be the first to tell them. Teach your children plainly, a little at a time, as they can bear it. If you’re silent, the world won’t be.
  2. Teach the gift before the warnings. Let them hear from you that God made this good and worth waiting for.
  3. Teach them to guard their bodies. Tell them no one may touch them in private places or ask them to keep such a secret. Tell them they can always tell you. If a child tells you of abuse, believe them, and read If a child is being hurt at once.
  4. Watch the screens. Keep devices out of bedrooms at night, use filters, and talk about what they see.
  5. When they fail, show them Christ. Answer a confession not with disgust, but the way your Father answered you, with truth and mercy.
