When Jesus therefore saw his mother, and the disciple standing by, whom he loved, he saith unto his mother, Woman, behold thy son! Then saith he to the disciple, Behold thy mother! And from that hour that disciple took her unto his own home.

John 19:26-27 (KJV)

Dying on the cross in agony, he still saw his mother standing there. He gave her a son to look after her, and a home. He hasn’t stopped seeing the one who is left behind.

A father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows, is God in his holy habitation.

Psalm 68:5 (KJV)

A judge of the widows is a defender who takes up their cause. God cares for children who have lost a parent. So if you have children at home, he hasn’t overlooked them. Let them see you cry, and let them cry too.

The next verse says that God setteth the solitary in families (68:6). He did that for his mother at the cross, and he does it still through his church. That verse is for the widower as much as the widow.

Paul tells a widow’s own family, and then the church, to honor her and care for her (1 Timothy 5:3-5, 16). If the cards and meals have stopped, you may tell your elders so. That isn’t complaining. It lets the church obey.

Anna lost her husband after seven years of marriage and lived as a widow into great old age. Her years alone weren’t empty. She served God with prayer night and day, and her long road brought her to the child Jesus (Luke 2:36-38).

For many, the mornings are the hardest. For a moment you forget, and then you remember. God put a whole book of weeping in the Bible. In the middle of it, with his city in ruins, Jeremiah wrote this.

It is of the Lord’s mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.

Lamentations 3:22-23 (KJV)

Jeremiah didn’t feel better first. Earlier he wrote that God had turned his hand against him all the day (3:3). Then he called God’s mercies to mind, and that gave him hope (3:21). If you’re his, no wrath waits for you in the morning, because Christ bore it on the cross. Mercy waits instead, enough for that one day.

The one who saw to his mother from the cross died there. On the third day he rose, and now he dieth no more (Romans 6:9). He lives to see to his own. Paul says marriage pictures Christ and his church (Ephesians 5:31-32). What it pictures is real. Christ’s love for his church hasn’t died, and if you’re his, it holds you still.

If you don’t know where your husband or wife stood with God, that’s too heavy to carry alone tonight. You aren’t asked to settle where they are. You may leave them with the Judge of all the earth, who will do right (Genesis 18:25), and bring your own soul to Christ. Read When You Fear They Did Not Know Christ, and tell your pastor this week. If they took their own life, that sorrow has its own place here.

Before you sleep, do these three things. If it’s been months or years and the grief still has hold of you, you aren’t failing. These are for you too.

  1. Say his or her name to God. Tell him what you miss, and what you thank him for. If you’re angry, tell him that too. Then pray Psalm 68:5 back to him.
  2. Let others carry the practical things. Call your pastor or an elder, and tell your deacons what needs doing. Give people real jobs, like the meals, help with the children, the papers, and the phone calls. If you have no church, call a Christian friend, and read Do Not Carry It Alone below.
  3. Eat a little, and don’t spend the evenings alone. If you can’t cook, say so. Let someone bring food or bring you to their table. Ask someone to come or call at the hour that’s hardest for you, often supper or bedtime. If you find yourself wanting to follow the one you lost, call 988 or text 988 tonight, and tell someone who can come.

When you are ready to ask about heaven

If your husband or wife belonged to Christ, their soul is with him now, made perfect in holiness (Shorter Catechism Q&A 37).

At the resurrection, believers being raised up in glory, shall be openly acknowledged and acquitted in the day of judgment, and made perfectly blessed in the full enjoying of God to all eternity.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 38

Paul says that when the Lord comes, the dead in Christ shall rise first. Then his people who are still alive will be caught up together with them, and so shall we ever be with the Lord (1 Thessalonians 4:16-17).

In the resurrection, Jesus said, they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven (Matthew 22:30). Yet Christ’s people are raised as themselves, with bodies made like his glorious body (Philippians 3:21). Marriage is God’s gift for this life (Romans 7:2).

Scripture says less than we might wish about how his people will know one another there. But the one who gave his mother a home from the cross is there now, and he is their joy. If you’re his, you’ll see him on the day he chooses, not the day you choose. Until then, he hasn’t finished with your life here.

When you are ready to search your heart

Your grief isn’t sin. Missing them isn’t sin, however long it lasts. This isn’t a search for some sin behind their death. God hasn’t told you why, and no one may guess (Deuteronomy 29:29). But a long sorrow opens doors. Ask God to search you (Psalm 139:23).

Naomi buried her husband and both her sons. She said, Call me not Naomi, call me Mara (Ruth 1:20). Mara means bitter. You may tell God it’s bitter. Don’t let it take root against him or his people. Loneliness can keep you home from worship. It can send you to drink to fill the evenings. It can rush you toward someone new before you have grieved and sought counsel. And the one you lost can come to fill the place only God should fill.

God didn’t scold Naomi, and he didn’t leave her alone. From her house came King David, and at last Christ (Ruth 4:14-17, Matthew 1:5-6). He hasn’t promised you her ending. He offers you her Redeemer. Like Anna, keep close to God’s house and to prayer (Luke 2:37). If you find any of this in you, bring it to Christ. He died for sins like these and rose again, and he turns away no one who comes to him. Confess it, and he is faithful and just to forgive (1 John 1:9).

What to Do This Week

  1. Keep your hours. Go to bed and get up at your usual times. Keep simple food in the house, and sit down to eat it, even alone.
  2. Don’t make big decisions yet. Don’t sell the house, move, or give away their things this week. Those can wait. Ask an elder or a trusted friend before you sign anything.
  3. Handle one practical task. Pick one thing, like calling the bank or finding the papers you need. Ask a friend to sit with you while you do it. Let the rest wait.
  4. Read Lamentations 3 in the morning. Read verses 21 to 24 when you first wake. Write one verse on a card and keep it by the bed.
  5. Go to church Sunday. Sit with someone you know. If walking in alone is too hard, ask a friend to pick you up.
