Find a congregation you can reasonably reach, confirm its service times, and ask the pastor or an elder about visiting. Use the questions below to understand its teaching and care. If distance, disability, transport, or safety makes visiting difficult, tell the congregation what help you need.

Why You Need One

Christ didn’t save you to follow him alone. He gave his Word, the sacraments, and prayer to his church. He set elders over his flock to watch for their souls (Hebrews 13:17). Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together is a command (Hebrews 10:25). A livestream, a podcast, or this website cannot administer the sacraments or provide membership in a local congregation. Remote teaching can help when you are unable to attend, while you seek pastoral care and a way to gather.

Finding a Reformed Presbyterian Church

This site is written from within the Reformed Presbyterian tradition. The Reformed Presbyterian Church of North America, the RPCNA, holds the Westminster Confession and the Larger and Shorter Catechisms as its standards. It also has its own Testimony, a further statement of what it believes and practices. Its congregations sing the Psalms only, without instruments.

  1. Search the directory. Go to reformedpresbyterian.org/congregations and use its map or selection list. Check the congregation’s own contact details and service times before traveling.
  2. For other RP churches, the official directory links sister denominations in Canada, Scotland, Ireland, and Australia. Canada has its own Reformed Presbyterian denomination, so Canadian readers should check that link too.
  3. Call or write the pastor first. Say you’re looking for a church and would like to visit. Ask the service times. Many congregations worship in the morning and again in the afternoon or evening.
  4. Visit on the Lord’s Day. Ask what services it holds and plan to attend as you are able. One visit will not show the whole life of a congregation.
  5. Ask about the Lord’s Supper before you come to it. Reformed Presbyterian elders guard the Table and will want to speak with visitors first. That’s care, not coldness.
  6. Stay for the meal or the fellowship. Ask whether there is a meal or time for fellowship. Let someone know about accessibility or dietary needs rather than assuming arrangements are available.

If There's None Near You

Ask a pastor or presbytery to help you find a faithful congregation you can reach. Look for a church that holds the Westminster Standards in earnest and practices what they teach. Then discuss the questions below with it. Genuine barriers to gathering need practical help, not an assumption that you refuse fellowship.

  1. Ask the RPCNA for help. Write to the nearest congregation or presbytery. Ask whether there is a mission work or a family nearby, or what they would counsel.
  2. Consider whether to move. Moving may be an option, but weigh employment, housing, medical care, custody, and duties to dependents before making a commitment. Seek practical advice and help. A move is not possible for every household.
  3. Do not settle for no church. Seek continuing contact with a faithful church even while attendance is difficult. Ask about transport, pastoral visits, or a nearer congregation rather than accepting permanent isolation.

Questions to Ask Any Church

  1. What do you believe? Does it have a written confession, and does it actually hold to it?
  2. Is the gospel clear? Salvation by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone, preached plainly. See How to Tell a True Church From a False One.
  3. Is the Word preached? Do the sermons open a passage of Scripture, explain it, and apply it?
  4. Is worship ordered by Scripture? Is it made of what God has commanded, Scripture read and preached, prayer, singing of psalms, and the sacraments (WCF 21.1, 21.5)? See How God Wants to Be Worshiped.
  5. Are there elders who shepherd? Do they know the members, visit them, and exercise loving discipline? Ask how concerns about leaders, child safety, and appeals are handled.
  6. How do I join? A faithful church will ask about your faith and your life before receiving you as a member. Expect that, and welcome it.

Then Join It

Visiting isn’t belonging. Once you’ve found a faithful church, ask about membership and read its vows before making them. Serve, give according to your responsibilities, pray, and attend worship. Tell the elders when illness or necessary care prevents attendance. If you’re not sure where to start, use the ministry contact or ask a congregation in the directory.
