What Is a Lawful Oath?

An oath solemnly calls God to witness that what we say is true. We must never use his name lightly.

People take oaths in court and sometimes in public office. Scripture also records lawful appeals to God as witness (Genesis 24:2-9, Hebrews 6:13-17). Westminster Confession 22 teaches that an oath is an act of religious worship and must be taken with reverence, truth, judgment, and righteousness.

The original Confession also states a positive duty. When lawful authority imposes an oath concerning a good and just matter, it ought to be taken. Refusing such an oath is a sin, not an exercise of Christian liberty (WCF 22.2-3).

Jesus forbids rash and dishonest swearing and requires plain truthful speech (Matthew 5:33-37). James gives the same warning (James 5:12). The Confession understands these commands as prohibitions of sinful swearing, rather than a rejection of all solemn lawful oaths. The apostle Paul called God to witness in a solemn matter (2 Corinthians 1:23).

When Must an Oath Be Refused?

No oath may bind a person to sin. If an office requires a promise that conflicts with allegiance to Christ, the Christian mustn't take it. An oath's solemnity doesn't make an unlawful promise good (WCF 22.3-4).

Don't enter an oath without understanding its terms. A Christian must speak truthfully in ordinary conversation, contracts, and testimony. Swearing can't repair dishonesty of heart. It adds the weight of an appeal to God and makes falsehood more serious. Take an oath in the plain sense of its words, without a hidden reservation. Keep a lawful oath even when performance costs you, and even when the promise was made to an unbeliever (WCF 22.4). A promise cannot require sin, but inconvenience alone does not release you from it.

What Is a Religious Vow?

A vow is a solemn promise made to God concerning lawful duties and things within his providence (WCF 22.5-7). Scripture warns against careless vows (Ecclesiastes 5:4-6). An obligation made to God mustn't be treated as an emotional gesture that can be forgotten tomorrow.

The Confession rejects monastic vows of perpetual single life, professed poverty, and regular obedience as higher states of perfection. Those aren't Christian obligations appointed by God. We must never promise an unlawful thing, hinder a duty God commands, or vow what is beyond our power without his promise of ability (WCF 22.7).

What About Church Membership Vows?

A profession of faith and a lawful commitment to live under the church's oversight are solemn matters. Their wording and administration belong to the church's lawful officers, and a candidate should understand what he is promising. Don't borrow membership vows from another denomination and pretend they're binding words of the original Westminster Confession.

Before making such promises, speak with your elders about the requirements of your own congregation and examine them by Scripture. Read Church Membership and Visiting and Joining an RP Church for practical help.
