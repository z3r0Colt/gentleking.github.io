I think I've committed the unpardonable sin

The scribes from Jerusalem couldn’t deny that Jesus was casting out devils. So they said he did it by the prince of the devils. Jesus answered them.

Verily I say unto you, All sins shall be forgiven unto the sons of men, and blasphemies wherewith soever they shall blaspheme: But he that shall blaspheme against the Holy Ghost hath never forgiveness, but is in danger of eternal damnation: Because they said, He hath an unclean spirit.

Mark 3:28-30 (KJV)

The sin Jesus named was a settled verdict. These men saw the Holy Spirit’s work in Christ and called it the devil’s. They weren’t asking for mercy. They were accusing the Savior. If you’re asking Christ for mercy, you’re doing the opposite of what they did, and whoever calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved (Romans 10:13). No exception is written into that promise, so whatever you fear you did, it hasn’t shut you out.

If the fear began with a blasphemous thought that came against your will, that’s no settled verdict. What’s written about thoughts you would never choose is for you.

Hebrews 6 and 10 frighten me

Hebrews 6 speaks of people who were enlightened and tasted the good word of God and were made partakers of the Holy Ghost, and then fell away. They had stood close to Christ and walked away from him with their eyes open. They were like ground that drinks the same rain as a good field and bears only thorns. The passage says such people can’t be renewed to repentance. If you’ve come back to Christ in repentance, or are coming now, you aren’t the person it describes.

But, beloved, we are persuaded better things of you, and things that accompany salvation, though we thus speak.

Hebrews 6:9 (KJV)

Being enlightened, tasting the good Word, and sharing in the life of the visible church aren't identical to regeneration. Some receive great outward privileges and experience the Spirit's work among God's people without possessing saving faith. Hebrews 6 distinguishes these experiences from the "things that accompany salvation" in verse 9. The warning concerns deliberate apostasy. Don't apply it to a repentant believer's ordinary battle with sin.

The writer expected better things of his readers (Hebrews 6:9). His warning was one means by which God would keep them watchful. And the chapter ends with an anchor. It’s a hope sure and steadfast, given with a promise and an oath, so that all who have fled to it for refuge might have strong consolation (6:17-20). If you’ve fled to Christ for refuge, that anchor is yours.

Hebrews 10:26 frightens many for the same reason. It says no sacrifice remains for those who sin wilfully after they've received the knowledge of the truth. A man who keeps falling into a sin he knows is wrong fears it means him. Verse 29 says who is meant. It’s the man who has trodden under foot the Son of God and counted the blood of the covenant an unholy thing. If you keep running back to that blood, you aren’t trampling it. You’re among them that believe to the saving of the soul (10:39).

I might lose my salvation

My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me: And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. My Father, which gave them me, is greater than all; and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father’s hand.

John 10:27-29 (KJV)

Jesus said that no man can pluck his sheep out of his hand, and he said more. They shall never perish. If a sheep could slip away and be lost for good, that promise wouldn’t be true.

This perseverance of the saints depends, not upon their own free will, but upon the immutability of the decree of election, flowing from the free and unchangeable love of God the Father; upon the efficacy of the merit and intercession of Jesus Christ; the abiding of the Spirit and of the seed of God within them; and the nature of the covenant of grace:

Westminster Confession of Faith 17.2

Not one of those supports is your willpower. On the night he was betrayed, Jesus told Peter that Satan wanted to sift the disciples like wheat. He said he had prayed for Peter himself, that his faith would not fail (Luke 22:31-32). Within hours Peter swore with curses that he didn’t know him. His courage failed. His faith didn’t finally fail, because Christ had prayed for it. Before Peter ever fell, Jesus told him to strengthen his brethren once he was turned back.

God preserves his people through warnings like these. Don't turn the doctrine of perseverance into a reason to ignore them. The same Christ who promises to keep his sheep causes them to hear his voice and follow him (John 10:27).

True believers may fall into grievous sins and stay in them for a time (WCF 17.3). They won’t fall all the way, and they won’t fall for good.

When You Fear Falling Away

  1. Ask for mercy out loud. You may ask Christ for mercy now, trusting his promise in Romans 10:13. You don’t need to repeat a prayer until it feels exactly right. If repeated checking or praying has taken over your day, seek professional care alongside pastoral counsel.
  2. Read the whole chapter. Don’t live on one scary verse. Read all of Hebrews 6 down to verse 20, and all of John 10. Notice how each one ends.
  3. Stop searching online at night. Late-night searching feeds the fear. Close the phone and pray instead. Come back to it in daylight with your Bible open.
  4. Tell an elder what you’re afraid of. Name the verse or the thought that scares you. Ask him to read the passage with you and help you understand its warning and promise. Fear may take time to ease.
