In your browser

The Same Sojourner, Anywhere

Sojourner now runs in a web browser, on a phone, a tablet, or a computer. It’s the same Bible study program as the Windows and Linux program, with the same Bibles, commentaries, confessions, Hebrew and Greek, atlas, and notes. There's nothing to buy and nothing to fetch from an app store.

Open app.gentleking.org and it’s there. It’s a progressive web app. That means you can put it on your home screen or in your dock, and then it opens in its own window like any other app. After your first visit, it’ll offer to do that for you.

Installing it

Putting It on Your Home Screen

  1. iPhone and iPad. Open it in Safari or Chrome. Tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen. Please do this. Adding it to the home screen helps with storage persistence, but keep exported backups because browser data can still be lost.
  2. Android. Tap Install when the app offers. Or open the menu in Chrome, the three dots, and choose Install app.
  3. Mac. In Safari, open the File menu and choose Add to Dock. That needs macOS Sonoma or later. In Chrome or Edge, click the install icon at the right of the address bar.
  4. Windows and Linux. In Chrome or Edge, click the install icon in the address bar. Firefox can’t install web apps, but Sojourner works fine in a Firefox tab. If you want everything on your own disk, the desktop program is still there.

Keep it open in one tab at a time. The app’s storage allows only one open copy, and a second tab will tell you so and offer to take over.

The library

The Books, and Using It Offline

The desktop program carries its included Bible-study texts inside the installer. The web app fetches the Bibles, commentaries, lexicons, and reference works in small pieces as you open them. It keeps each piece on your device once you’ve read it. So it starts quickly and grows as you use it.

To use it with no connection at all, open Settings, choose Data and backups, and pick Use without the internet. That downloads the included Bible-study texts, about 900 MB. Optional book shelves are separate. From then on the app works on a plane, in a waiting room, or anywhere without a signal.

The book shelves, such as the Puritans and the Church Fathers, are added from Settings and read online. Keep on this device stores a single book for reading offline. The Atlas has its terrain and satellite maps built in.

Privacy

Your Notes Stay on Your Device

Your notes, highlights, prayers, and sermons are kept in your browser’s private storage on the device you’re using, the way the desktop program keeps them in a file on your computer. There’s no account and no sync. Your notes, searches, prayers and sermons are stored locally rather than uploaded as account data. Requests for online books still reach the content server.

The books come from data.gentleking.org. Cloudflare, which hosts both addresses, keeps ordinary request logs, which can include your network address and the files requested. The app has no account or analytics service.

Back up now and then. A browser can clear a site’s data, and clearing browser storage or removing the app can erase local work. Open Settings, choose Data and backups, and Export. That saves everything to one file, and the app will remind you to do it. The desktop program uses the same kind of file. So you can carry your notes from your phone to Windows or Linux and back again with Import.

Honest limits

What the Web Version Leaves Out

Almost everything in the desktop program is here. A few things aren’t, because a browser can’t do them.

  1. The natural reading voice. The desktop program reads aloud with a voice built into it. In the browser, Sojourner uses your device’s own voices instead.
  2. Backups to a folder. Use Export instead, and keep the file somewhere safe.
  3. Your own audio, video, and MOBI files. Your own EPUB and PDF books do work. They stay in the browser and are not part of an export.
  4. Sync. Each device keeps its own notes. Export and Import carry them across.

If you want all of it on your own computer, with nothing fetched from anywhere, the Windows and Linux program is unchanged and free.
