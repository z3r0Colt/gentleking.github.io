Sometimes I wonder if any of it is true

The pull to walk away isn’t new. When many of his disciples turned back, Jesus put the question to the twelve.

Then said Jesus unto the twelve, Will ye also go away? Then Simon Peter answered him, Lord, to whom shall we go? thou hast the words of eternal life.

John 6:67-68 (KJV)

Peter answered with a question of his own. He knew the other voices on offer, and none of them had the words of eternal life. When doubt tells you to leave, ask it where it means to take you.

The arguments are real, and the answers to objections set them out for a day when your mind is clear. But you didn’t come to faith by argument alone. The Confession says in 1.5 that our full persuasion of the truth of Scripture comes from the inward work of the Holy Spirit, bearing witness by and with the Word in our hearts. So when doubt comes, go back to the Word, and pray as a desperate father once prayed.

And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.

Mark 9:24 (KJV)

He had just admitted he wasn’t sure Jesus could help. Jesus healed his son anyway. He didn’t first make the man measure how much of him believed.

I have thoughts I would never choose

It may be a blasphemy that flashes through your mind in prayer. The harder you push it away, the louder it comes back. John Bunyan, who later wrote The Pilgrim’s Progress, went through a season like this. Blasphemies against God and Christ poured through his mind until he feared he was lost. His book Grace Abounding to the Chief of Sinners tells the story. He came through it and preached Christ for the rest of his life.

A thought that frightens you may have entered your mind without your consent. Ask whether you've embraced it or resisted it, and speak to an elder when you need counsel.

A thought you hate isn’t a thought you hold. Satan once told the Son of God himself to fall down and worship the devil, and Christ was without sin (Matthew 4:9-10). Being tempted isn’t the same as sinning. Whatever in these thoughts rises from the old nature still in you is pardoned in Christ with the rest of your sin. Your horror at them shows where your heart is.

Temptation and consent to sin must be distinguished (Hebrews 4:15 and James 1:14-15).

So don’t argue with them or keep checking whether you meant them. Turn from them to Christ, the way you’d walk past a man shouting in the street. If they come in a loop you can’t break, don’t keep them secret. See a doctor, and if you’re young, tell your parents first. It’s a known problem, sometimes a form of OCD, and there’s real help for it.

When Doubts Keep Returning

  1. Write down your hardest question. Then read the page in the answers to objections that speaks to it, on a morning when you’re rested.
  2. Read one Gospel straight through. Mark is short enough to read in an evening or two. Look at Jesus himself, not only the arguments about him.
  3. Don’t fight unwanted thoughts head on. When one comes, say a short prayer like Mark 9:24 and go on with what you were doing. Don’t go back to check it.
  4. Tell someone. Tell your pastor, an elder, or a parent. If the thoughts come in loops, ask your doctor about OCD.
