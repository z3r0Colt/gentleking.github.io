A loving God would not send anyone there

Your own sense of justice already asks for some kind of hell. A man murders and is never caught, and dies old and comfortable in his bed. Something in you says that can’t be the end of it. It isn’t. What you may not have reckoned with is that God will do to all evil what you want done to some of it. And he will count yours. No one in the Bible warned of hell more often than Jesus, the gentlest man who ever lived.

But I will forewarn you whom ye shall fear: Fear him, which after he hath killed hath power to cast into hell; yea, I say unto you, Fear him.

Luke 12:5 (KJV)

The warning comes from a God who swears by his own life that he has no pleasure in the death of the wicked (Ezekiel 33:11). And hell is exact. Some will be beaten with many stripes and some with few, and the many are for the servant who knew his lord’s will and did not do it (Luke 12:47-48). No one will bear a stroke more than he deserves.

Forever is too long for a short life of sin

A crime isn’t measured by how long it took. A murder can take a second. We weigh a wrong by what was done and by who was wronged, and striking your mother is worse than striking a stranger. Sin is a wrong done to the infinitely holy God, to whom you owe all you have.

Every sin, even the least, being against the sovereignty, goodness, and holiness of God, and against his righteous law, deserveth his wrath and curse, both in this life, and that which is to come; and cannot be expiated but by the blood of Christ.

Westminster Larger Catechism Q&A 152

In Matthew 25:46 the Authorized Version calls the punishment everlasting and the life eternal. In the Greek they’re the same word. If one ends, so does the other. The catechism ends where it must, with the blood of Christ, who bore that wrath so that those who trust him never will.

What to Do This Week

  1. Read Luke start to finish. Notice how often the warnings of hell and the offers of mercy come from the same mouth. Luke 12 and Luke 15 sit only a few pages apart.
  2. Write down what seems unjust to you. Put it in one sentence. Then ask a pastor how he answers it, and listen to the whole answer.
  3. Go to a faithful church on Sunday and listen. Hear whether the preacher warns of judgment and still holds out Christ to you. Both are there for your good.
