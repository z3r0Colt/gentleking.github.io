Reading and Study

Where to Start

Good books are a gift. They are not the gift. The Lord feeds his people by his Word, read and preached, and everything below is a servant of that.

If you are new to the faith, or coming back after years away, the pile can feel heavy. There are a thousand titles and a hundred voices, and it is hard to know what to pick up first. What is below is short on purpose, and it is set in the order a man should read it.

Read a little and read often. The steady reader gets further than the man who reads hard for a week and then puts the book down. Read the Bible before you read about the Bible. Get under faithful preaching. Learn the confession your church holds, because it will keep you from a hundred detours. And read with the aim of knowing Christ, not the aim of winning arguments. Knowledge that does not end in worship has gone wrong somewhere.

Here is an order that has served many people well.

  1. Open the Bible first. Begin with the Gospel of John, then Genesis, then Romans. Read a chapter a day. If you miss a day, pick up where you left off. There is no penance owed for it.
  2. Find a faithful local church and join it. One that preaches the whole counsel of God, guards the Lord’s Table, baptizes, disciplines in love, and sings the truth. Sit under that ministry week after week. No reading list can replace it.
  3. Learn the catechism. The Westminster Shorter Catechism gives you the shape of the whole faith in short answers you can carry in your head. One question a week is not too slow.
  4. Read one book about the God you are meeting. Packer or Sproul will do. You want your thoughts of God enlarged before you read much of anything else.
  5. Then read one book about the gospel and one about holiness. Justification first, then the life that follows it. That order matters more than people think.
  6. Pray what you read. Turn the page into asking. This is the step most often skipped, and it is the step that turns study into communion.

2 Timothy 3:16-17 (ESV)

The Standards

A confession is not a rival to Scripture. It is the church saying out loud, carefully and publicly, what she understands the Scriptures to teach. That is a mercy. It means you do not have to build your theology from scratch, alone, in your kitchen, and it means the church can be held to something.

This site holds the Westminster Standards.

Westminster Confession of Faith
Thirty-three chapters covering Scripture, God, the covenants, Christ, justification, adoption, sanctification, the church, the sacraments, and the last things. Cited here as WCF 7.3, chapter and section.
Westminster Shorter Catechism
One hundred and seven questions, written for children and useful for everyone. Cited as WSC Q&A 33. Start here if you have never read a catechism.
Westminster Larger Catechism
Fuller and slower, and especially rich on the law and on the work of Christ. Cited as WLC Q&A 39.

Man by his fall having made himself incapable of life by that covenant, the Lord was pleased to make a second, commonly called the covenant of grace; wherein He freely offereth unto sinners life and salvation by Jesus Christ; requiring of them faith in Him, that they may be saved, and promising to give unto all those that are ordained unto eternal life His Holy Spirit, to make them willing, and able to believe.

Westminster Confession of Faith 7.3

The offer in that sentence is free, and it is held out to anyone who hears it. And the same God who offers also gives his Spirit to make dead sinners willing. You are not asked to make yourself alive before you come. Come, and you will find that he was at work in you first.

You should also know the sister documents of the Reformation and the century after it. The Heidelberg Catechism is warm and pastoral and begins with the question of your only comfort in life and in death. The Belgic Confession is an older and sturdy summary of the same faith. The Canons of Dort answer the Remonstrants point by point and give us the doctrines of grace in their clearest confessional form. The 1689 London Baptist Confession follows Westminster closely and differs chiefly on baptism and the church, and our Baptist brothers who hold it are reading the same gospel we are.

The full text of the Westminster Confession is posted free by the Orthodox Presbyterian Church.

Books for Your First Year

Nine books. You do not need all of them, and you certainly do not need them at once. Pick one, finish it, then pick another.

Knowing God

J.I. Packer

A good first book to put in a new believer’s hands. It is about God himself, and it will not let you stay at arm’s length.

The Holiness of God

R.C. Sproul

Isaiah in the temple, and what happens to a man when he sees the Lord high and lifted up. Read this before you read anything about yourself.

Chosen by God

R.C. Sproul

A short introduction to election and the sovereignty of God in salvation. Patient with objections, and unwilling to trim the text.

Holiness

J.C. Ryle

Searching, and full of Christ. Ryle will show you your sin and then show you your Savior, and he never reverses the order.

The Gospel According to Jesus

John MacArthur

What our Lord actually called people to when he called them. A needed corrective if you have only heard a gospel with no repentance in it.

The Pilgrim’s Progress

John Bunyan

The Christian life pictured as a hard road out of the City of Destruction. Read it aloud with your family. It has carried plain believers for centuries.

All of Grace

Charles Spurgeon

Tender, and aimed straight at the anxious sinner who wants to know whether Christ will have him. He will.

Concise Theology

J.I. Packer

Short chapters, each on one doctrine. Useful as a reference you keep near the Bible rather than a book you read straight through.

The Doctrine of Repentance

Thomas Watson

A Puritan on what real turning from sin looks like and what counterfeits look like. Small book, long shadow.

When You Are Ready for Weight

These are slower. Some of them use older English, and you will want a modernized edition for a few. The slow pace is the point. A page of Owen thought through is worth a chapter of almost anything else skimmed.

The Mortification of Sin

John Owen

On killing remaining sin by the Spirit. Owen is exact and relentless, and he keeps your eye on Christ while he works.

Communion with God

John Owen

Fellowship with the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, considered distinctly. One God in three persons, and Owen shows you how to draw near to each. It will change how you pray.

The Rare Jewel of Christian Contentment

Jeremiah Burroughs

Contentment as something learned, not something felt. Written for people with real losses, not comfortable people with small complaints.

Precious Remedies Against Satan’s Devices

Thomas Brooks

The enemy’s tactics laid out, with the Scriptural answer to each. Practical in a way few modern books manage.

A Body of Divinity

Thomas Watson

The Shorter Catechism expounded, question by question, with more warmth than you expect from the title.

The Bruised Reed

Richard Sibbes

For the weak and the failing. Sibbes opens the promise that our King will not break a bruised reed, and the book is balm for a wounded conscience.

The Christian in Complete Armour

William Gurnall

Ephesians 6 opened at great length. A lifetime of daily reading rather than a book you finish in a month.

Institutes of the Christian Religion

John Calvin

Far more devotional than its reputation. Read Book One and Book Three first if the whole thing looks like too much.

The Whole Christ

Sinclair Ferguson

Legalism, antinomianism, and assurance, worked out through an old Scottish controversy. It will help you hold the law and the gospel together the way the confession does.

You do not receive the benefits of Christ apart from Christ himself. Every one of these books is only useful as it hands you to him.

Preaching to Sit Under

Reading forms you slowly. Preaching forms you weekly, and the preaching that forms you most is the preaching in the room where you can be known and corrected. Everything here is second to that.

With that said, there are faithful men whose recorded teaching has fed many. John MacArthur on the text, verse by verse, at Grace to You. R.C. Sproul and Sinclair Ferguson on doctrine and the holiness of God at Ligonier. John Piper on the worth of God at Desiring God. Spurgeon’s sermons, still preaching after all this time, are collected at the Spurgeon Center.

Paul Washer preaches repentance and the fear of God with a plainness that is hard to shake off. Voddie Baucham is worth hearing on the sufficiency of Scripture and on the home. Jeff Durbin is useful when you want to see the faith defended in public without apology. For help thinking about what a healthy church actually is, 9Marks is a good place to go, and Banner of Truth keeps the Puritans in print.

One caution, and it is not a small one. No teacher on this list is a substitute for your local church, and no teacher on this list is beyond error. Weigh every one of them, and weigh this site too, against the Scriptures.

Acts 17:11 (ESV)

How to Actually Read

A plan helps. Pick one and keep it, whether that is a chapter a day or a yearly plan that walks you through the whole of Scripture. The plan is a servant. If it becomes a burden that shames you every time you fall behind, shorten it rather than quit it.

Read slowly. Most of us have been trained to skim, and skimming is exactly the wrong habit here. Take a short passage and stay in it. Ask what it says, what it says about God, what it says about you, and where Christ is in it. He is always somewhere in it.

And beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself.

Luke 24:27 (ESV)

Pray the text. Turn the promise into thanks and the warning into confession. This is the difference between studying a document and meeting a person. And read with others. Read with your wife or your husband, and with your children at the table. Read with a friend from church who will ask you what you saw. You will see more, and you will be kept from your own blind spots.

And let us consider how to stir up one another to love and good works, not neglecting to meet together, as is the habit of some, but encouraging one another, and all the more as you see the Day drawing near.

Hebrews 10:24-25 (ESV)

Finally, remember who you are reading toward. Not a system, though the system is true. A King who is gentle with those who come to him. He rode into his own city on a donkey, and he holds all authority in heaven and on earth. That King calls you, and he turns no one away.

Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls.

Matthew 11:29 (ESV)

If you are not sure where you stand with him, start at the gospel rather than a reading list.
