I do not know if I am saved

You can know. The Confession calls assurance something far firmer than a guess. It names three grounds for it, in an order that protects a frightened conscience.

This certainty is not a bare conjectural and probable persuasion grounded upon a fallible hope; but an infallible assurance of faith, founded upon the divine truth of the promises of salvation, the inward evidence of those graces unto which these promises are made, the testimony of the Spirit of adoption witnessing with our spirits that we are the children of God:

Westminster Confession of Faith 18.2

Start with the promises, because they don’t change when you do. God has promised everlasting life to everyone who believes on the Son (John 3:36). That was as true on the morning you felt nothing as on the morning you wept for joy.

Then look at the graces with an honest eye. A weak trust in Christ counts. So does hatred of the sin you keep falling into, and grief that you love him so little. Every grace is imperfect in this life, so the graces confirm the promise and never replace it. Start with them and you’ll find a reason to doubt each one.

Last comes the Spirit himself, who bears witness with our spirit that we are the children of God (Romans 8:16). He bears that witness through the Word, often quietly. You see it in the simple fact that you come to God as a child comes to his father.

Maybe my faith was never real

The Confession says in 18.3 that assurance is not of the essence of faith, and that a true believer may wait long before he has it. A lack of certainty doesn’t prove a lack of faith. The Larger Catechism goes further.

One who doubteth of his being in Christ, or of his due preparation to the sacrament of the Lord’s supper, may have true interest in Christ, though he be not yet assured thereof; and in God’s account hath it, if he be duly affected with the apprehension of the want of it, and unfeignedly desires to be found in Christ, and to depart from iniquity:

Westminster Larger Catechism Q&A 172

Maybe it troubles you that you aren’t sure, and you truly want to be found in Christ and done with your sin. Then the Catechism says you may belong to Christ before you’re sure of it, and that in God’s account you do. That answer asks what you want. It doesn’t ask whether you’ve won the fight yet.

Some people are certain they're saved because they once made a profession, grew up in church, or had a powerful experience. The Confession warns that hypocrites and other unregenerate people may deceive themselves with false hopes (WCF 18.1). Assurance isn't the same as confidence in an old decision. Scripture calls us to examine whether we're in the faith (2 Corinthians 13:5), and to look for repentance and love as fruits of genuine faith (1 John 2:3-6). Yet those fruits don't purchase salvation, and constant self-inspection can't give life. Christ saves sinners who trust him.

There’s also a shorter road than weighing your faith. You don’t have to settle whether the first time was real. You can come to Christ today.

I am afraid I was not chosen

You don't need to discover the secret decree before believing Christ. The gospel commands you to repent and believe, and Christ promises to receive those who come to him (John 6:37). Election is true, and the Confession says it should bring abundant consolation to all who sincerely obey the gospel. The gospel is obeyed by believing it, and Paul puts the two words side by side (Romans 10:16). You aren’t asked to read the decree to find your name there. Nobody can read it and find his name left out.

Jesus said no one can come to him unless the Father draws him (John 6:44). If you’ve come to Christ, however weakly, the Father drew you. So believers may be assured of their election from the certainty of their calling (WCF 3.8), and Peter tells us to make our calling and election sure (2 Peter 1:10). Look for your election in the call you answered.

Return to Christ's Promises

  1. Read one promise a day. Try John 3:36, John 6:37, Romans 10:13, and 1 John 5:13. Read it slowly and ask, is this true? Then rest on it.
  2. Pray simply. Tell God you want Christ and want to be done with your sin. Ask him to make you sure. You don’t need the right words.
  3. Stop checking your feelings every hour. Feelings rise and fall. When the fear comes, turn it into a short prayer and get back to your day.
  4. Talk to your pastor or an elder. Tell them you aren’t sure you’re saved. This is part of their job, and they’ve heard it before.
  5. Be in church Sunday. God strengthens faith through the preached Word and the sacraments. Go even if you feel nothing.
