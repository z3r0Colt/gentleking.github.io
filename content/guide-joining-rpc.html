Your First Lord's Day

You can visit a Reformed Presbyterian congregation without knowing the order of worship or all the answers to a membership interview.

Find a congregation through the RPCNA congregation directory. Check the service times and introduce yourself to the pastor or an elder. Ask where to sit and whether the congregation meets again later in the day.

You should expect Scripture to be read and preached, public prayer, and the singing of inspired Psalms without instrumental accompaniment. Listen carefully to the sermon and follow the words of the Psalm being sung. Ask for a psalter or help following the service if the words or tunes are unfamiliar. Let someone know about hearing, mobility, or other accessibility needs before you travel if possible.

If the Lord's Supper is administered, speak with an elder before approaching the Table. The session has responsibility for admitting communicants. A respectful conversation protects both the visitor and the congregation.

Which Standards Should You Read?

This website teaches the original 1647 Westminster Confession and Catechisms. The RPCNA has its own contemporary Constitution, including a Testimony and directories governing membership, discipline, and worship. The two sets of documents must be distinguished. The RPCNA's present procedures aren't quotations from the original 1647 Confession.

Read the original Westminster Standards study index. For the denomination's current requirements, consult its published Constitution. Ask the elders how the congregation subscribes to and applies those standards.

The Membership Conversation

Tell the session that you want to join. If you were baptized elsewhere, describe the baptism and the church where it occurred. Bring any membership transfer information you have. If records are unavailable or contact with a former church is unsafe, explain that rather than delaying the conversation indefinitely. The session will examine your profession of faith and explain the covenant of communicant membership.

Expect to discuss the authority of Scripture, the Trinity, repentance, faith in Christ, baptism, the Lord's Day, and submission to the church's biblical government. You're not being asked to prove you know every detail of the Westminster Larger Catechism. You should understand the basic doctrine you're professing and the promises you're making.

Ask the session to show you the current Directory for Church Government and the actual membership vows, and explain how they apply to your admission. Read the promises before making them, and ask about anything you cannot yet affirm. Current denominational documents should govern the process rather than a simplified summary on this page. What About Your Spouse and Children?

If you have children, ask the elders about their place in the covenant community and their baptism. The original Westminster Confession teaches the baptism of children of believing parents (WCF 28.4). Parents are responsible to teach their children Scripture, lead them in prayer, and bring them into the church's worship.

Don't assume that your spouse's journey and yours will be identical. Speak openly about religious differences in the home. Seek pastoral counsel without treating the church as a weapon in a family dispute. A spouse does not have to profess a faith he or she does not hold. If religious conflict involves threats or abuse, seek protective help.

After You're Received

Get to know the congregation. Attend public worship, join its prayer and fellowship, and let the elders know when you need counsel. Learn the Psalms you sing. Prepare for the Lord's Table with self-examination and confidence in Christ, not confidence in your own worthiness.

Read Church Membership, Baptism, and The Lord's Supper. If you haven't been baptized, the session can arrange the sacrament according to the church's order.
