What Heresy Is

A heresy is not any teaching you disagree with. It is the denial of a truth so central that without it there is no Christian faith left. Peter called them damnable heresies, even denying the Lord that bought them (2 Peter 2:1).

It helps to sort disagreements into three kinds.

  1. Heresy. Denies the gospel or the God of the gospel. A man who holds it and will not be corrected is outside the faith, whatever he calls himself.
  2. Serious error. Wrong on an important teaching, and harmful to the church, but not a denial of the gospel itself. Worth contending about. Not a reason to call someone lost.
  3. Differences among the faithful. Questions on which godly, confessional Christians have disagreed for centuries, such as the millennium. Hold your view, and hold it charitably.

The Truths That Mark the Line

The early church settled these in the Apostles’, Nicene, and Athanasian Creeds and at Chalcedon, and the Reformation recovered the gospel itself. Denying any of them is heresy.

  1. One God in three persons. The Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, one God, the same in substance, equal in power and glory (WSC 6). Denying the Trinity is heresy.
  2. Christ fully God. The eternal Son, not a created being or a lesser god (John 1:1, Colossians 2:9).
  3. Christ fully man. A true body and a reasonable soul, born of the virgin Mary (Hebrews 2:14-17, 1 John 4:2-3).
  4. Christ one person. Two natures, not two persons, and not a mixture of the two.
  5. His death for sinners and his bodily resurrection. If Christ be not raised, your faith is vain (1 Corinthians 15:17).
  6. Salvation by grace alone through faith alone. Any message that makes our works part of the ground of our acceptance with God is another gospel (Galatians 1:6-9, Romans 3:28).
  7. Scripture as God’s Word. A teaching that sets a new revelation or a church above Scripture cuts away the ground every other truth stands on.

Where It Shows Up

Some groups that use Christian words deny these truths in their official teaching.

  1. Jehovah’s Witnesses deny the Trinity and teach that Jesus is a created being.
  2. The Latter-day Saints teach that God was once a man and that there are many gods, and they add books to Scripture.
  3. Oneness Pentecostals deny the Trinity and teach that the Father, Son, and Spirit are one person in different modes.
  4. The Roman Catholic Church at the Council of Trent condemned justification by faith alone, and it still teaches that grace infused into the believer and his works are part of his justification. That is a denial of the gospel the Reformers recovered.
  5. The prosperity gospel turns Christ into a means to wealth and health, and it often teaches that faith is a force men use to get what they want.

This is about what a group officially teaches. God alone knows each heart, and people inside those groups need the gospel told to them plainly and kindly.

How to Respond

  1. Know the truth well. The surest guard is a firm grasp of the Shorter Catechism and the Creeds. Counterfeits are spotted by those who know the real thing.
  2. Earnestly contend for the faith which was once delivered unto the saints (Jude 3). Do not shrug off heresy as a difference of opinion.
  3. Be gentle with the person. Paul says to correct them in meekness, if God peradventure will give them repentance to the acknowledging of the truth (2 Timothy 2:25).
  4. Do not give a heretic a platform. John told the church not to receive one who brings another doctrine of Christ or to bid him God speed (2 John 10).
  5. Leave church discipline to the church. A man that is an heretick after the first and second admonition reject (Titus 3:10). That is the elders’ work, not a private verdict.
