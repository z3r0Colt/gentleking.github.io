The Word of God

Sola Scriptura

Everything else on this page rests here. If God hasn’t spoken, we have nothing to say.

God has spoken, and his speech has been written down. The old word for it's inspiration. The Bible didn’t float down from heaven. Men wrote it, in their own words and settings and styles, and the Holy Spirit carried them along so that what they wrote is what God says.

All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness: That the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works.

2 Timothy 3:16-17 (KJV)

Because God breathed it out, and because God doesn’t lie, the Bible is true in all that it affirms. Inerrancy concerns the Scriptures as God gave them. God has also preserved his Word by his singular care and providence through the ages (WCF 1.8). We do not appeal to a revelation lost to the church. The Hebrew and Greek Scriptures remain its authoritative rule. Copies and translations must be tested against that text.

Scripture is also sufficient for what God calls us to believe and do. Sufficiency means you don’t need a new revelation to be saved and to live before God. Neither private revelations nor hidden traditions can add to the rule God has given. The whole counsel of God needed for salvation, faith and life is either expressly set down in Scripture or follows from it by good and necessary consequence, as the Confession says in WCF 1.6.

And because God meant to be understood, the Bible is clear where it matters most. Not every verse is equally easy. Peter said as much about Paul. But the things you must know to be saved are set out clearly enough that an unlearned reader, using the ordinary means, can understand them. WCF 1.7 says so. That’s why a working man with a Bible and a lunch break can feed on the Word of God.

The Confession says the Old Testament was written in Hebrew and the New in Greek, and that these original texts are finally authoritative in disputes (WCF 1.8). It calls for Scripture to be translated into the common languages of the nations. A faithful translation is a gift to the church. We ought to study difficult words carefully rather than treat a preferred English wording as the only inspired text.

When a verse is hard, read what comes before and after it, and compare other passages that speak to the same matter. Scripture is its own infallible interpreter (WCF 1.9). The true and full sense of a passage isn't ours to invent. God gave it, and we're to seek it.

What then about the confessions and catechisms this site holds? They're subordinate standards. They’re a faithful summary of what Scripture teaches, tested by the church across centuries. They sit under the Bible and never beside it. This site teaches the original 1647 Westminster Confession and Catechisms. Later revisions mustn't be mistaken for their wording.

The supreme Judge, by which all controversies of religion are to be determined, and all decrees of councils, opinions of ancient writers, doctrines of men, and private spirits, are to be examined, and in whose sentence we are to rest, can be no other but the Holy Spirit speaking in the Scripture.

Westminster Confession of Faith 1.10
