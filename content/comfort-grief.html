In the first days you may need someone to answer calls, help with arrangements, or simply sit with you. If children are grieving, let them ask questions and tell the truth gently.

When Jesus therefore saw her weeping, and the Jews also weeping which came with her, he groaned in the spirit, and was troubled, And said, Where have ye laid him? They said unto him, Lord, come and see. Jesus wept.

John 11:33-35 (KJV)

Jesus knew he was about to raise Lazarus, yet he wept with those who mourned. His tears show that sorrow at death is no failure of faith. You may grieve the person you’ve lost while trusting Christ’s promise of resurrection.

If they took their own life, that grief has its own place here. If you fear they didn’t know Christ, there’s a word for you below.

Paul doesn’t tell Christians not to grieve. He tells them not to grieve as those who have no hope.

But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.

1 Thessalonians 4:13-14 (KJV)

Scripture calls the death of a believer sleep. That’s a picture, and Jesus explained it himself. When the disciples thought Lazarus was only resting, he told them plainly that Lazarus was dead (John 11:11-14). The picture belongs to the body, which lies down for a while and will wake. The soul neither dies nor sleeps (Westminster Confession 32.1).

The souls of believers are at their death made perfect in holiness, and do immediately pass into glory; and their bodies, being still united to Christ, do rest in their graves till the resurrection.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 37

If the one you love belonged to Christ, they’re with him now, free of every sin they ever fought. Paul calls being with Christ far better (Philippians 1:23). He also recognized that his own work and responsibilities remained while he lived (1:24). You may have trouble imagining tomorrow without the person you lost. If your grief has become thoughts of ending your own life, please tell someone safe near you now and read the direct help for those thoughts. You deserve care for that danger as well as for your grief.

And if they're his, the grave won’t keep them. The one who wept at the tomb of Lazarus went into a tomb of his own. On the third day he left it empty.

But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.

1 Corinthians 15:20 (KJV)

The firstfruits were the first sheaves of the harvest. They were brought to God as a pledge that the rest of the field was coming in. Christ’s empty tomb is that pledge. On the last day God will wipe away all tears from the eyes of his people, and there will be no more death (Isaiah 25:8, Revelation 21:3-4). The Lord himself will put an end to their mourning, and death will never return.

If you fear they didn’t know Christ, that grief is real, and Scripture makes room for it. Jesus wept over a city that would not come to him (Luke 19:41). You aren’t asked to settle where they are. Leave them with the Judge of all the earth, who will do right (Genesis 18:25), and bring your own soul to Christ. When You Fear They Did Not Know Christ says more.

When a Date Brings It Back

Birthdays, holidays, and the anniversary of a death can reopen sorrow after months of relative peace. There’s no required date by which a Christian must stop weeping. It may help to speak with someone who knew the person, or to set aside time to remember them.

Grief can leave you impatient or angry with people around you. Where you sin, confess it. But don’t treat the return of sadness as wrongdoing. Paul’s teaching on sorrow with hope is grounded in Christ’s resurrection, not in how quickly a person recovers (1 Thessalonians 4:13-14).

After the Visitors Leave

Church members may not know how hard the evenings have become. Tell a pastor or friend when you need a visit. Read a psalm with someone if you can’t pray much alone. If sadness or sleeplessness makes ordinary life unmanageable, speak with a doctor.

You may want to keep photographs and stories close, or put them away for a season. Neither choice measures your faith. Remembering someone who has died can remain part of your life while you continue to serve the people God has placed beside you.
