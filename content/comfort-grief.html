When Jesus therefore saw her weeping, and the Jews also weeping which came with her, he groaned in the spirit, and was troubled, And said, Where have ye laid him? They said unto him, Lord, come and see. Jesus wept.

John 11:33-35 (KJV)

Jesus knew he was about to call Lazarus out of the grave. He wept anyway. He didn’t hurry Mary past her grief, and he won’t hurry you past yours. Your tears aren’t a failure of faith. They're love, going on after the one you love has gone.

If they took their own life, that grief has its own place here. If you fear they didn’t know Christ, there’s a word for you below.

Paul doesn’t tell Christians not to grieve. He tells them not to grieve as those who have no hope.

But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.

1 Thessalonians 4:13-14 (KJV)

Scripture calls the death of a believer sleep. That’s a picture, and Jesus explained it himself. When the disciples thought Lazarus was only resting, he told them plainly that Lazarus was dead (John 11:11-14). The picture belongs to the body, which lies down for a while and will wake. The soul neither dies nor sleeps (Westminster Confession 32.1).

The souls of believers are at their death made perfect in holiness, and do immediately pass into glory; and their bodies, being still united to Christ, do rest in their graves till the resurrection.

Westminster Shorter Catechism Q&A 37

If the one you love belonged to Christ, they’re with him now, free of every sin they ever fought. Paul calls being with Christ far better (Philippians 1:23). In the next breath he says that to stay is more needful, because others still need him (1:24). Others still need you too, and God hasn’t finished with your life. And if they're his, the grave won’t keep them. The one who wept at the tomb of Lazarus went into a tomb of his own. On the third day he left it empty.

But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.

1 Corinthians 15:20 (KJV)

The firstfruits were the first sheaves of the harvest. They were brought to God as a pledge that the rest of the field was coming in. Christ’s empty tomb is that pledge. On the last day God will wipe away all tears from the eyes of his people, and there will be no more death (Isaiah 25:8, Revelation 21:3-4). The Lord himself will put an end to their mourning, and death will never return.

If you fear they didn’t know Christ, that grief is real, and Scripture makes room for it. Jesus wept over a city that would not come to him (Luke 19:41). You aren’t asked to settle where they are. Leave them with the Judge of all the earth, who will do right (Genesis 18:25), and bring your own soul to Christ. When You Fear They Did Not Know Christ says more.

Before you sleep, do these three things. If your loss was long ago and the grief hasn’t let go, that isn’t a failure of faith. These are for you too.

  1. Tell the Lord the truth. Martha and Mary both told Jesus that if he had been there, their brother wouldn’t have died. You can speak as plainly as they did. He didn’t rebuke either of them (John 11:21, 32).
  2. Call your pastor or an elder. Even at night. If you have no church, call a Christian friend, and read Do Not Carry It Alone below. If you’re young, tell your mom or dad, or a teacher or counselor at school.
  3. Let someone sit with you. Job’s friends did their best work when they sat with him and said nothing (Job 2:13). Eat a little, and lie down even if sleep won’t come.

When you're ready to search your heart

Not tonight. This isn’t a search for some sin behind their death. God hasn’t told you why, and no one may guess (Deuteronomy 29:29). Your grief isn’t sin. Jesus wept. Grief may also expose bitterness, envy, or anger. Bring those things before the Lord as you notice them. Pray, Search me, O God, and know my heart (Psalm 139:23).

You may pour out all your sorrow to God, and even ask him why. Jesus did, on the cross. But don’t accuse him. Job lost his children in one day, and in all this Job sinned not, nor charged God foolishly (Job 1:22). Watch for bitterness toward family or friends (Ephesians 4:31). Watch for drink or drugs to dull the nights. Don’t stay away from worship for long. And don’t let love for the one you lost take the place of God. Loving them isn’t the sin. Living for them in place of him would be.

If you find any of this in you, don’t despair. Thomas wasn’t with the others the first time the risen Jesus came. Eight days later he was back with them, and Jesus came again and said, Peace be unto you (John 20:24-26). Christ died for sins like these, and rose again, and he turns away no one who comes to him. Confess it, and he is faithful and just to forgive (1 John 1:9).

Through the Next Few Days

  1. Eat and sleep. Grief wears out the body. Eat something at each meal, even a little, and go to bed at your usual time. If you can’t eat or sleep for days, see your doctor.
  2. Say yes to one offer of help. When someone asks what they can do, give them a real answer. Let one person bring a meal or run one errand.
  3. Read Psalm 23 once a day. Read it slowly and pray it back to God. If one verse is all you can take in, that’s enough.
  4. Don’t make big decisions yet. Don’t sell the house, move, or give away their things this week. Those choices can wait. Ask someone you trust before you sign anything.
  5. Handle one practical task. Pick one thing, like one phone call or one form. Do just that one, and let the rest wait for another day.
