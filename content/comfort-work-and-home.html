The Son of God was rich, and for his people’s sake he became poor (2 Corinthians 8:9). He had nowhere to lay his head (Matthew 8:20). He is not ashamed of your need, and you may grieve what is gone. He said this to his disciples.

Therefore take no thought, saying, What shall we eat? or, What shall we drink? or, Wherewithal shall we be clothed? (For after all these things do the Gentiles seek:) for your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things. But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.

Matthew 6:31-33 (KJV)

Take no thought means do not be anxious. This is a Father’s promise to his children, and he keeps it his own way. The Larger Catechism teaches us to ask for a competent portion, as to his fatherly wisdom shall seem best (Q&A 193). A competent portion means enough. He promises what you truly need. He does not promise that the old life comes back.

Habakkuk wrote this knowing an army was coming to strip the fields and the flocks his people lived on.

Although the fig tree shall not blossom, neither shall fruit be in the vines; the labour of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no meat; the flock shall be cut off from the fold, and there shall be no herd in the stalls: Yet I will rejoice in the Lord, I will joy in the God of my salvation.

Habakkuk 3:17-18 (KJV)

Habakkuk’s joy was not in the loss but in the God who was not lost. He trembled first (3:16), and you may too. If a fire or a flood took your home, God is still his people’s refuge, a very present help in trouble (Psalm 46:1). If it took someone you love, that grief has its own place here.

If anyone told you that God wants you rich, or that you lost it for lack of faith, Scripture says otherwise. Paul went hungry too, and learned to be content in hunger and in plenty through Christ (Philippians 4:11-13). Losing work is not a sin. If your own sin had a part in it, confess it to God, who is faithful and just to forgive (1 John 1:9).

Christ died on the cross in the place of sinners and rose on the third day. By his resurrection God gives his people new birth into a living hope, and an inheritance that fadeth not away, reserved in heaven for them (1 Peter 1:3-4). If you are his, no fire, flood, or lost job can reach it.

God’s adopted children are pitied, protected, and provided for by their Father (Westminster Confession 12.1), often through his church.

Saints, by profession, are bound to maintain an holy fellowship and communion in the worship of God, and in performing such other spiritual services as tend to their mutual edification; as also in relieving each other in outward things, according to their several abilities and necessities.

Westminster Confession of Faith 26.2

Christians are bound to help one another with food, money, and a roof, and the deacons lead that work. Asking for help is not a failure of faith. It is how the church does what Christ gave it to do.

Before you sleep, do these three things.

  1. Ask for today’s bread. Pray as Jesus taught, Give us this day our daily bread (Matthew 6:11). Tell the Lord the fear, and any shame too.
  2. Tell the people who can help. If you are married, tell your husband or wife all of it. Tell your deacons what you need, whether rent, food, or a bed tonight. If you have no church, call a Christian friend, and read Do Not Carry It Alone below.
  3. Eat something, and lie down. Tomorrow’s bread is tomorrow’s prayer. Your life is worth more than any job, savings, or house. If you think others would be better off without you, that thought is a lie. If you have thoughts of ending your life, call 988 or text 988 tonight, and tell someone near you.

When you are ready to search your heart

This is not a hunt for the cause of your loss. It is a look at how you are bearing it, for want has temptations of its own.

Fear can harden into distrust of God. You may envy those who kept what you lost. Asaph confessed, I was envious at the foolish, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked (Psalm 73:3). Want can tempt you to cut a corner, or to chase quick money. Agur asked God for neither poverty nor riches, because he knew want could tempt him to steal (Proverbs 30:8-9). If an employer wronged you, seeking what you are owed is right, but do not let a grudge take root. Pride can refuse help, or refuse humbler work. Humble work is no fall. The Son of God worked with his hands as a carpenter (Mark 6:3). And the job may have been where you kept your worth. If you are his, your worth is kept in Christ, where no lost job can reach it.

If you find any of this in you, bring it to Christ, who became poor for his people, died, and rose again. He turns away no one who comes, and he is faithful and just to forgive (1 John 1:9). Then ask for this day’s bread, and do the next thing.
