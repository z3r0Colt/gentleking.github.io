Why a Household Worships

God commanded parents to teach his words to their children at home, when they sit, walk, lie down, and rise up (Deuteronomy 6:6-7). The Confession says God is to be worshiped in private families daily, as well as in secret and in public (WCF 21.6). The Church of Scotland wrote a whole Directory for Family-Worship in 1647 to help households do it.

Fathers, this is your charge first (Ephesians 6:4). But a mother with children, a single believer with roommates, or a couple with no children can keep it too.

What to Do

It does not need to be long. Ten or fifteen minutes is enough. The old pattern has three parts.

  1. Read. A short passage, read straight through the Bible a chapter or part of a chapter at a time. Ask one or two simple questions about it. What does this teach us about God? What does it tell us to do?
  2. Sing. A psalm, or part of one. Learn a few tunes and use them often.
  3. Pray. Thank God, confess sin, and ask for the needs of the family, the church, and the world. Let older children pray too.

Many households add a question or two from the Shorter Catechism, or the Catechism for Young Children with little ones.

How to Start and Keep Going

  1. Pick a time you already share. Right after supper works for many families, because everyone is already at the table.
  2. Start small. One passage, one psalm, one prayer. Do it tonight.
  3. Do not wait until you feel ready. You do not need to preach. Read, sing, and pray.
  4. Miss a night, pick it up the next. Do not give up because you broke a streak. There is no streak.
  5. Ask your elders for help. Ask them what other families in your church do.
