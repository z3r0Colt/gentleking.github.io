I keep going back to the same sin

If you think a real Christian would be free of this by now, read Romans 7. Paul wrote it as an apostle, and he says he does the very thing he hates. There’s a war in every believer between the flesh and the spirit, and it lasts as long as this life (WCF 13.2).

God doth continue to forgive the sins of those that are justified; and although they can never fall from the state of justification, yet they may by their sins fall under God’s fatherly displeasure, and not have the light of his countenance restored unto them, until they humble themselves, confess their sins, beg pardon, and renew their faith and repentance.

Westminster Confession of Faith 11.5

God gave his verdict when you believed, and he doesn’t reopen the case. What your sin can bring on you is his fatherly displeasure. He may hide the light of his face until you come back humbled and confess it. He may use the rod. Yet he won’t take his lovingkindness from you (Psalm 89:30-33). That’s a father correcting his son, and there’s nothing of the judge in it.

My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous: And he is the propitiation for our sins:

1 John 2:1-2 (KJV)

Your advocate doesn’t plead that you’re innocent. He pleads his own blood. That plea doesn’t wear thin as your record grows, because it never rested on your record.

So don’t hide the sin, and don’t make peace with it. Remaining sin may win for a time, but the part of you that is born again will overcome, because the Spirit of Christ keeps giving it strength (WCF 13.3). For the long fight itself, read Struggling With Sin.

What I did is too great to forgive

You may carry one sin heavier than all the rest. If you did it after you came to Christ, it may feel heavier still.

As there is no sin so small but it deserves damnation, so there is no sin so great that it can bring damnation upon those who truly repent.

Westminster Confession of Faith 15.4

David took another man’s wife and had the man killed to cover it. When he confessed, Nathan told him the Lord had put away his sin (2 Samuel 12:13). The consequences stayed in his house, but the guilt was gone. Paul had hunted Christians into prison. He called himself the chief of sinners. He said Christ showed him mercy as a pattern for everyone who would believe after him (1 Timothy 1:15-16). If your sin is great, that pattern was set up for sinners like you.

Once you’ve confessed it and turned from it, trusting Christ’s blood and not your confession, the pardon is yours. The memory may come back for years, and the shame with it, but the guilt doesn’t. Take him at his word, and thank him for it.

He must be tired of forgiving me

We get tired of forgiving because every pardon costs us something. Your pardon cost God his Son, and that price was paid once and in full. He isn’t paying it again each time you come.

Who is a God like unto thee, that pardoneth iniquity, and passeth by the transgression of the remnant of his heritage? he retaineth not his anger for ever, because he delighteth in mercy. He will turn again, he will have compassion upon us; he will subdue our iniquities; and thou wilt cast all their sins into the depths of the sea.

Micah 7:18-19 (KJV)

He has promised to subdue the very sins he pardons. When Peter asked how often he must forgive his brother, Jesus said seventy times seven (Matthew 18:22). He doesn’t ask more mercy of you than he shows you.

What to Do This Week

  1. Confess it each time, right away. Don’t wait until you feel sorry enough. Name the sin to God plainly and thank him for the blood of Christ.
  2. Change the place and the hour. Romans 13:14 says to make no provision for the flesh. If you keep falling in the same place at the same time, change your routine there. Move the phone, leave the room, go to bed earlier.
  3. Tell one person. Pick a mature Christian of your own sex, or an elder, and tell them what you’re fighting. Ask them to check on you every week.
  4. Go to church Sunday. Don’t stay home because you fell. The preached Word and the Lord’s Supper are for sinners who are still fighting.
  5. Write down one verse. Put 1 John 1:9 or Micah 7:18-19 where you’ll see it, and read it when the shame comes back.
