Keep in touch with your care team about symptoms, treatment, and questions you don’t understand. Ask someone to help with appointments if needed. This page addresses a new diagnosis, long illness, caring for someone, and care near the end of life.

But I trusted in thee, O Lord: I said, Thou art my God. My times are in thy hand:

Psalm 31:14-15 (KJV)

The day of the diagnosis is in his hand, and every day after it.

The night before he died, in the garden of Gethsemane, Jesus told his friends, My soul is exceeding sorrowful unto death (Mark 14:34). Then he fell on the ground and prayed.

And he said, Abba, Father, all things are possible unto thee; take away this cup from me: nevertheless not what I will, but what thou wilt.

Mark 14:36 (KJV)

In Scripture a cup often pictures the portion God hands to someone. Jesus asked for his to pass, and he asked more than once (14:39). So you may ask for healing, and keep asking. And he bowed to his Father’s will. Faith doesn’t pretend the cup is sweet. It asks, and then it trusts the Father who holds it.

He asked to be spared, and he wasn’t spared. The cup he dreaded was God’s fury against sin (Isaiah 51:17). Jesus drank all of it on the cross, and on the third day he rose. If you’re his, your cup may be bitter, but it holds no condemning wrath.

Paul was given something that tormented him (2 Corinthians 12:7).

For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me. And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.

2 Corinthians 12:8-9 (KJV)

The answer was grace, and it was enough. Someone may tell you that with enough faith you would be healed. Remember that Paul left his friend Trophimus sick at Miletum (2 Timothy 4:20). Being sick isn’t a sign that your faith has failed.

Jesus prayed to his Father, and that Father rules over all of this.

God, the great Creator of all things, doth uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all creatures, actions, and things, from the greatest even to the least, by his most wise and holy providence,

Westminster Confession of Faith 5.1 (excerpt)

God ordinarily works through means (WCF 5.3). The Larger Catechism includes a sober use of physic, meaning medical care, among the duties of the sixth commandment (Q&A 135). Receiving appropriate treatment is consistent with faith in his providence.

You may wonder if this is punishment. The punishment for a believer’s sin fell on Christ, and God doesn’t collect the same debt twice. What his child may receive is a Father’s chastening, for his good and never in condemning wrath (Hebrews 12:6-11, WCF 12.1, 20.1). And not every sickness is sent for some sin (John 9:3). So don’t hunt for a hidden sin behind yours, and don’t let anyone name one for you. If an old sin troubles you, confess it. He is faithful and just to forgive (1 John 1:9).

God may heal now, or he may not. Matthew says his healings fulfilled Isaiah’s word, Himself took our infirmities, and bare our sicknesses (Matthew 8:17). They were previews of what is coming. For those who are his, the cross bought the redemption of the body, and at the resurrection the healing will be whole (Romans 8:23).

When you have questions about your diagnosis, write them down for your next appointment. Ask your elders to pray with you, including in the hospital if you can’t come to worship (James 5:14).

When it has gone on for years

You've asked for healing more times than you can count, and fewer people ask how you are. God put this prayer in the Bible for you.

How long wilt thou forget me, O Lord? for ever? how long wilt thou hide thy face from me? How long shall I take counsel in my soul, having sorrow in my heart daily? how long shall mine enemy be exalted over me?

Psalm 13:1-2 (KJV)

David felt forgotten, and he said so. A hidden face is a picture of a season when God’s favor can’t be felt, and such seasons are real (WCF 18.4). But God hadn’t forgotten David, and by the end of the psalm David trusts his mercy again (13:5). For you it may take longer than six verses. He got there by praying the question, not by hiding it.

If you’re his, the grace Paul was given is yours too. It doesn’t always take the weakness away. It holds you up inside it. Your outward man, the body, may be wasting away. Yet the inward man is renewed day by day (2 Corinthians 4:16). Paul also says the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us (Romans 8:18). Paul grounds that hope in the resurrection while acknowledging real suffering now.

You may keep asking, and you may rest from asking. Let others pray when you can’t. And tell your elders you’re still here, since churches forget the long sick. If the long years bring thoughts of ending your life, tell someone near you, and call 988 or text 988. God hasn’t finished with your life.

If you're caring for someone

For detailed help organizing daily care, finding respite and supporting someone with dementia, see When You Are Caring for Someone.

Maybe it’s a hospital room, or hospice, or the long decline of a parent, a husband or wife, or a child. You're tired in ways few people see. When Lazarus was sick, his sisters sent Jesus this word, Lord, behold, he whom thou lovest is sick (John 11:3). Pray that with their name in it.

God told his people, even to hoar hairs will I carry you (Isaiah 46:4). Hoar hairs are gray hairs. The carrying is a picture of his keeping, and his keeping is real. If they're his, he will carry them all the way, and if you're his, he carries you too.

Caring for them is Christlike work. While he was dying, Jesus saw to his mother’s care (John 19:26-27). Accept help, and give people real jobs, for we are told to bear one another’s burdens (Galatians 6:2). Choosing a care home isn’t a failure of love. God works through means, and good nurses are among them.

If dementia is taking their memory, they may no longer know your name. That’s a real grief, a goodbye that comes a little at a time, and you may mourn it. They may forget the Lord’s name too. If they are his, he won’t forget them (Isaiah 49:15). Will God Keep Me to the End? says more.

If death is near

For those who belong to Christ, death doesn’t take them from him. It brings them to him. Jesus told the dying thief beside him, who had asked only to be remembered, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise (Luke 23:42-43). At death the souls of believers are made perfect in holiness and immediately pass into glory, and their bodies, still united to Christ, rest in their graves till the resurrection (Shorter Catechism Q&A 37). Christ rose, and they'll rise.

Paul called being with Christ far better, yet said his staying was more needful for others (Philippians 1:23-24). While God keeps you here, your days aren’t wasted.

Jesus told his disciples not to let their hearts be troubled. He has gone to prepare a place for his people, and when he comes again he will receive them to himself, that where he is, they may be also (John 14:1-3).

You may face hard choices about treatment, for yourself or someone who can no longer speak. The Larger Catechism warns against neglecting the lawful and necessary means of preserving life (Q&A 136). Whether a treatment remains beneficial requires careful attention to medical facts. There is a difference between allowing natural death when treatment is no longer beneficial and deliberately causing death. Ask doctors about the likely benefits and burdens of treatment, and seek pastoral counsel on moral duties. Hospice care may relieve suffering without intending to end life.

If you fear pain or being a burden, tell your care team and the people closest to you. They may be able to arrange better relief and support for your caregivers. If thoughts of ending your life arise, tell someone near you and call 988 or text 988.

Tell your family where your hope is, and call for your elders to pray with you (James 5:14). If you dread dying itself, read Afraid of Dying.

If you’re keeping watch at the bed, you don’t need many words. Hold their hand, read Psalm 23 aloud, and pray. If you don’t know where they stand with God, it’s not too late to pray for them, to speak of Christ simply, or to ask your pastor to come.

When Healing Doesn't Come

Paul prayed repeatedly for his affliction to be removed, yet the Lord answered him with sufficient grace (2 Corinthians 12:7-9). Continuing illness doesn’t prove that God is displeased with you or that your faith has failed. Scripture doesn’t give us the reason for each diagnosis.

If someone says you must be hiding a sin because you haven’t recovered, remember Jesus’ answer concerning the man born blind (John 9:2-3). You can pray for healing while receiving treatment and adjusting to lasting limitations. If pain has made you impatient with someone, seek forgiveness for that particular wrong without turning the illness itself into an accusation.

Questions to Bring to an Appointment

Ask what the treatment is meant to accomplish, what burdens it carries, and what may happen if you decline it. For difficult decisions, especially near the end of life, it can help to bring a trusted person who will listen and remember what the doctor says.

Doctors can explain medical benefits and burdens. Your elders can help you consider the moral duties Scripture lays upon you. A decision about treatment needn’t be made from fear or pressure when there’s time for careful counsel.
