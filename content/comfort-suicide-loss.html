If you came to this page because you are thinking of taking your own life, stop and read this first. What this page says about God’s keeping was written to comfort the people left behind. It is not permission for you, and no promise of God ever is. Your life is not yours to end, and God has not finished with it. Call 988 or text 988 now, then read If You Are Not Safe. There is a page written for you, When You Are Depressed.

Yea, the darkness hideth not from thee; but the night shineth as the day: the darkness and the light are both alike to thee.

Psalm 139:12 (KJV)

You couldn’t see all that was in their heart. He could.

When Lazarus died, his sisters met Jesus one after the other with the same cry, Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died (John 11:21, 32). It’s the if-only cry, and he answered it with tears, not a rebuke (11:33, 35). You may weep for the one you lost, without shame. Bring him every what if. Bring the sign you think you missed, the message you didn’t answer, the last words between you. He forgives the sins of all who come to him, and he lifts the guilt that was never yours to carry.

Taking one’s own life is a grave sin against the sixth commandment (Exodus 20:13, Larger Catechism Q&A 136). That’s true, and so is this. For those who are Christ’s, salvation never rested on their last hour. It rests on what he finished on the cross. Those God has accepted in Christ can neither totally nor finally fall away from the state of grace (Westminster Confession 17.1).

For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Romans 8:38-39 (KJV)

If the one you lost belonged to Christ, even this death couldn’t separate them from his love. Christ died for sinners, and on the third day he rose. He said of his sheep that no man can pluck them out of his hand (John 10:28-29). Christ's promise to keep his sheep is sure. The risen Lord who makes that promise also showed his wounded hands to Thomas. After he rose, he showed Thomas the print of the nails in them (John 20:25-27). His grip doesn’t fail.

You may have heard that taking one’s own life is the unforgivable sin. It isn’t. Jesus named one sin that is never forgiven, blasphemy against the Holy Ghost. He spoke of it because men said he had an unclean spirit (Mark 3:28-30).

This comfort is for those left behind. It never gives anyone leave to take their own life. If the thought comes that God’s keeping would make it safe to end your own life, that thought is a temptation. The devil once quoted God’s promise of protection to urge Jesus into danger, and Jesus answered, It is written again, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God (Matthew 4:6-7). God has not finished with you. Call 988 or text 988 now, and tell someone near you.

If you fear the one you lost didn’t know Christ, that grief is real. You aren’t asked to settle where they are, and your fear doesn’t get the last word. Leave them with the Judge of all the earth, who will do right (Genesis 18:25), and bring your own soul to Christ, who turns away no one who comes. When You Fear They Did Not Know Christ says more.

Before you sleep, do these three things.

  1. Tell the Lord all of it. Bring him the questions and the anger too.
  2. Call your pastor or an elder. Even at night. And hear this plainly. You didn’t make this choice for them. If you have no church, call a Christian friend, and read Do Not Carry It Alone below. If you’re young, tell a grown-up you trust, or a counselor at school.
  3. Do not be alone tonight. Ask someone to stay with you, and eat a little. If the thought of following them comes, call 988 or text 988. If you were the one who found them, or if a heaviness settles on you in the weeks ahead, see a doctor, and read When You Are Depressed.

When you are ready to search your heart

Not tonight. In the weeks ahead, ask God to search you (Psalm 139:23). Ask him to sort what’s truly yours from what isn’t.

Much of what you carry may be false guilt. You didn’t choose this for them. If you truly sinned against them, confess it plainly, and in Christ it is forgiven (1 John 1:9). Even then, the choice wasn’t yours. What was never yours, you may lay down.

Anger may come too, at the one who died, or at people you think should have seen it. Be ye angry, and sin not (Ephesians 4:26). Don’t let it settle into bitterness. If it rises against God, tell him so, but don’t let it harden into a charge that he has done wrong. Shame may tell you to stay away from church. Go anyway. This death isn’t your disgrace.

If the thought of following them comes, stop the searching and get help tonight. You are not strange, and you must not carry it alone. Call 988 or text 988, and tell someone near you.

Christ died on the cross for sinners and rose again. Whoever you are, come to him with what is truly yours, and leave the rest with him.

What to Do This Week

  1. Save 988 in your phone. Do it today, so it’s there when you need it. If the thought of following them comes, call 988 or text 988 right away, and tell someone near you.
  2. Eat and sleep. Shock wears the body down. Eat something at each meal, even a little, and lie down at your usual time.
  3. Let someone help with the hard calls. There may be phone calls and papers you dread. Ask a friend or an elder to make them with you, or for you.
  4. Read Psalm 139 slowly. Read the first twelve verses once a day. Then tell God the one what if that weighs most on you today, and leave it with him.
  5. Go to church Sunday. Shame may tell you to stay home. Go, and sit with someone who knows what happened.
