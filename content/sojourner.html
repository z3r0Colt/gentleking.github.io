What it is

Scripture, and the Church Reading It With You

Sojourner is a free, offline Windows desktop application for reading the Bible beside the commentaries, confessions, and reference works the Reformed church has been handing down for four hundred years. The Hebrew and Greek lie underneath, and the tools for preparing a sermon and leading family worship sit on top. All of it is on your own machine. None of it asks you to sign in.

Most study software wants something from you first. An account. A subscription. A connection. A tier that hides the good commentaries until you pay. Sojourner wants none of it. You install it, and the Bibles, the commentaries, and the reference works are already there. It is for the pastor, the elder, the family, and the student who want Scripture and the best of the old books close at hand without a bill.

The word sojourner is what the saints called themselves while they were still walking. You are passing through, and you need the road marked. That is the whole idea behind it.

It is a servant, not a substitute. A program cannot preach to you, baptize you, or feed you at the Lord’s Table. Use it through the week, and be under the Word in your own church on the Lord’s Day.

Everything Sojourner does

Convictions

Offline, and Yours

Sojourner uses the network for exactly one thing. You press a button on the About page to check whether a newer version exists. That is all. There is no account, no sync service, and no telemetry of any kind. Nothing you read, search, write, or pray is reported anywhere, because there is nowhere for it to go. Even the book shelves install from files you download yourself, so they can just as well arrive on a USB stick.

Everything you write lives in one file on your machine. Snapshots and automatic backups run in the background. You can export the whole database and import it again, run an integrity check, keep a copy in a OneDrive or Dropbox folder if you want one, and look at your own reading statistics on a heatmap. If the program ever crashes, the log stays on your computer.

This is not a marketing position. It is the only arrangement that makes sense for a book you are supposed to read for the rest of your life. Companies fold and services shut down. A file on your own disk does not.

For Windows

Free Bible Software for Windows

If you are looking for a free Bible program for your Windows PC, Sojourner is one. It costs nothing, it needs no account, and once it is installed it works with no internet connection at all. It is a Bible study app for the desktop, not a phone app or a website.

Anyone who has used e-Sword or theWord will know the idea. The Bible sits in one pane and the commentaries follow along in another. Sojourner differs in what comes in the box. Matthew Henry, Calvin, the Westminster Standards, the Three Forms of Unity, the Psalter, and the Hebrew and Greek with their lexicons are there when it opens, chosen for readers in the Reformed and Presbyterian tradition. Nothing has to be hunted down and added one module at a time.

Logos and Accordance do far more, and they cost far more. Sojourner is not trying to be them.

What It Does Not Have

It runs on Windows only, and nothing syncs between computers. It does not update itself either.

It cannot include the ESV, the NIV, or the NASB, because they are under copyright. The same is true of BDAG and HALOT, the standard scholarly lexicons for Greek and Hebrew. If you own a modern translation as a Zefania XML file, you can add it yourself.
