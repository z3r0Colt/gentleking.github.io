What it is

What Sojourner Is

Sojourner is a free Bible study program for your phone, tablet, or computer. You read the Bible beside the commentaries, confessions, and reference works the Reformed church has used for four hundred years. The Hebrew and Greek are underneath, with tools on top for preparing a sermon and leading family worship. It runs in any browser at app.gentleking.org, or as a program on Windows and Linux. Your notes stay on your own device, and you never sign in.

Most study software wants something from you first. An account. A subscription. A connection. A tier that hides the good commentaries until you pay. Sojourner asks for none of it. You install it, and the Bibles, the commentaries, and the reference works are already there. It’s for the pastor, the elder, the family, and the student who want Scripture and the best of the old books close at hand for free.

Sojourner is what believers called themselves on their way through this life. You’re passing through, and you need the road marked. That’s the idea behind the name.

It’s a tool, not a substitute. A program can't preach to you, baptize you, or feed you at the Lord’s Table. Use it through the week, and be under the Word in your own church on the Lord’s Day.

Everything Sojourner does

Convictions

Private and Offline

Sojourner uses the network for exactly one thing. You press a button on the About page to check whether a newer version exists. That’s all. There’s no account, no sync service, and no telemetry of any kind. Nothing you read, search, write, or pray is reported anywhere, because there’s nowhere for it to go. Even the book shelves install from files you download yourself, so they can also come on a USB stick.

That’s the desktop program. The web app keeps the same promise in a browser. Your notes stay in your browser’s private storage on your own device. There’s no account, and the app sends nothing about you. It fetches the books from this ministry’s own server as you read them. It can also download the whole library once so that it never needs the network again. More on the web app and your data.

Everything you write lives in one file on your machine. Snapshots and automatic backups run in the background. You can export the whole database and import it again, and run an integrity check. You can keep a copy in a OneDrive or Dropbox folder if you want one, and see your own reading statistics on a heatmap. If the program ever crashes, the log stays on your computer.

This is the only arrangement that makes sense for a book you’re meant to read for the rest of your life. Companies fold and services shut down. A file on your own disk doesn’t.

For every device

Free Bible Software for Your Phone and Computer

If you’re looking for a free Bible app for your iPhone, your Android, your Mac, your Windows PC, or your Linux machine, Sojourner is one. It’s the same program everywhere. It costs nothing and needs no account. Once it’s installed it works with no internet connection at all. In the browser it can download its whole library and do the same.

Anyone who has used e-Sword or theWord will know the idea. The Bible sits in one pane and the commentaries follow along in another. Sojourner differs in what comes with it. Matthew Henry, Calvin, the Westminster Standards, the Three Forms of Unity, the Psalter, and the Hebrew and Greek with their lexicons are there when it opens. They were chosen for readers in the Reformed and Presbyterian tradition. The Reformed library is ready to use when you open the program.

Logos and Accordance do far more, and they cost far more. Sojourner isn’t trying to be them.

What It Doesn't Have

Nothing syncs between devices, though Export and Import carry your notes across. The desktop program doesn’t update itself. In the browser, a few things are left out because a browser can’t do them, and What the Web Version Leaves Out names them.

It can’t include the ESV, the NIV, or the NASB, because they’re under copyright. The same is true of BDAG and HALOT, the standard scholarly lexicons for Greek and Hebrew. If you own a modern translation as a Zefania XML file, you can add it yourself.
