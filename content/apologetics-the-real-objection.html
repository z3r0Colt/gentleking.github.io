And this is the condemnation, that light is come into the world, and men loved darkness rather than light, because their deeds were evil. For every one that doeth evil hateth the light, neither cometh to the light, lest his deeds should be reproved.

John 3:19-20 (KJV)

Jesus exposes the resistance of the human heart in John 3. I once resisted that light myself. So set the arguments down and answer honestly. No one is watching but God.

I'm a good person

Consider how God’s law searches your own life. Have you lied, taken what wasn’t yours, or sought credit you didn’t deserve? Jesus said that whoever looks on a woman to lust after her has already committed adultery with her in his heart (Matthew 5:28). John calls hatred murder (1 John 3:15). Have you hated someone or treated God’s name carelessly (Exodus 20:7)? The first commandment reaches deeper still. God requires your whole heart, and none of us has loved him perfectly. Even our best record cannot answer that law.

Perhaps you hope the good you’ve done will outweigh the bad. It won’t. Whoever keeps the whole law and yet breaks it in one point is guilty of all (James 2:10), because the God who said do not commit adultery also said do not kill (James 2:11). Break his law at any point and you’ve defied the one who gave all of it.

Now we know that what things soever the law saith, it saith to them who are under the law: that every mouth may be stopped, and all the world may become guilty before God.

Romans 3:19 (KJV)

Jesus said that evil thoughts, adulteries, murders, thefts and deceit come from within, out of the heart (Mark 7:21-23). Ask yourself whether you want to be free of your sins, or only free of what they cost you. Wanting to keep a sin while escaping its judgment is precisely the resistance Jesus describes as loving darkness. You aren’t a good man with a few faults and some questions. You’re a sinner by nature and by choice. You were born that way, a child of Adam, and Paul says we were all by nature the children of wrath (Ephesians 2:3). I don’t say it from above you. The same law shut my mouth.

Then what hope is there for me?

Then drew near unto him all the publicans and sinners for to hear him. And the Pharisees and scribes murmured, saying, This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them.

Luke 15:1-2 (KJV)

His enemies meant it as a charge, and it’s the best news you’ll ever hear. Christ came for sinners exactly like you. He kept the law you’ve broken. On the cross he bore its curse for everyone who trusts him, and God raised him from the dead. The gospel page tells it in full.

So the call is the one Jesus preached. Repent, and believe the gospel (Mark 1:15). Turn your back on your sin, and rest on Christ alone, not your own record, to answer for you before God. Come to Christ now, even with questions you haven't resolved.

Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts: and let him return unto the Lord, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon.

Isaiah 55:7 (KJV)

The unrighteous man must forsake his thoughts as well as his way. Turn from false accusations against God, self-justification, and refusal to heed his Word. Honest questions deserve careful answers. They need not keep you from coming to Christ for pardon.

  1. Call on him. You don’t need fine words. A tax collector asked God to be merciful to him as a sinner, and Jesus said he went home justified (Luke 18:13-14).
  2. Read the Gospel of John. A chapter a day is enough. John wrote it so that you would believe that Jesus is the Christ and have life through his name (John 20:31).
  3. Hear the Word preached. Go to a faithful church this Sunday. Faith comes by hearing the word of God (Romans 10:17), and no one has to believe anything to walk in and listen.
  4. Write to me. If an objection is still standing in your way, tell me what it is.
