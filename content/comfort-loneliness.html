Tell one trusted person what you need, such as a regular call, a shared meal, or help getting to worship. If you’ve tried and been overlooked, ask an elder for specific help. If you’re confined at home, the visiting guidance below is for you.

Loneliness comes in many shapes. You may be single and wish you weren’t. You may be divorced, or the last of your old friends still living. You may have moved where no one knows your name. You may be married and still alone in your own house. Or you may sit in church every Lord’s Day and leave without a word from anyone. If your husband or wife has died, that sorrow has its own page.

David knew this. He wrote it in a cave, hiding from a king who wanted him dead.

I looked on my right hand, and beheld, but there was no man that would know me: refuge failed me; no man cared for my soul.

Psalm 142:4 (KJV)

A refuge is a place to run to. David didn’t pretend he had friends he didn’t have. He said it to God, and in the next breath he said, Thou art my refuge and my portion in the land of the living (142:5). You may say it to God the same way, plainly, before you feel anything better.

Turn thee unto me, and have mercy upon me; for I am desolate and afflicted.

Psalm 25:16 (KJV)

The Son of God was left alone too. The night before he died, he asked three friends to watch with him, and they slept (Matthew 26:38-40). Within hours all the disciples forsook him, and fled (26:56). He had told them it would come.

Behold, the hour cometh, yea, is now come, that ye shall be scattered, every man to his own, and shall leave me alone: and yet I am not alone, because the Father is with me.

John 16:32 (KJV)

On the cross he cried, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? (Matthew 27:46). He bore that forsaking in the place of sinners, and on the third day he rose. So those who trust him will never be forsaken. He has said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee (Hebrews 13:5). If you’re his, the loneliness you feel tonight is real. But it doesn’t tell you the truth about where you stand. He is with you.

Paul was alone at the end of his life too. Most of his friends had gone, and one had left him, having loved this present world (2 Timothy 4:10).

At my first answer no man stood with me, but all men forsook me: I pray God that it may not be laid to their charge. Notwithstanding the Lord stood with me, and strengthened me;

2 Timothy 4:16-17 (KJV)

Paul’s loneliness didn’t keep him from speaking kindly of those who failed him. You can ask the Lord for that grace while still telling the truth about how much their absence hurts.

God Gives the Lonely a Family

God doesn’t leave his people alone with him and no one else. The same psalm that calls him a father of the fatherless says, God setteth the solitary in families (Psalm 68:5-6). He does it through his church.

Jesus said that whoever does the will of God is his brother, and sister, and mother (Mark 3:35). To those who leave house and family for his sake he promised houses, and brethren, and sisters, and mothers, and children, now in this time, with persecutions (Mark 10:29-30). Those are the people of God, and if you’re his, they’re given to you.

Saints, by profession, are bound to maintain an holy fellowship and communion in the worship of God, and in performing such other spiritual services as tend to their mutual edification; as also in relieving each other in outward things, according to their several abilities and necessities.

Westminster Confession of Faith 26.2

Church membership creates real duties toward one another (1 Corinthians 12:25-26). A congregation may fail to notice a quiet member, and that failure should be corrected. It’s right to tell your elders when you need company, while remembering that the duty to offer fellowship belongs to the whole church.

If You're Single

If you long to be married, the longing isn’t sin. God himself said, It is not good that the man should be alone (Genesis 2:18). But marriage is his gift to give, and he doesn’t give it to every one of his people in this life. Paul was unmarried, and he said that every man hath his proper gift of God, one after this manner, and another after that (1 Corinthians 7:7). A single believer isn’t loved less by God than a married one.

If you marry, marry only in the Lord (1 Corinthians 7:39). Loneliness is a poor counselor in that choice. Talk it over with your elders and with believers who know you.

If You're Married and Alone

You may share a house and a name with someone and still be alone. Your husband or wife may not believe, or may have grown cold. They may be away working long hours, struggling with illness, or unable to talk about the things you most need to say. It can be difficult to tell someone else you're lonely when everyone assumes a married person has company.

If it's safe to talk, tell your spouse what you've been missing in concrete terms. You might ask for a quiet half hour together, a regular meal without phones, or a chance to speak about something other than bills. Listen to what they say they need as well. Don't assume a spouse knows what you've been carrying simply because you live under the same roof. If every conversation ends in blame or silence, ask for careful marriage counsel rather than repeating the same argument. See When You Keep Having the Same Argument.

Peter wrote to wives whose husbands obey not the word (1 Peter 3:1). You can love an unbelieving spouse without pretending the difference is easy. Ask God for their salvation and seek Christian friendship and counsel outside your home. It's right to need the fellowship of the church even when you are married. A husband or wife is not the whole congregation.

If you're afraid of your spouse, being monitored, forced, or threatened, ordinary conversation advice isn't safe. Read the guidance for an unsafe home and get independent help. If your marriage is breaking through betrayal, separation, or desertion, read When Your Marriage Is Breaking. Tell a trustworthy pastor or elder what is actually happening, not only that you want someone to pray for the marriage.

When the Church Has Overlooked You

You may have attended public worship faithfully without being included in the ordinary friendships of the congregation. Some people are too ill, shy, or exhausted to make every first move. Other members should notice who is absent from meals and conversation and invite them in (Romans 12:13).

Tell an elder how long this has been happening, and ask for specific help meeting other members. If your congregation has a pattern of neglecting people who live alone, the elders should address it with the church. Fellowship grows through ordinary visits and shared meals as well as public worship.

If You Cannot Get to Worship

Age, illness, a disability, or caring duties may keep you at home. Ask the elders for visits, prayer, and help hearing the Word. Other members can call, bring food, or spend an afternoon with you.

If you can attend but find the gathering overwhelming, speak to a trusted member about sitting with them. A personal relationship may begin slowly. The church’s love is more than a Sunday greeting.
