I can be good without God

In one sense you can, and Christians have never denied it. God’s law is written on every heart, so men who never read a Bible know a great deal about right and wrong (Romans 2:14-15). But good by whose measure? Jesus said the first and great commandment is to love God himself with everything you are (Matthew 22:37-38). A man can be kind to his neighbor and break that commandment every day he lives. Even his good works, the Confession says, are sinful and cannot please God. They don’t come from a heart purified by faith or aim at the glory of God. Yet leaving them undone would be more sinful still (WCF 16.7, Hebrews 11:6).

Morality is only evolution or culture

An evolutionary account may describe how moral habits developed, but description alone cannot establish what anyone ought to do. Nature has produced cruelty as surely as kindness, and it can’t tell you which is right. If moral rules depend entirely on a society's approval, there is no standard above that society by which to judge its conduct. The men who ended the slave trade weren’t right, only different. No one lives that way.

You say torturing a child for fun is wrong everywhere and always, and you’re right. That’s a law above every culture. You might call it simply a fact, like two and two making four. But a sum doesn’t accuse you, and you wrong no one when you add it up badly. A law that binds every man and accuses him when he breaks it needs a Lawgiver with the right to bind him. His law is no whim, as if he could have made cruelty good. He commands what agrees with his own nature, and he cannot deny himself (2 Timothy 2:13). And you don’t only know that law. You hold other people to it every time they break their word to you or run you down behind your back. You’ve done both.

Therefore thou art inexcusable, O man, whosoever thou art that judgest: for wherein thou judgest another, thou condemnest thyself; for thou that judgest doest the same things.

Romans 2:1 (KJV)

Examine Your Life

  1. Read Matthew start to finish. Slow down at the Sermon on the Mount in chapters 5 to 7. Measure yourself by it, not by your neighbors.
  2. Write down where your sense of right and wrong comes from. Try to ground it without God, in a sentence or two. Then ask a pastor to read it with you.
  3. Visit a faithful church on Sunday and listen. Listen for how the prayers and the sermon speak about sin. Ask yourself whether you could honestly say amen.
