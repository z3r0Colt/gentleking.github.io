God Governs What He Has Made

The Lord hasn't left the world to run itself. What happens to you isn't outside his care, even when you can't understand it.

Creation answers where everything came from. Providence answers why creation doesn't fall apart and who governs it now. The Westminster Confession says God upholds, directs, disposes, and governs all creatures and actions, from the greatest to the least (WCF 5.1). Jesus said a sparrow doesn't fall to the ground apart from your Father (Matthew 10:29-31). Nothing is too small for his care.

That doesn't mean every event is good in itself. Joseph's brothers sold him into slavery. Their malice was real. Years later Joseph could say that they meant evil against him, but God meant it for good (Genesis 50:20). Joseph held his brothers responsible for their treachery while confessing God's wise purpose in the same events.

Does God Cause People to Sin?

God's decree includes everything that comes to pass, but he is never the author or approver of sin (WCF 3.1 and 5.4). The wicked act willingly and bear responsibility for what they do. God is holy, even when he overrules their actions for his purpose.

Look at the crucifixion. Christ was delivered by the determined counsel and foreknowledge of God, and those who crucified him acted with wicked hands (Acts 2:23). The greatest evil done by men fulfilled God's saving purpose. Their guilt wasn't imaginary, and neither was God's sovereignty.

Don't use providence to excuse your own choices. If you've sinned, repent. If someone has harmed you, the wrong remains wrong. God's government of the event doesn't turn cruelty into righteousness.

God Works Through Ordinary Means

Because the Lord orders outcomes, we can pray with confidence. Because he also orders the means, we still have work to do. The farmer sows and the Lord gives the increase. A sick person may see a doctor while praying for healing. A parent teaches a child while asking God to open that child's heart.

Westminster Confession 5.3 says God ordinarily uses means, yet remains free to work without, above, and against them. Faith in providence doesn't mean staying in danger when you can escape it. Paul knew God had promised the lives of those aboard his ship, but he still told the centurion that the sailors must remain if they were to be saved (Acts 27:22-25, 31).

When Providence Hurts

Some readers will arrive here after a diagnosis, a death, or a betrayal. Scripture hasn't told us why every particular sorrow comes. Job never received an itemized explanation of his losses. The Lord answered him by revealing his greatness, and Job humbled himself before him.

The Confession teaches that God sometimes permits his own children to experience temptations and the corruption remaining in them, to humble them and teach them dependence (WCF 5.5). But that isn't permission for us to tell every sufferer what secret lesson God must be teaching. We don't know the secret counsel of God.

What we do know is that the Father's care doesn't fail. If you belong to Christ, your suffering isn't the condemnation he bore in your place (Romans 8:1). God works all things together for good to those who love him and are called according to his purpose (Romans 8:28). Paul goes on to define that good as being conformed to the image of his Son. The promise isn't that this week will be pleasant.

What Should I Do With This?

Do the duty in front of you. Pray for what you need. Take wise counsel from Scripture and your elders. Give thanks for mercies you've noticed. When the Lord's ways are hidden, you may grieve honestly without calling him unfaithful. Psalm 77 gives you words to pray.

Trust the Lord where his purposes are hidden, and keep doing the duty he has given you. If you're struggling with the sense that God has left you, read When God Feels Far. If you're grieving a loss, the Comfort section speaks more directly to that sorrow.
