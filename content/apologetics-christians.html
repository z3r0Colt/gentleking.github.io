The church is full of hypocrites

Some who sit in churches are hypocrites, and Jesus said there would be (Matthew 7:21-23). His hardest words were for religious men who looked clean outside and were rotten within (Matthew 23:27-28). So on this point you and he agree. But a hypocrite fails to live by what he claims. That tells you about the man and nothing against the claim. Counterfeit money is only worth making because real money exists. Many of the Christians you have in mind aren’t hypocrites at all. They’re sinners who know it, which is why they’re in church.

They that are whole have no need of the physician, but they that are sick: I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.

Mark 2:17 (KJV)

Religion has done great harm

It has, and some of it was done in the name of Christ. There’s no excuse to make, and Reformed Christians have no clean hands to hold up. Geneva burned Michael Servetus for heresy in 1553, and Calvin had pressed for his death. Cruelty like that defies Christ. He rebuked James and John when they wanted to call down fire on a village that would not receive him (Luke 9:54-55).

But the harm doesn’t leave when religion does. The officially atheist governments of the last century killed their own people by the millions. The trouble is the human heart, which goes wherever men go, into churches and into prison camps alike. Am I a Good Person? turns to it.

What to Do This Week

  1. Read Mark start to finish. It’s the shortest Gospel, and you can read it in an evening. Judge Jesus by what he said and did, not by his followers.
  2. Write down what you hold against Christians. Be specific. Then ask a pastor about it and see whether he defends it or owns it.
  3. Visit a faithful church on Sunday. Listen to what they confess about themselves in the service. Ask whether these people claim to be good, or claim to need a Savior.
