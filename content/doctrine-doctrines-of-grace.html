How God Saves Sinners

The Doctrines of Grace

These five points were never meant to be the whole of the faith. They're an answer to a specific question. When God saves a sinner, who does the saving?

Read them as one sentence about one God working one salvation. Taken apart they can sound cold. Taken together they're a deep comfort, because they mean your salvation doesn’t finally rest on how tightly you hold on.

Total Depravity

Also called total inability

Sin has touched every part of us, mind and will and affections, so that no one seeks God on his own. The word total is about extent, not degree. No part of us is clean enough to save the rest. Paul lays this out in Romans 3, and Ephesians 2 calls us dead in trespasses.

Unconditional Election

Ephesians 1:4-5

Before the foundation of the world God set his love on a people in Christ. He didn’t choose because he looked ahead through time and saw who would choose him. It rested on the good pleasure of his will. Paul defends it at length in Romans 9, including the objections it raises.

Particular Redemption

Commonly called limited atonement

Christ didn’t die to make salvation merely possible. He died to actually save particular people, and he won’t lose them. His death is of infinite worth, and Christ is freely offered in the gospel to every sinner who hears. But its saving design and effect are for his sheep.

Effectual Calling

Sometimes called irresistible grace

Alongside the outward call of the gospel to all, the Spirit gives an inward call that raises the dead. Ezekiel 36 promised it, and the new birth is where God makes good on the promise. New life comes first, and then the sinner comes willingly, because he has been made willing.

Perseverance of the Saints

Kept by the power of God

Those truly united to Christ will be kept to the end. They may fall badly and grieve the Spirit, but they'll be brought back. This isn’t a promise that a prayer you once prayed guarantees heaven. It’s a promise that what God began in you he will bring to completion, as Philippians 1:6 says.

I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.

John 10:11 (KJV)

He doesn’t say he makes a sheepfold available. He lays down his life for the sheep, and he gets them.

All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.

John 6:37 (KJV)

Both halves of that verse are yours to hold. The first half tells you election works. The second half tells you the door is open, right now, to you. If you’re worried about whether you’re chosen, stop looking inward and come to Christ. Nobody who comes is turned away.
