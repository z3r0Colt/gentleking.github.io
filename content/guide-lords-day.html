Set Sunday apart for public and private worship and rest from ordinary work and recreation. Prepare ahead where you can, and continue necessary care for people who depend on you. Necessity and mercy are part of keeping the day, not failures to keep it.

Why It Matters

God set apart one whole day in seven as holy to himself. From the creation to the resurrection of Christ it was the last day of the week. Since the resurrection it has been the first day, the Lord’s Day, the Christian Sabbath (WCF 21.7, Acts 20:7, Revelation 1:10).

Christ said the Sabbath was made for man (Mark 2:27). God gives us a whole day set apart for worship, rest from ordinary labor, and fellowship with his people.

If thou turn away thy foot from the sabbath, from doing thy pleasure on my holy day; and call the sabbath a delight, the holy of the Lord, honourable; and shalt honour him, not doing thine own ways, nor finding thine own pleasure, nor speaking thine own words:

Isaiah 58:13 (KJV)

How to Keep It

The Shorter Catechism says the day is kept by a holy resting all that day, even from such worldly employments and recreations as are lawful on other days, and by spending the whole time in the public and private exercises of God’s worship, except so much as is to be taken up in the works of necessity and mercy (Q&A 60).

  1. Prepare on Saturday. Plan meals, travel, and necessary household tasks ahead where possible. Share the preparation so one person does not carry it all. Aim for enough sleep to attend to worship. The Larger Catechism calls for this kind of foresight (Q&A 117).
  2. Worship with your church. Be at the public worship your congregation holds that day. If it meets morning and evening, plan to attend both as you are able. If illness, disability, transport, or dependent care prevents attendance, tell your elders what help is needed. Ask about visits and ways to hear the preaching while you are unable to gather. Public worship is the center of the day (Hebrews 10:25, WCF 21.6).
  3. Rest from your work. Leave ordinary business and avoidable errands for another day. Essential care and urgent protective action continue.
  4. Rest from your entertainments too. Lawful recreation has its place on other days. Give this day to the worship and rest God appoints.
  5. Fill the rest of the day. Read, pray, talk about the sermon at the table, sing psalms, visit the sick, show hospitality, and rest. See How to Lead Family Worship.

Works of Necessity and Mercy

Jesus healed on the Sabbath and defended his disciples for picking grain when they were hungry (Matthew 12:1-13). Some things must be done on any day, and some ought to be.

  1. Necessity. Feeding your family, caring for dependents and animals, and urgent work that cannot wait. Healthcare and emergency services can include such duties, but an occupation’s name does not make every task necessary.
  2. Mercy. Caring for the sick, helping someone in trouble, visiting the lonely.

Your job may require Sunday work that isn’t a work of necessity or mercy. Discuss the actual duties and possible changes with your employer and elders. If losing the job would put food, housing, or dependents at risk, ask for practical help while seeking a lawful arrangement. This page cannot settle every employment situation, and church counsel does not replace local advice about workplace rights.

With Children

Make the day a delight, not just a list of things they can’t do. Help them take part in worship according to age and ability. Arrange needed feeding, medicine, rest, or accessibility support. Talk about something they understood from the sermon on the way home. Save special books, walks, and meals for that day. Let them see that you love it.
