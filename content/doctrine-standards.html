Where to Go Next

The Westminster Standards were written to instruct the church. Read them carefully, with Scripture open beside them.

This site uses the original 1647 Westminster Confession of Faith and the original Larger and Shorter Catechisms. It does not substitute the American revision of 1788. The distinction matters in particular for Christian liberty (WCF 20.4), the civil magistrate (23.3), and synods (31.2). Begin with the Shorter Catechism if you're new to the Standards, then read the Confession with a Bible open beside it. You can consult the full Confession and its Scripture proofs, the Larger Catechism, and the Shorter Catechism at The Westminster Standard.

Take it slowly. Read each paragraph with its proof texts rather than assuming the Confession says what a later edition says. A historical explanation of an amendment can be useful, but it doesn't change the text this ministry teaches. The Standards were written for the church, and no website can take the church’s place. Find a faithful local church under the ordinary means of grace. Join it, and sit under the Word. If the gospel itself is still unclear to you, go to the gospel page first. That’s the doctrine everything else serves.

And remember who all of this is about. The King who ordained the covenant before the world was, and who will judge the living and the dead, says this to you.

Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.

Matthew 11:28-29 (KJV)

Learn the doctrine so you can see more of him, and then go to him. If something here raised a question you can’t settle, you are welcome to write and ask.
