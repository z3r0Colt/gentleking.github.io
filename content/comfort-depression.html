If thoughts of ending your life come, call 988 or text 988 at any hour, tell someone near you, and read If You Are Not Safe.

In Gethsemane Jesus began to be sorrowful and very heavy, and he said so. My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death (Matthew 26:37-38). He did not hide his sorrow, and you need not hide yours. Matthew also tells how he treats the weak.

A bruised reed shall he not break, and smoking flax shall he not quench,

Matthew 12:20 (KJV)

A bruised reed is a cracked stalk, and smoking flax is a wick burned down to smoke. They picture someone nearly spent, and his gentleness to such a one is real. If you belong to him, he will not snap you or snuff you out.

In Psalm 42 a man with tears for his food day and night speaks to his own soul (42:3).

Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God.

Psalm 42:11 (KJV)

Disquieted means troubled, and countenance means face. The question comes three times (42:5, 42:11, 43:5), and each time the answer is hope in God.

The sinless Son of God was very heavy, so heaviness of soul is not itself a sin. Neither is depression, and it is not proof that you lack faith. If the dark came after your baby was born, it is no failure of love. Elijah once lay down under a juniper tree and asked to die. God did not scold him, and he did not grant that prayer. He let him sleep and fed him twice, for the journey was too great for him (1 Kings 19:4-8). Then he gave him work to do (19:15-16). Your life is not yours to end either, and God has not finished with it.

Your mind lives in a body, and bodies get sick. God usually works through means (Westminster Confession 5.3). Sleep and food are means, and so are a doctor and medicine. When God Feels Far says more.

Your darkness may feel like God’s wrath. The man who wrote Psalm 88 said, Thy wrath lieth hard upon me (88:7), yet he kept crying to God day and night (88:1). On the cross, in the darkness over all the land, Christ bore the weight of God’s wrath for his people (Matthew 27:45, Larger Catechism Q&A 49). If you are his, no condemning wrath is left for you, however heavy this feels (WCF 20.1).

He died there, rose on the third day, and still does not quench smoking flax. God has not told us when this will lift. The man in Psalm 42 did not know either, yet he said, I shall yet praise him. Many of God’s people have walked through this dark and seen it lift, sometimes slowly, and a doctor’s care can help. Whether it lifts soon or late, if you are his, the dark will not have the last word. When Christ comes again, he will make his people’s lowly bodies like his glorious body (Philippians 3:21).

The dark may tell you that you were never his. It is a poor judge of that, for even those who fear God may walk in darkness and have no light (WCF 18.4). If you cannot tell, read Am I Really His. Come to Christ now. You do not have to settle it first.

Before you sleep, do these three things.

  1. Tell God how dark it is. If you cannot pray, tell him that. It is a prayer too. Then say Psalm 42:11 to your own soul, even if you feel nothing.
  2. Tell someone today. Tell your pastor or an elder how long this has lasted. If you are the pastor, tell an elder or a minister you trust. If you have no church, call a Christian friend. If you are young, tell a parent or a school counselor.
  3. Care for your body. Eat something, and keep a set bedtime if you can. With a new baby, let someone else take part of the night. See a doctor this week, or go back to yours and say it has not lifted. Tell the doctor everything, even thoughts of ending your life. If anyone says you only need more faith, go anyway. Do not stop a medicine without asking your doctor. If those thoughts come, day or night, call 988 or text 988, and tell someone near you.

When you are ready to search your heart

Do this in a better hour, and not alone if you can help it. It is not a hunt for a sin behind the dark (John 9:3). For you, the searching should be short and the looking to Christ long.

Who is among you that feareth the Lord, that obeyeth the voice of his servant, that walketh in darkness, and hath no light? let him trust in the name of the Lord, and stay upon his God.

Isaiah 50:10 (KJV)

To stay upon God is to lean your whole weight on him. Watch for only a few things. The dark may tempt you to give up the Word, prayer, and worship, because they feel empty. Keep to them, even briefly, and let others pray with you. It may tempt you to dull the pain with drink or drugs, or to hide how bad it is. If the dark tells you that God will never be kind to you again, it is lying.

If the dark tells you that others would be better off without you, that is a lie too. If thoughts of ending your life come, stop the searching and get help now. Do not keep them secret. Call 988 or text 988, and tell someone near you.

Where you find sin, confess it simply. Christ died for sinners and rose again, and he turns away no one who comes. So look away from yourself to him.
