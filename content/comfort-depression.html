If thoughts of ending your life come, call 988 or text 988 at any hour, tell someone near you, and read If You Are Not Safe.

In Gethsemane Jesus began to be sorrowful and very heavy, and he said so. My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death (Matthew 26:37-38). He didn’t hide his sorrow, and you don’t need to hide yours. Matthew also tells how he treats the weak.

A bruised reed shall he not break, and smoking flax shall he not quench,

Matthew 12:20 (KJV)

A bruised reed is a cracked stalk, and smoking flax is a wick burned down to smoke. They picture someone nearly spent, and his gentleness to such a person is real. If you belong to him, he won’t snap you or snuff you out.

In Psalm 42 a man with tears for his food day and night speaks to his own soul (42:3).

Why art thou cast down, O my soul? and why art thou disquieted within me? hope thou in God: for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God.

Psalm 42:11 (KJV)

Disquieted means troubled, and countenance means face. The question comes three times (42:5, 42:11, 43:5), and each time the answer is hope in God.

The sinless Son of God was very heavy, so heaviness of soul isn’t itself a sin. Neither is depression, and it isn’t proof that you lack faith. If the dark came after your baby was born, it’s no failure of love. Elijah once lay down under a juniper tree and asked to die. God didn’t scold him, and he didn’t grant that prayer. He let him sleep and fed him twice, for the journey was too great for him (1 Kings 19:4-8). Then he gave him work to do (19:15-16). Your life isn’t yours to end either, and God hasn’t finished with it.

Your mind and body affect each other. Illness can make prayer, concentration, and ordinary work harder. God usually works through means (Westminster Confession 5.3). Sleep and food are means, and so are a doctor and medicine. When God Feels Far says more.

Your darkness may feel like God’s wrath. The man who wrote Psalm 88 said, Thy wrath lieth hard upon me (88:7), yet he kept crying to God day and night (88:1). On the cross, in the darkness over all the land, Christ bore the weight of God’s wrath for his people (Matthew 27:45, Larger Catechism Q&A 49). If you’re his, no condemning wrath is left for you, however heavy this feels (WCF 20.1).

He died there, rose on the third day, and still doesn’t quench smoking flax. We don't know when this darkness will lift. Scripture gives no timetable. The man in Psalm 42 didn’t know either, yet he said, I shall yet praise him. Many believers have known long seasons of depression. Some recover quickly. Others need years of care. A doctor’s care can help. It may lift soon, or late, or only when he comes. Either way, if you’re his, the dark won’t have the last word. When Christ comes again, he will make his people’s lowly bodies like his glorious body (Philippians 3:21).

The dark may tell you that you were never his. It’s a poor judge of that, because even those who fear God may walk in darkness and have no light (WCF 18.4). If you can’t tell, read Am I Really Saved? Come to Christ now. Come to Christ with the question still troubling you.

Tell someone how dark things have become. Ask that person to help make a medical appointment if you cannot manage the call. Eating, rest, and daylight may help you get through a day, but they aren’t a cure for every form of depression.

When Ordinary Things Are Hard

Depression can disturb sleep, appetite, attention, and work. It isn’t by itself proof of a hidden sin. Jesus refused the assumption that a man’s blindness must be traced to a particular fault (John 9:3). You don’t need a moral explanation before seeking treatment.

Who is among you that feareth the Lord, that obeyeth the voice of his servant, that walketh in darkness, and hath no light? let him trust in the name of the Lord, and stay upon his God.

Isaiah 50:10 (KJV)

Tell your doctor how long the darkness has lasted and whether you’re safe. Don’t stop prescribed medicine without medical advice. Ask someone you trust to check in with you. If you cannot read much Scripture, a few verses of a psalm may be all you can manage. The Spirit helps our infirmities (Romans 8:26). If you might hurt yourself, tell someone nearby and call 988 or text 988 now.

Care That Continues

A first appointment may only begin the work of treatment. Follow up if symptoms change or treatment isn’t helping. Let someone know when you cannot manage ordinary tasks. If you cannot attend worship, ask your elders to visit.
