Safety

If You're Not Safe

Use the urgent contacts first. Find help for thoughts of suicide, violence at home, and child abuse, with guidance on safety, reporting, and pastoral care.

On this page

  1. If you're thinking of ending your life
  2. If someone is hurting you
  3. If a child is being hurt
  4. Ask the Church to Help
  5. Christ Is Offered to You

About 8 minutes to read

The contacts below serve the United States. Elsewhere, use your local emergency number for immediate danger and seek a local crisis or abuse service.

If you’re in danger right now, call 911.

If you’re thinking of ending your life, call 988 or text 988. It answers at any hour.

If someone at home is hurting you, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788.

If a child is in immediate danger, call 911. For other concerns about child abuse, contact local child protective services or police. Childhelp at 1-800-422-4453 can help you find reporting options, but it is not itself a reporting line.

If someone monitors your phone, they may see searches, messages, or calls. A trusted person’s device may be safer, but no device guarantees privacy. Contact an advocate from a place the person harming you cannot observe when possible. Don’t depend on clearing browser history to make you safe.

Outside the United States, call your local emergency number.

If you're thinking of ending your life

If you might act on thoughts of suicide now, call 911 or go to an emergency department. Otherwise, call 988 or text 988 and tell someone safe near you. Ask that person to stay with you. Have a trusted person secure medicines, weapons, or other means of harming yourself.

God gave you life, and the sixth commandment requires us to preserve our own life as well as the lives of others (Larger Catechism Q&A 135). When Elijah wished to die, the Lord provided for him and continued to deal with him patiently (1 Kings 19 (opens in a new tab)). Tell someone plainly that you need help staying safe. Your pastor can pray with you, but don’t delay urgent medical or crisis care while seeking him.

If someone is hurting you

For he shall deliver the needy when he crieth; the poor also, and him that hath no helper. He shall spare the poor and needy, and shall save the souls of the needy. He shall redeem their soul from deceit and violence: and precious shall their blood be in his sight.

Psalm 72:12-14 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

This is a psalm about the true King, and Christ is that King. He isn’t neutral about what’s being done to you. A husband or a wife or anyone in your home may hit you, threaten you, force you, or make you afraid for your life or your children. You may seek safety away from that person. If leaving could expose you or your children to greater danger, speak with an abuse advocate about a plan suited to your situation. In immediate danger, contact emergency services.

The Larger Catechism names just defense against violence, and protecting and defending the innocent, among the duties God requires (Q&A 135). Scripture says a prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself (Proverbs 22:3 (opens in a new tab)). When Saul threw a javelin at David, David fled, and his wife let him down through a window (1 Samuel 19:10-12 (opens in a new tab)). Jesus told his disciples that when they were persecuted in one city, they should flee into another (Matthew 10:23 (opens in a new tab)).

Leaving to be safe isn’t a lack of faith, and it isn’t a sin. You’re not to blame for another person’s violence. Questions about the marriage itself can wait, and your elders can help you with them later.

Calling the police isn’t revenge, and it isn’t a betrayal of the church. Scripture calls the ruler the minister of God, who bears the sword against those who do evil (Romans 13:4 (opens in a new tab)). Tell your elders too, once you’re safe. If the one hurting you is an elder, or close to one, tell another pastor you trust. If anyone tells you to go back into danger, don't go back. Seek help from civil authorities or an abuse advocate, and from a pastor independent of the person harming you.

Christ is the Bridegroom who gave himself for his bride, and who nourishes and cherishes her (Ephesians 5:25 (opens in a new tab), 29). What’s being done to you is the opposite of what he does. When a crowd at Nazareth tried to throw him off a cliff, he passed through the midst of them and went his way (Luke 4:29-30 (opens in a new tab)). His hour hadn't yet come. When it came, he gave himself up on the cross, of his own will and in the place of sinners. On the third day he rose. That work is finished. Your suffering can’t add to it, so you’re not called to stay under violence.

If a child is being hurt

If a child is in danger, call 911 now. Otherwise report it to the police or to your county’s child protective services. Childhelp can tell you how. Don't wait for anyone at church to handle it first. Suspected abuse needs civil assessment and protection. Church proceedings don’t replace contacting the appropriate child-protection authority. A faithful church should support protective action. If a church leader tries to stop a report, contact the appropriate civil authority anyway.

If you’re young and someone is hurting you, tell a teacher, school counselor, or another safe adult. In immediate danger, call 911. You can also speak with a Childhelp counselor at 1-800-422-4453. Childhelp provides support and guidance about reporting. Its counselors may have to share information in certain circumstances, depending on the facts and the law.

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

You can hear Christ’s call while questions about your suffering remain unanswered. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.
