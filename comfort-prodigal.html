A wandering child

When Your Child Walks Away From the Lord

You taught them to pray, and now they want nothing to do with Christ. You can't give anyone a new heart. Only God can, and he has done it for sons and daughters as far off as yours.

In danger, or thinking of ending your life? Call 911, or call 988 or text 988, now. If someone at home is hurting you, read If You Are Not Safe. Outside the United States, call your local emergency number.

On this page

  1. Love Without Feeding the Sin
  2. Praying Without Predicting
  3. Ask the Church to Help
  4. Christ Is Offered to You

About 7 minutes to read

Your child or grandchild may mock the faith, or deny God under your roof, or be lost to drink or drugs. Jesus told of a son who wasted his substance with riotous living (Luke 15:13 (opens in a new tab)).

And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.

Luke 15:20 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

The father in this story pictures God, and his welcome to a sinner who comes home is real. Jesus came to seek and to save the lost, and there is joy in heaven over one sinner that repenteth (Luke 19:10 (opens in a new tab), 15:7). He knows what it’s like to be refused by his own (John 1:11 (opens in a new tab)). You may grieve this loss, though no one sends flowers for it.

God promised this to his scattered people.

A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.

Ezekiel 36:26 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

This is what God does for his people, and only he can do it. It gives you good reason to pray for your child without claiming to know what God has decreed. The stony heart describes a will that God himself must renew.

When God calls those he has chosen, he renews their wills and by his almighty power draws them to Christ. Yet they come most freely, being made willing by his grace (Westminster Confession 10.1 (opens in a new tab)). He hasn’t told us who they are, so no one may write your child off. If your child once professed faith, you don’t need to settle tonight whether it was real. The Lord knoweth them that are his (2 Timothy 2:19 (opens in a new tab)).

Repentance is God’s gift, and he may yet give it. Ask him. Don’t quarrel with your child, but be gentle and patient (2 Timothy 2:24-25 (opens in a new tab)). Keep the door open, and love your child without approving their sin. An open door never means letting danger in.

If your child was baptized, the working of baptism isn’t tied to the moment it was done. The Holy Ghost gives its grace to those it belongs to, as God wills, in his appointed time (Westminster Confession 28.6 (opens in a new tab)). Baptism is a sign and seal to pray on. It isn’t a guarantee.

Some read Proverbs 22:6 (opens in a new tab), Train up a child in the way he should go, as a promise that your child must come back, or as proof that you failed. It’s neither, and God hasn’t broken his word. A proverb tells how things usually go, not what must happen every time. God said of Israel, I have nourished and brought up children, and they have rebelled against me (Isaiah 1:2 (opens in a new tab)). Their rebellion wasn’t his failure.

Where you sinned against your child, by harsh words or neglect, confess it to God, and to your child if you can. But don’t take their sin on yourself. God says a parent doesn’t bear the iniquity of a child (Ezekiel 18:20 (opens in a new tab)).

Christ suffered once for sins, the just for the unjust, to bring us to God. He died, and on the third day he rose again (1 Peter 3:18 (opens in a new tab), 1 Corinthians 15:4 (opens in a new tab)). If you’re his, your sins were laid on him and are forgiven. He is able to bring your child to God too.

Pray for your child by name and speak with them when you can without financing what harms them. If you and your spouse disagree about boundaries, seek counsel together. Changing a heart belongs to the Lord.

Love Without Feeding the Sin

You can love an unbelieving adult child and still refuse threats, theft, or money that would feed an addiction. A meal or a conversation doesn’t require approval of their sin. If others at home are unsafe, protect them first. Seek appropriate help for addiction or violence.

The father in Luke 15 (opens in a new tab) welcomed his returning son. This parable teaches God’s mercy toward repentant sinners. It doesn’t promise that every child will return. Pray for your own child by name. Only the Spirit can give a new heart (Westminster Confession 10.1 (opens in a new tab)).

Praying Without Predicting

God can save a son or daughter after years of rebellion. He hasn’t promised every parent that every child will repent. Pray with hope in his power, speak truth when you can, and protect those under your care.

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

You don’t have to settle every question about the person you lost before hearing Christ’s call to you. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.
