A wandering child

When Your Child Walks Away From the Lord

Love your child without approving destructive behavior. Find help with conversation, boundaries, and prayer without claiming God has promised every child will return.

On this page

  1. If Your Child Is an Adult
  2. If They Ask to Come Home
  3. Ask the Church to Help
  4. Christ Is Offered to You

About 7 minutes to read

Keep speaking honestly and kindly with your child when conversation is possible. You can offer love without financing destructive behavior. If drugs, theft, or violence affect the household, go to the guidance on boundaries and seek qualified help alongside pastoral care.

Your child or grandchild may mock the faith, or deny God under your roof, or be lost to drink or drugs. Jesus told of a son who wasted his substance with riotous living (Luke 15:13).

And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.

Luke 15:20 (KJV)

The father in this story pictures God, and his welcome to a sinner who comes home is real. Jesus came to seek and to save the lost, and there is joy in heaven over one sinner that repenteth (Luke 19:10, 15:7). He knows what it’s like to be refused by his own (John 1:11). You may grieve this loss, though no one sends flowers for it.

God promised this to his scattered people.

A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.

Ezekiel 36:26 (KJV)

This is what God does for his people, and only he can do it. It gives you good reason to pray for your child without claiming to know what God has decreed. The stony heart describes a will that God himself must renew.

When God calls those he has chosen, he renews their wills and by his almighty power draws them to Christ. Yet they come most freely, being made willing by his grace (Westminster Confession 10.1). He hasn’t told us who they are, so no one may write your child off. If your child once professed faith, you don’t need to settle tonight whether it was real. The Lord knoweth them that are his (2 Timothy 2:19).

Repentance is God’s gift, and he may yet give it. Ask him. Don’t quarrel with your child, but be gentle and patient (2 Timothy 2:24-25). Keep the door open, and love your child without approving their sin. An open door never means letting danger in.

If your child was baptized, remember that baptism is a sign and seal of the covenant of grace, but it doesn’t guarantee regeneration (Westminster Confession 28.1, 28.5). The Spirit confers the grace promised to those to whom it belongs, in God’s appointed time (28.6). Pray for your child without presuming to know God’s decree.

Proverbs 22:6 teaches the importance of training a child, but it isn’t an unconditional promise that every child of believing parents will be converted. Parents must teach faithfully, and children remain responsible for their response. God said of Israel that he had brought up children who rebelled against him (Isaiah 1:2).

Where you sinned against your child, by harsh words or neglect, confess it to God, and to your child if you can. But don’t take their sin on yourself. God says a parent doesn’t bear the iniquity of a child (Ezekiel 18:20).

Christ suffered once for sins, the just for the unjust, to bring us to God. He died, and on the third day he rose again (1 Peter 3:18, 1 Corinthians 15:4). If you’re his, your sins were laid on him and are forgiven. He is able to bring your child to God too.

Keep praying for your child by name. Where conversation is possible, listen carefully and speak honestly about sin and the gospel. If you and your spouse disagree about boundaries, seek counsel together before a crisis forces a decision.

If Your Child Is an Adult

An adult child may refuse worship, use drugs, steal, or threaten family members. You can offer hospitality and speak kindly without financing destructive behavior. Set clear limits around money and safety. If younger children live with you, protect them from violence or dangerous conduct.

If your child cuts off contact, you cannot force a conversation. Keep the door open to a safe and truthful relationship as circumstances allow. If addiction or violence is involved, ask for help from qualified services as well as your elders.

If They Ask to Come Home

The father in Luke 15 welcomed a son who returned in repentance. His joy teaches us about God’s mercy toward repentant sinners. If your child begins to seek reconciliation, hear them patiently and give thanks for every sign of genuine repentance.

Receiving someone in love doesn’t require ignoring a history of violence, theft, or addiction. You may need counsel about what living together would involve. The parable gives hope in God’s grace without promising that every child will return.

The church

Ask the Church to Help

Scripture gives comfort that a page can explain. Christ also gives his people a church where they can receive care from others.

Tell your pastor or an elder what has happened. You can begin with one sentence and ask him to visit or pray with you. If the need is a meal, a ride, or help with bills, speak to the deacons. Those are real needs, and serving the congregation in practical matters belongs to their office. Elders are charged to feed the flock of God (1 Peter 5:2).

If you’re in the hospital, confined at home, or caring for someone who cannot be left alone, ask for visits and prayer there. If you don’t have a faithful congregation, speak to a trustworthy Christian while you seek one. If a pastor or elder is harming you, seek help from someone independent of him. In a dangerous home, reach safety before seeking church counsel.

Gather for public worship on the Lord’s Day when you’re able. Christ nourishes faith through his Word, sacraments, and prayer (Shorter Catechism Q&A 88, Westminster Confession 14.1). If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table by a faithful church, examine yourself as Scripture requires (1 Corinthians 11:28). Weakness and doubt by themselves don’t mean you must stay away. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to doubting believers in Q&A 172. Speak with your elders if you’re uncertain about coming.

If you need help finding a faithful church, you can write to me. I’m a layman, not a pastor, and I may not answer quickly. Don’t wait for an email in an emergency. Use the urgent help contacts instead.

Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort; Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (KJV)

As the Lord helps you, let his people share the burden.

If you do not know him

Christ Is Offered to You

You may have come here because someone died or because your life has changed in a painful way. Scripture speaks of a comfort that belongs to those who are in Christ, and it freely calls you to come to him.

Jesus met Martha in the sorrow of her brother’s death and spoke these words to her (John 11:17-26).

Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?

John 11:25-26 (KJV)

You can hear Christ’s call while questions about your suffering remain unanswered. Scripture teaches that death entered through sin and that each of us must answer to God (Romans 5:12, Hebrews 9:27). Christ died for his people, bearing the punishment they deserved, and rose again on the third day. Every sinner who hears the gospel is commanded to repent and believe in him. Whoever comes to Christ will not be cast out (John 6:37).

If you don’t know him, come to him now. You can read the gospel explained in full here. Your grief doesn’t have to end before you turn to Christ.
