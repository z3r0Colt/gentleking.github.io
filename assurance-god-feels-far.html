Spiritual dryness

When God Feels Far

A loss of comfort does not by itself prove that God has forsaken you. Seek pastoral care, attend to health needs, and read what Scripture says to troubled believers.

I've prayed, but God still feels far away

If exhaustion, illness, or depression is disrupting your life, go to the guidance on health and care as well as seeking pastoral help. You may read the Bible and find that its words seem to stop at the page. You pray, and you wonder whether God hears. If his promises once brought comfort and now seem distant, tell the Lord how much that grieves you. The Psalms give words to believers who know this darkness.

Who is among you that feareth the Lord, that obeyeth the voice of his servant, that walketh in darkness, and hath no light? let him trust in the name of the Lord, and stay upon his God.

Isaiah 50:10 (KJV)

Notice who is walking in darkness. It's someone who fears the Lord and obeys his servant. Isaiah doesn't tell him to manufacture a feeling. He tells him to trust his God. The Lord's promise remains true when you can barely feel its comfort.

Psalm 88 ends in darkness. Heman doesn't receive a tidy answer before the last verse, yet he begins by calling upon the God of his salvation. In Psalm 77 Asaph asks whether God's mercy has clean gone for ever. He brings the question to God, then remembers how the Lord led Israel through the sea. He couldn't see God's footsteps, but God was there (Psalm 77:19-20). You may not be able to trace what the Lord is doing now.

Has God withdrawn his love from me?

There's a difference between losing the comfort of God's love and being cast out of his family. A believer can lose the sense of God's fatherly countenance for a time. He can't lose the justification Christ purchased for him (Romans 8:1 and WCF 11.5). Christ doesn't cease to intercede because your heart has gone cold (Hebrews 7:25).

Westminster Confession 18.4 says true believers may have their assurance shaken, diminished, and interrupted. It doesn't say this happens only to people who haven't prayed enough. It names negligence, particular sins, sudden temptation, and God's withdrawing the light of his countenance for a time. The Confession also says that God never leaves his own utterly destitute of the seed of God and the life of faith.

Don't make today's feelings your final judgment on the Lord's faithfulness. The promise of Christ is a better witness than your present comfort (John 6:37 and John 10:27-29).

What if my sin has brought this darkness?

Sometimes it has. David knew what it was to keep silent about his sin and feel the Lord's hand heavy upon him. Read Psalm 32. If you know you're cherishing a sin, don't excuse it by calling your trouble a dry season. Confess it plainly. Turn from it. If you've wronged someone, seek to make it right.

God's fatherly displeasure is real. The Confession says a justified believer may lose the light of his countenance until he humbles himself, confesses, seeks pardon, and renews his faith and repentance (WCF 11.5). Yet the Father isn't demanding that his child pay for sins Christ has already borne. He calls his child back through repentance.

But don't search for a secret sin behind every hard day. Job's friends were wrong to assume his suffering proved some hidden wickedness. Christ also rejected the disciples' attempt to trace a man's blindness to a particular sin (John 9:1-3). Examine yourself honestly, and repent where Scripture exposes sin. Don't invent guilt where God hasn't shown it.

I've repented. Why hasn't the comfort returned?

Because comfort isn't something we can command. David prayed for the joy of salvation to be restored (Psalm 51:12). That prayer itself shows that a forgiven man may long for a joy he doesn't presently have.

You might confess your sin sincerely and still feel low the next morning. That doesn't mean your repentance was false. Don't keep confessing the same matter merely to obtain a particular feeling. Believe what God says about pardon, and go on putting sin to death. If there's a duty you've neglected, take it up again.

Some darkness lasts longer than we expect. Psalm 88 doesn't hide that. The Lord may restore assurance gradually, through his Word and the ministry of his church. WCF 18.3 says a true believer may have to wait long and struggle with many difficulties before he has assurance. Come to Christ now, even while assurance remains weak.

How do I seek him while I feel nothing?

Keep going where the Lord has promised to meet and strengthen his people. Hear the Word preached. Read Scripture in its context, even if you only manage a short passage. Pray honestly. If all you can say is that you're afraid and need help, say that. Pray Psalm 13 or Psalm 77 when your own words fail.

Don't withdraw from the church because you feel empty. Tell your pastor or an elder what's happening. Ask for prayer and counsel. If you're a communicant member troubled by doubts, speak with your elders about coming to the Lord's Table. The Supper is given to strengthen weak faith, but it must be received with repentance and self-examination (WLC 171-173).

These are means of grace, not a bargain with God. You aren't earning his presence by keeping a schedule. You're coming to the places where he ordinarily nourishes his people (WCF 14.1).

What about exhaustion, grief, or illness?

Your body matters. Severe weariness, grief, depression, or illness can make it harder to think and pray. Elijah was afraid and worn down under the juniper tree, and the Lord provided rest and food (1 Kings 19:4-8). But that's not the whole account. At Horeb the Lord questioned him, corrected him, and sent him back to his calling (1 Kings 19:9-18). Don't turn the passage into a claim that spiritual darkness is cured by a meal and a nap.

If you're exhausted, begin by finding out what has changed in your sleep, health, work, or household. If you've been persistently low, anxious, or unable to manage ordinary responsibilities, make a medical appointment and describe the symptoms plainly. Ask an elder to visit or pray with you if you can't get to church. These practical needs deserve attention even as you seek the Lord through his Word and prayer.

If your distress includes thoughts of ending your life, tell someone safe near you and read the dedicated suicide-support guidance. In the United States, 988 is available by call or text. In immediate danger, seek emergency care.

What to do now

  1. Bring the real question to God. If you fear he has forsaken you, tell him so. Read Psalm 13 and pray it rather than pretending all is well.
  2. Deal with known sin. Confess it and turn from it. Don't keep hunting for a hidden offense just because the feeling won't lift.
  3. Go hear the Word. Gather for public worship when you're able, even if you feel numb. If illness or caring duties keep you home, ask an elder to visit and pray with you.
  4. Hold to a promise. Read John 6:37 in its setting. Your warrant to come to Christ is his invitation, not a feeling of being chosen.
  5. Get help with what is actually wrong. If exhaustion, illness, grief, or depression has interrupted your ordinary life, tell someone you trust and seek appropriate care. Physical illness and spiritual doubt can occur together, and each needs proper attention.

While you wait

Do Not Fight This Alone

Tell your pastor or an elder what you’re afraid of. Gather for public worship when you’re able. If you’re confined at home, ask for visits and prayer. If you’ve been admitted to the Lord’s Table, speak with your elders about preparation and doubts. The Larger Catechism gives counsel to weak and doubting believers who sincerely seek Christ and desire to depart from sin (Q&A 172). Doubt alone doesn’t require staying away, but self-examination and the church’s oversight still matter. Will God Keep Me to the End? gives a few things to do while you wait for the fear to lift.

If you can’t tell whether you’ve ever come to Christ, don’t wait to settle it. Come to him now. He promised to cast out none who come (John 6:37), and the gospel is set out for you in full.
