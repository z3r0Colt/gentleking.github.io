Spiritual dryness

When God Feels Far

There are seasons when prayer feels like talking to the ceiling and the Bible is only ink. They don't mean that God has gone.

I've prayed, but God still feels far away

You read the Bible, and the words seem to stop at the page. You pray, and you wonder whether God hears. If his promises once brought comfort and now seem distant, tell the Lord how much that grieves you. The Psalms give words to believers who know this darkness.

Who is among you that feareth the Lord, that obeyeth the voice of his servant, that walketh in darkness, and hath no light? let him trust in the name of the Lord, and stay upon his God.

Isaiah 50:10 (opens in a new tab) (KJV)

Notice who is walking in darkness. It's someone who fears the Lord and obeys his servant. Isaiah doesn't tell him to manufacture a feeling. He tells him to trust his God. The Lord's promise remains true when you can barely feel its comfort.

Psalm 88 (opens in a new tab) ends in darkness. Heman doesn't receive a tidy answer before the last verse, yet he begins by calling upon the God of his salvation. In Psalm 77 (opens in a new tab) Asaph asks whether God's mercy has clean gone for ever. He brings the question to God, then remembers how the Lord led Israel through the sea. He couldn't see God's footsteps, but God was there (Psalm 77:19-20 (opens in a new tab)). You may not be able to trace what the Lord is doing now.

Has God withdrawn his love from me?

There's a difference between losing the comfort of God's love and being cast out of his family. A believer can lose the sense of God's fatherly countenance for a time. He can't lose the justification Christ purchased for him (Romans 8:1 (opens in a new tab) and WCF 11.5 (opens in a new tab)). Christ doesn't cease to intercede because your heart has gone cold (Hebrews 7:25 (opens in a new tab)).

Westminster Confession 18.4 (opens in a new tab) says true believers may have their assurance shaken, diminished, and interrupted. It doesn't say this happens only to people who haven't prayed enough. It names negligence, particular sins, sudden temptation, and God's withdrawing the light of his countenance for a time. The Confession also says that God never leaves his own utterly destitute of the seed of God and the life of faith.

Don't make today's feelings your final judgment on the Lord's faithfulness. The promise of Christ is a better witness than your present comfort (John 6:37 (opens in a new tab) and John 10:27-29 (opens in a new tab)).

What if my sin has brought this darkness?

Sometimes it has. David knew what it was to keep silent about his sin and feel the Lord's hand heavy upon him. Read Psalm 32 (opens in a new tab). If you know you're cherishing a sin, don't excuse it by calling your trouble a dry season. Confess it plainly. Turn from it. If you've wronged someone, seek to make it right.

God's fatherly displeasure is real. The Confession says a justified believer may lose the light of his countenance until he humbles himself, confesses, seeks pardon, and renews his faith and repentance (WCF 11.5 (opens in a new tab)). Yet the Father isn't demanding that his child pay for sins Christ has already borne. He calls his child back through repentance.

But don't search for a secret sin behind every hard day. Job's friends were wrong to assume his suffering proved some hidden wickedness. Christ also rejected the disciples' attempt to trace a man's blindness to a particular sin (John 9:1-3 (opens in a new tab)). Examine yourself honestly, and repent where Scripture exposes sin. Don't invent guilt where God hasn't shown it.

I've repented. Why hasn't the comfort returned?

Because comfort isn't something we can command. David prayed for the joy of salvation to be restored (Psalm 51:12 (opens in a new tab)). That prayer itself shows that a forgiven man may long for a joy he doesn't presently have.

You might confess your sin sincerely and still feel low the next morning. That doesn't mean your repentance was false. Don't keep confessing the same matter merely to obtain a particular feeling. Believe what God says about pardon, and go on putting sin to death. If there's a duty you've neglected, take it up again.

Some darkness lasts longer than we expect. Psalm 88 (opens in a new tab) doesn't hide that. The Lord may restore assurance gradually, through his Word and the ministry of his church. WCF 18.3 (opens in a new tab) says a true believer may have to wait long and struggle with many difficulties before he has assurance. Come to Christ now, even while assurance remains weak.

How do I seek him while I feel nothing?

Keep going where the Lord has promised to meet and strengthen his people. Hear the Word preached. Read Scripture in its context, even if you only manage a short passage. Pray honestly. If all you can say is that you're afraid and need help, say that. Pray Psalm 13 (opens in a new tab) or Psalm 77 (opens in a new tab) when your own words fail.

Don't withdraw from the church because you feel empty. Tell your pastor or an elder what's happening. Ask for prayer and counsel. If you're a communicant member troubled by doubts, speak with your elders about coming to the Lord's Table. The Supper is given to strengthen weak faith, but it must be received with repentance and self-examination (WLC 171-173 (opens in a new tab)).

These are means of grace, not a bargain with God. You aren't earning his presence by keeping a schedule. You're coming to the places where he ordinarily nourishes his people (WCF 14.1 (opens in a new tab)).

What about exhaustion, grief, or illness?

Your body matters. Severe weariness, grief, depression, or illness can make it harder to think and pray. Elijah was afraid and worn down under the juniper tree, and the Lord provided rest and food (1 Kings 19:4-8 (opens in a new tab)). But that's not the whole account. At Horeb the Lord questioned him, corrected him, and sent him back to his calling (1 Kings 19:9-18 (opens in a new tab)). Don't turn the passage into a claim that spiritual darkness is cured by a meal and a nap.

Get the rest and care you need, and don't treat medical help as unbelief. If the heaviness persists or interferes with daily life, speak to a doctor as well as your elders. Physical care can help you bear the burden. It can't replace the promises of Christ or the ordinary means of grace.

If you're thinking of ending your life, tell someone near you today. In the United States you can call 988 or text 988 at any hour. Elsewhere, seek immediate help through your local emergency service.

What to do now

  1. Bring the real question to God. If you fear he has forsaken you, tell him so. Read Psalm 13 (opens in a new tab) and pray it rather than pretending all is well.
  2. Deal with known sin. Confess it and turn from it. Don't keep hunting for a hidden offense just because the feeling won't lift.
  3. Go hear the Word. Be at public worship this Lord's Day, even if you're numb. Ask an elder to pray with you.
  4. Hold to a promise. Read John 6:37 (opens in a new tab) in its setting. Your warrant to come to Christ is his invitation, not a feeling of being chosen.
  5. Receive appropriate care. If your health or mood has suffered, seek practical and medical help. Don't ask a night's sleep to do what only the Lord can do.

While you wait

Do Not Fight This Alone

A page cannot sit with you or pray over you. Tell your pastor or an elder what you are afraid of. Keep sitting under the preached Word, and if you are a communing member, do not stay away from the Lord’s Table while your doubts are being answered. The Larger Catechism says it was appointed for the relief even of weak and doubting Christians (Q&A 172). Will God Keep Me to the End? gives a few things to do while you wait for the fear to lift.

If you cannot tell whether you have ever come to Christ, do not wait to settle it. Come to him now. He promised to cast out none who come (John 6:37), and the gospel is set out for you in full.
